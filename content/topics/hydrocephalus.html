Hydrocephalus is an excessive accumulation of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) in the ventricles with raised pressure, from blocked flow or poor absorption. In infants, whose sutures are open, the head enlarges; in older children with closed sutures, raised intracranial pressure develops quickly.

CSF pathway and types

Obstructive (non-communicating)
  • Aqueduct stenosis (commonest congenital)
  • Chiari II malformation with myelomeningocele
  • Dandy–Walker malformation
  • Posterior fossa tumour
Communicating
  • After meningitis (bacterial, TB)
  • After intraventricular haemorrhage (preterm)
  • Congenital infection (toxoplasmosis)

Clinical features

Infants
  • Rapidly enlarging head (crossing centiles), bulging, tense anterior fontanelle, separated sutures
  • Sunset sign (eyes deviated down), dilated scalp veins, shiny scalp
  • Irritability, vomiting, poor feeding, developmental delay, spasticity of the legs
  • "Cracked-pot" sound (Macewen)
Older children
  • Morning headache, vomiting, irritability, drowsiness
  • Papilloedema, VI nerve palsy, upward-gaze palsy
  • Late: Cushing triad (bradycardia, hypertension, irregular breathing) = herniation

Investigations and treatment

  • Cranial ultrasound (open fontanelle) · CT / MRI: dilated ventricles, cause
  • Measure and plot OFC serially
  • Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt · endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV) for aqueduct stenosis
  • Acute: temporary external drain, acetazolamide while waiting

Shunt complications

BlockageCommonest · features of raised ICP return (headache, vomiting, drowsiness, bulging fontanelle) · urgent neurosurgery
InfectionUsually within 6 months of surgery (Staph epidermidis) · fever, redness along the tract, peritonitis
OthersOver-drainage (slit ventricles, subdural haematoma), disconnection, migration, peritoneal pseudocyst

Neural tube defects (NTDs) result from failure of the neural tube to close in the 3rd–4th week of pregnancy. They are common in Sudan, linked to folate deficiency, and largely preventable by folic acid before conception.

Problems with myelomeningocele

  • Flaccid paralysis and sensory loss below the lesion, club feet, hip dislocation
  • Neuropathic bladder (dribbling, retention, UTI, reflux, renal damage) and neuropathic bowel (constipation, incontinence)
  • Hydrocephalus (Chiari II: hindbrain herniation, stridor, apnoea)
  • Pressure sores, latex allergy, scoliosis, learning difficulties, psychosocial problems

Management

  1. At birth: cover the sac with sterile moist (saline) gauze, nurse prone, avoid pressure, IV antibiotics if leaking
  2. Surgical closure within 48–72 hours (prevents infection and further damage)
  3. VP shunt for hydrocephalus · monitor OFC
  4. Clean intermittent catheterisation (CIC), anticholinergics (oxybutynin), bowel programme
  5. Orthopaedics and physiotherapy, wheelchair/calipers · multidisciplinary follow-up

Prevention and prenatal diagnosis

  • Folic acid 0.4 mg daily from before conception to 12 weeks · 5 mg daily if previous NTD, on valproate/antiepileptics, diabetes, obesity
  • Food fortification
  • Maternal serum α-fetoprotein ↑ (16–18 weeks)
  • Ultrasound: lemon and banana signs, spinal defect
  • Recurrence risk ≈ 3–5% after one affected child
