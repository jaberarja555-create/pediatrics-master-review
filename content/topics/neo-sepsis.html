Neonatal sepsis is a systemic infection in the first 28 days, confirmed by a positive blood culture. It is a leading cause of neonatal death in Sudan. The signs are non-specific, so any sick newborn is treated as septic until proved otherwise, and antibiotics are started before culture results.

Early versus late onset

FeatureEarly-onset (EOS)Late-onset (LOS)
Onset< 72 hours (most in the first 24 h)> 72 hours to 28 days
SourceMother: ascending infection of the birth canalEnvironment: hospital (hands, lines) or community (cord, skin)
OrganismsGBS, E. coli, Listeria, KlebsiellaKlebsiella, Staph. aureus, CoNS (lines), E. coli, Pseudomonas, Candida
PicturePneumonia, respiratory distress, shockMeningitis more often, focal infection (umbilicus, skin, bone, UTI)

Risk factors

Maternal (EOS)
  • Prolonged rupture of membranes > 18 h
  • Maternal fever ≥ 38 °C, chorioamnionitis (foul liquor), UTI
  • GBS colonisation · preterm labour
Neonatal (LOS)
  • Prematurity, LBW (low IgG)
  • IV lines, ventilation, long stay, crowded nursery, poor hand hygiene
  • Home delivery, unclean cord care, formula feeding

Clinical features (non-specific)

  • Poor feeding, lethargy, "not doing well" (often the first sign)
  • Temperature instability: fever or hypothermia
  • Respiratory distress, apnoea, cyanosis
  • Jaundice, hepatomegaly, petechiae, sclerema
  • Vomiting, abdominal distension, feed intolerance
  • Poor perfusion: CRT > 3 s, mottling, tachycardia, shock
  • Hypoglycaemia or hyperglycaemia, acidosis
  • Meningitis: seizures, bulging fontanelle, high-pitched cry (neck stiffness is rare)

Local infections

OmphalitisRed, swollen, discharging cord base · local only: chlorhexidine / gentian violet · redness spreading to the skin = systemic antibiotics (cloxacillin + gentamicin)
Skin pustulesStaph. aureus · < 10 pustules: local antiseptic · many or sick: cloxacillin
Ophthalmia neonatorumGonococcal (day 2–5, profuse pus, corneal risk): ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg IM once (max 125 mg) · Chlamydia (day 5–14): oral erythromycin 12.5 mg/kg 6-hourly × 14 days
Oral thrushCandida: white plaques that do not wipe off · nystatin 100 000 IU 6-hourly × 7 days

Investigations (septic screen)

Blood cultureThe gold standard · take before antibiotics (≥ 1 mL)
CBCWBC < 5000 or > 20 000, I : T ratio > 0.2 (immature : total neutrophils), platelets < 100 000
CRP> 10 mg/L · serial values: two normal values 24–48 h apart help to stop antibiotics
Lumbar punctureIn LOS, positive culture, or signs of meningitis (when stable) · CSF in the newborn: WBC > 20–30/µL, protein > 1.5 g/L, glucose < 60% of blood
OthersUrine culture (LOS, catheter/suprapubic), CXR, glucose, blood gas, swabs

Antibiotic treatment

SituationRegimenDuration
First line (EOS, community LOS)Ampicillin 50 mg/kg IV 12-hourly (8-hourly after week 1) + gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV once daily (4 mg/kg if preterm, 36-hourly if < 30 wk)Culture-negative, well: stop at 48–72 h · proven sepsis: 7–10 days
MeningitisCefotaxime 50 mg/kg IV 12-hourly (week 1), 8-hourly (after) + ampicillin (Listeria) ± gentamicin14 days (GBS) · 21 days (Gram-negative)
Hospital LOS / failurePer local sensitivity: meropenem 20–40 mg/kg 8-hourly ± vancomycin 15 mg/kg (MRSA, CoNS)10–14 days
Staph skin / cordCloxacillin 50 mg/kg IV 12-hourly + gentamicin7–10 days

Ceftriaxone is avoided in jaundiced and preterm newborns (displaces bilirubin; precipitates with IV calcium). Gentamicin: monitor levels and hearing if prolonged.

Supportive care and monitoring

  • Warmth, oxygen, IV fluids (10% dextrose), glucose monitoring
  • Shock: normal saline 10 mL/kg over 10–20 min, repeat up to 40 mL/kg; then dopamine
  • Breast milk by NG tube when stable
  • Improving: temperature and feeding normalise, CRT < 3 s, CRP falling at 48 h
  • Failing: persistent fever, shock, rising CRP, new seizures → LP, change to second line, look for a focus (abscess, NEC)

Prevention

  • Hand washing (the single most important measure), clean delivery and clean cord care (chlorhexidine)
  • Exclusive breastfeeding, KMC, avoid unnecessary lines · intrapartum antibiotics to GBS-positive mothers or mothers with PROM > 18 h / fever
