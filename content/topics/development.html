Development is the acquisition of skills in four fields: gross motor, fine motor and vision, speech, language and hearing, and social, emotional and behaviour. The order is constant in all children; only the rate varies. Assess each field separately, give it an age, then compare with the child's age.

Principles of development

  • Proceeds cephalo-caudal (head control before sitting, sitting before walking) and proximal to distal (whole-hand grasp before pincer grasp)
  • Primitive reflexes must disappear before voluntary movement appears
  • Median age = age at which 50% achieve a skill · limit age = age by which 97.5% achieve it (+2 SD): failure to reach the limit age needs assessment
  • Preterm babies: use the corrected age (subtract the weeks born early) until 2 years: a 9-month-old born at 28 weeks is expected to behave like a 6-month-old
  • Gross motor skills expand most in year 1, speech from 18 months, social skills from 2½ years

Postural (protective) reflexes

  • Appear as primitive reflexes go, and stay for life
  • Parachute reflex: 6–9 months (mature by 9 months): arms extend when the baby is tipped forward
  • Lateral propping: 6–7 months
  • Persisting primitive reflexes (Moro or ATNR after 6 months) = cerebral palsy (10.3)
  • Asymmetric Moro = brachial plexus (Erb's) palsy, fractured clavicle or humerus
  • Absent Moro at birth = severe CNS depression (HIE)

Vision and hearing

Vision
  • Pupils react to light from birth; fixes and follows a face by 6 weeks
  • Follows 180° at 3 months · binocular vision by 6 months
  • Squint is normal (intermittent) until 3–6 months; any squint after 6 months needs referral
  • Tears appear at about 2–4 months
Hearing
  • Newborn startles to loud sound; quiets to voice at 3 months
  • Distraction test at 7–9 months: turns to a quiet sound at ear level, 1 m away
  • Audiometry from about 3–4 years
  • Any child with delayed speech needs a hearing test first

Order of copying shapes

Line (2 yr) → circle (3 yr) → cross (4 yr) → square (4½ yr) → triangle (5 yr) → diamond (6 yr). Cubes: tower of age in years × 3 (2 years = 6 cubes, 3 years = 9).

Loss of skillsAt any age: regression = neurodegenerative disease or autism

Types of delay

  • Global delay: two or more fields delayed · causes: genetic (Down syndrome, fragile X), birth asphyxia, kernicterus, congenital infection, congenital hypothyroidism, meningitis, severe malnutrition
  • Specific delay: one field only · motor: CP, muscle disease · speech: deafness · social: autism

Autism spectrum disorder

  • Impaired social interaction (poor eye contact, no pointing to share interest, no response to name) + impaired communication + restricted, repetitive behaviour (hand flapping, lining up toys, insists on sameness)
  • Boys more than girls · onset before 3 years · regression of language at 15–24 months in some
  • Management: early referral, speech and behavioural therapy, special education; vaccines do not cause autism

Assessment of a child with delay

  1. History: pregnancy (infections, drugs), birth (asphyxia, prematurity), neonatal jaundice, meningitis, family history, consanguinity, milestones, regression
  2. Examination: OFC, dysmorphic features, skin (neurocutaneous), tone and reflexes, vision and hearing
  3. Developmental age in each field · developmental quotient (DQ) = developmental age ÷ chronological age × 100
  4. Tests as indicated: hearing and vision, TSH, karyotype, CK (boys not walking), CT/MRI brain, EEG if fits
  5. Multidisciplinary care: physiotherapy, speech therapy, occupational therapy, special schooling
