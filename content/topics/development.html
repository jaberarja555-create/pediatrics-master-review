Development is the gain of skills in four fields. The sequence of milestones is the same in all children; the rate varies. Development goes from head to feet (head control → sitting → standing → walking) and from reflex to voluntary movement.

Fields of development

Gross motorPosture and movement: head control, sitting, standing, walking · fastest progress in the first year
Fine motor and visionGrasp, pincer, cubes, drawing · needs normal vision · fastest after 1 year
Hearing, speech and languageBabble → words → sentences · needs normal hearing · fastest after 18 months
Social, emotional and behaviourSmiling, stranger awareness, feeding, dressing, toilet control, play · fastest after 2½ years

Interpreting milestones

  • Median age: half of normal children have the skill · limit age: about 2 SD later, the age by which almost all have it · failure to reach the limit age needs assessment
  • Preterm: use corrected age (subtract the weeks born early) until 2 years: a 9-month-old born at 28 weeks is assessed as a 6-month-old
  • Assess each field separately, then compare with the child's age: delay in one field (specific) or in two or more (global)
  • Bottom shufflers: move sitting on the bottom, often skip crawling, walk late (up to 18–24 months), often familial, normal tone and other fields · a normal variant: no investigation

Abnormal reflexes and postural reactions

Moro absent on both sidesCNS depression: HIE, kernicterus, intracranial bleed, sepsis, sedation
Moro absent on one sideErb's palsy, fractured clavicle or humerus, shoulder dislocation
Persistent beyond 6 monthsCerebral palsy: Moro, ATNR, grasp persist
Obligatory ATNRAbnormal at any age
  • Postural (protective) reactions appear as primitive reflexes go and persist for life: they are not primitive reflexes
  • Lateral propping: 6–7 months (needed to sit alone)
  • Parachute reaction: appears at 9 months · infant tilted head-down → arms extend to protect
  • Extensor plantar response (Babinski) is normal until the child walks (12–18 months)
Exam trap

The parachute is a protective reaction, not a primitive reflex. An extensor plantar is normal at 7 months; a Moro reflex or head lag at 7 months is not.

Definitions

Developmental delayMilestones not reached by the limit age · in one field (specific) or two or more fields (global)
RegressionLoss of skills already gained · always serious: neurodegenerative or metabolic disease, autism, uncontrolled epilepsy, hypothyroidism
Intellectual disabilityGlobal delay that persists (low IQ, poor adaptive function) · confirmed when older

Causes

Global delayPrenatal: chromosomal (Down syndrome), TORCH, fetal alcohol, brain malformation, congenital hypothyroidism · Perinatal: birth asphyxia (HIE), prematurity (IVH, PVL), kernicterus, hypoglycaemia · Postnatal: meningitis, encephalitis, head injury, severe malnutrition, deprivation
Gross motorCerebral palsy · neuromuscular (Duchenne in boys) · spina bifida · rickets, hypotonia · severe chronic illness · bottom shuffling (normal variant)
SpeechHearing loss (exclude first) · intellectual disability · autism · lack of stimulation · structural (cleft palate)
SocialAutism spectrum disorder · deprivation · hearing or visual loss

Assessment of a child with delay

  • History: pregnancy (infection, drugs), birth (asphyxia, prematurity), neonatal (jaundice, seizures, NICU) · consanguinity, family history · current skills in each field · regression
  • Examination: OFC, weight, length · dysmorphic features · skin (neurocutaneous signs) · tone, power, reflexes · vision and hearing · developmental examination by play
  • Investigations (guided by findings): hearing test (every speech delay) · vision · thyroid function · CK (boy with motor delay) · karyotype · MRI brain · metabolic screen (regression) · EEG (seizures)
  • Management: early intervention by a team: physiotherapy, speech and occupational therapy, special education · treat the cause (e.g. thyroxine) · support the family
Best next step

Isolated speech delay → hearing test first. Regression at any age → urgent referral: MRI brain and metabolic screen.
