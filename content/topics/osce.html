Paediatric OSCE stations last 5–10 minutes. Marks are given for a structured approach: introduce yourself, hand hygiene, permission, explain to the mother, do the task in order, summarise, and give the likely diagnosis and next steps. The stations below have appeared in previous years.

Plotting and interpreting a growth chart

  1. Choose the correct chart (boys/girls, WHO) and measurement (weight, length/height, OFC)
  2. Calculate the exact age (corrected if preterm and < 2 years) · plot the point accurately with a dot
  3. State the centile/Z-score and the trend (following a line, crossing centiles, flat)
  4. Interpret: normal, underweight, wasting, stunting, FTT, overweight · compare with OFC and height · give causes and next step

Developmental assessment

  • Greet, put the child on the mother's lap, use toys (cubes, ball, crayon, picture book)
  • Assess each field: gross motor → fine motor and vision → speech and hearing → social
  • Give a developmental age range for each field, then compare with the chronological age
  • Conclude: normal, global or specific delay · suggest causes and plan (hearing test, TSH, referral)
  • Common: "This child sits without support, has a pincer grasp, says mama: age ≈ 9–10 months"

Preparing and teaching ORS

  1. Wash hands · use 1 litre of clean (boiled and cooled) water · pour the whole packet · mix well
  2. Give by cup and spoon, small frequent sips · if vomits, wait 10 minutes and give more slowly
  3. Amount: < 2 years 50–100 mL and 2–10 years 100–200 mL after each loose stool (Plan A) · Plan B 75 mL/kg in 4 h
  4. Use within 24 hours, then discard · continue breastfeeding and food · give zinc · return if danger signs

Vaccination history and counselling

  • Ask for the card · check each vaccine against the Sudan EPI schedule (1.4) · identify missed doses
  • Ask about reactions (fever, abscess, BCG scar) and contraindications
  • Explain the next due vaccines and their dates · reassure about mild reactions (fever, pain: paracetamol)
  • Missed doses: catch up without restarting

Common picture spotters

  • Kwashiorkor (oedema, flaky skin) vs marasmus (old-man face)
  • Rickets (bow legs, rosary, wide wrists)
  • Down syndrome face, single palmar crease
  • Hydrocephalus (sunset eyes), myelomeningocele
  • Measles rash, Koplik spots · chickenpox
  • Neonatal jaundice, cephalhaematoma vs caput
  • Erb's palsy (waiter's tip)
  • Periorbital oedema (nephrotic) · HSP purpura
  • Dactylitis (sickle cell) · frontal bossing (thalassaemia)
  • CXR: boot-shaped heart, consolidation, effusion, double bubble
