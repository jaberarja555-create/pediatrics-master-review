Perinatal (birth) asphyxia is a failure of gas exchange before, during or just after birth, causing hypoxia, hypercapnia and metabolic acidosis. Its brain injury is hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy (HIE), a major cause of neonatal death and of cerebral palsy in Sudan, where many labours are prolonged or obstructed.

Causes

  • Maternal: hypotension, eclampsia, severe anaemia, shock
  • Placental: abruption, placenta praevia bleeding, insufficiency
  • Cord: prolapse, tight knot, compression
  • Labour: prolonged / obstructed labour, oxytocin hyperstimulation, difficult breech or instrumental delivery, shoulder dystocia
  • Fetal: anaemia, infection, anomalies · sedative drugs to mother

Diagnosis of HIE

  • Evidence of asphyxia: Apgar ≤ 5 at 10 min or need for resuscitation at 10 min, cord/early pH < 7.0 or base deficit ≥ 12
  • Plus encephalopathy in the first hours: abnormal consciousness, tone, reflexes, feeding, seizures
  • Plus multi-organ injury: kidney (oliguria, AKI), heart (shock), liver (↑ enzymes), gut (NEC), DIC, SIADH, hypoglycaemia

Management of HIE (supportive)

  1. Ventilation and oxygen: avoid both hypoxia and hyperoxia; keep PaCO₂ normal
  2. Perfusion: treat hypotension (saline 10 mL/kg, dopamine)
  3. Fluids: restrict to 60 mL/kg/day on day 1 (risk of SIADH and AKI); monitor urine output, weight, Na
  4. Glucose 2.6–7 mmol/L: avoid both hypo- and hyperglycaemia · correct Ca, Mg
  5. Temperature: avoid hyperthermia · therapeutic hypothermia (33.5 °C for 72 h, started within 6 h) for moderate–severe HIE in NICUs with full intensive care
  6. Treat seizures (below) · no routine prophylactic phenobarbitone · nil by mouth for 24–72 h, then breast milk by NG

Neonatal seizures

  • Often subtle: lip smacking, chewing, eye deviation, blinking, cycling of the legs, apnoea with tachycardia · also tonic, clonic, myoclonic
  • Jitteriness is not a seizure: stopped by holding the limb, provoked by touch, no eye signs
Causes
  • HIE (commonest, day 1)
  • Hypoglycaemia, hypocalcaemia, hypo/hypernatraemia, hypomagnesaemia
  • Meningitis, sepsis, TORCH · intracranial haemorrhage
  • Kernicterus · drug withdrawal · pyridoxine dependency, inborn errors

Treatment of neonatal seizures

FirstABC, oxygen · check glucose: if low, 10% dextrose 2 mL/kg IV · check Ca: if low, 10% calcium gluconate 2 mL/kg IV slowly (ECG)
First-line drugPhenobarbitone 20 mg/kg IV over 20 min · extra 10 mg/kg up to a total of 40 mg/kg
Second linePhenytoin 20 mg/kg IV over 30 min (ECG) · or levetiracetam 40–60 mg/kg · midazolam infusion if refractory
MaintenancePhenobarbitone 5 mg/kg/day once daily · stop when seizure-free for 72 h and the examination is normal
InvestigateGlucose, Ca, Mg, Na, CBC, blood culture, LP, cranial ultrasound, EEG
Exam trap

Diazepam is avoided in newborns (apnoea, contains benzoate that displaces bilirubin). Always check and correct glucose before anticonvulsants. A seizure on day 5–7 in a well baby fed cow's milk formula suggests late hypocalcaemia (high-phosphate feeds).
