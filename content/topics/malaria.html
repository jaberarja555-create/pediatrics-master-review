Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites spread by the female Anopheles mosquito. P. falciparum causes about 90% of cases in Sudan and almost all severe malaria; P. vivax causes most of the rest. Children under 5 years carry most of the deaths. Treatment follows the WHO 2025 malaria guidelines and the national malaria treatment protocol.

Life cycle and how it causes disease

  • Sporozoites (mosquito bite) → liver → merozoites → red cells: each cycle of red cell rupture causes fever with rigors · some become gametocytes (taken up by mosquitoes)
  • Falciparum: infected red cells stick to vessel walls (cytoadherence, sequestration) in brain, kidney, gut → cerebral malaria, acidosis · vivax and ovale leave hypnozoites in the liver → relapses
  • Protection: sickle cell trait, HbF in young infants, maternal antibodies in the first months

Uncomplicated malaria

Fever (any pattern in children), chills, headache, vomiting, diarrhoea, cough, poor feeding, febrile convulsion · pallor and a palpable spleen · in a malaria area every febrile child is tested.

Diagnosis

Blood film (microscopy)Thick film to find parasites and count them · thin film to name the species (falciparum: small ring forms, several per cell, banana-shaped gametocytes) · shows parasite density and response to treatment · the gold standard
Rapid diagnostic test (RDT)Detects parasite antigen (HRP2 for falciparum, pLDH) in 15 minutes · no microscope needed · stays positive for weeks after treatment (HRP2), so cannot follow response
Other testsCBC (anaemia, low platelets) · blood glucose · creatinine, bilirubin, lactate in severe malaria · LP if meningitis cannot be excluded
Falciparum: ring forms and a banana-shaped gametocyte
Malaria RDT: control and test lines both positive
Exam trap
  • Treat for malaria only after a positive test (RDT or film); a negative test means look for another cause.
  • A child with sickle cell trait is partly protected against severe falciparum malaria; a child with sickle cell disease is at great risk from it.

Severe (complicated) malaria (WHO): any one

ClinicalImpaired consciousness (cerebral malaria) · prostration (cannot sit or feed) · multiple convulsions (> 2 in 24 h) · respiratory distress (acidotic breathing) · shock · pulmonary oedema · significant bleeding · jaundice
LaboratoryHypoglycaemia < 2.2 mmol/L (40 mg/dL) · severe anaemia Hb < 5 g/dL · acidosis (lactate ≥ 5 mmol/L) · kidney injury · hyperparasitaemia > 10% · haemoglobinuria (blackwater)

Treatment

Uncomplicated falciparumArtemisinin-based combination (ACT): artemether–lumefantrine (first line), 6 doses over 3 days (0, 8, 24, 36, 48, 60 h) with milk or fatty food · by weight: 5–14 kg 1 tablet, 15–24 kg 2, 25–34 kg 3, ≥ 35 kg 4 per dose · second line: dihydroartemisinin–piperaquine
VivaxACT, then primaquine 0.25–0.5 mg/kg daily for 14 days (radical cure) after a G6PD test · not under 6 months
Severe malariaIV (or IM) artesunate: 3 mg/kg if < 20 kg · 2.4 mg/kg if ≥ 20 kg at 0, 12 and 24 h, then daily · for at least 24 h and until the child can take oral drugs, then a full 3-day ACT · if not available: IM artemether or quinine (20 mg/kg loading, then 10 mg/kg 8-hourly)
Before referralRectal artesunate 10 mg/kg (children under 6 years) or IM artesunate, then refer
SupportiveCheck glucose (10% glucose 5 ml/kg IV if low) · transfuse if Hb < 4 g/dL, or < 6 g/dL with distress or shock (10 ml/kg packed cells) · treat convulsions (diazepam) · careful fluids (no fluid overload) · antibiotics if bacterial infection is possible · paracetamol
Best next step · a febrile child with a positive malaria test becomes unconscious
  • Check blood glucose at once and correct it; give IV artesunate without waiting for other results.
  • Treat convulsions, and consider meningitis: give antibiotics too, and do an LP when it is safe.

Prevention

  • Insecticide-treated bed nets · indoor spraying · drain stagnant water
  • Malaria vaccine (RTS,S or R21) from 5 months, 4 doses, in moderate–high transmission areas (WHO)
  • Intermittent preventive treatment in pregnancy (sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine) · seasonal chemoprevention where used

Try it · exam-style
How long is IV artesunate given in severe malaria?
ANSWERAt least 24 hours and until oral drugs are tolerated, then a full 3-day ACT.
Name four features of severe malaria in a child.
ANSWERImpaired consciousness, prostration, repeated convulsions, severe anaemia (also hypoglycaemia, acidosis).
