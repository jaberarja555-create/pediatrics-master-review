Malaria is a protozoan infection transmitted by the female Anopheles mosquito. In Sudan it is endemic (highest after the rainy season, July–November), and Plasmodium falciparum causes most cases and nearly all deaths; P. vivax is increasing. Children under 5 have little immunity and develop severe malaria quickly.

Life cycle and its clinical meaning

Uncomplicated malaria

  • Fever (often without a pattern in children), rigors, sweating, headache, body aches
  • Vomiting, diarrhoea, cough, poor feeding: can mimic gastroenteritis or pneumonia
  • Pallor, mild jaundice, splenomegaly (repeated attacks), hepatomegaly
  • Incubation 7–30 days (falciparum ≈ 12 days)

Severe malaria (WHO): any one in a child with P. falciparum

Cerebral malariaImpaired consciousness (Blantyre coma score ≤ 2) or repeated convulsions (≥ 2 in 24 h)
ProstrationUnable to sit, stand or feed
Severe anaemiaHb < 5 g/dL (Hct < 15%)
Respiratory distressDeep (acidotic) breathing · pulmonary oedema
MetabolicHypoglycaemia (< 2.2 mmol/L) · acidosis (bicarbonate < 15) · lactate > 5
OthersShock (algid malaria) · jaundice · AKI · haemoglobinuria (blackwater) · abnormal bleeding · hyperparasitaemia > 10%

Diagnosis

  • Thick blood film: detects parasites (sensitive) · thin film: species and % parasitaemia · gold standard (microscopy)
  • RDT (HRP2 antigen): quick; stays positive 2–4 weeks after treatment
  • In severe malaria also: glucose, Hb, CBC (low platelets), RFT, bilirubin, blood gas, blood culture (co-infection with salmonella)
  • LP when unsure about meningitis (after stabilising)

Treatment of severe malaria

Drug of choiceIV (or IM) artesunate at 0, 12 and 24 h, then daily until the child can take oral drugs (at least 24 h) · dose 3 mg/kg if < 20 kg · 2.4 mg/kg if ≥ 20 kg
ThenA full 3-day course of oral ACT (artemether–lumefantrine)
AlternativeQuinine 20 mg/kg loading in 10 mL/kg 5% dextrose over 4 h, then 10 mg/kg 8-hourly over 2–4 h · risk of hypoglycaemia, cinchonism, arrhythmia · or IM artemether
Pre-referralRectal artesunate 10 mg/kg or IM artesunate when the child cannot be treated on site

Supportive care in severe malaria

  • Hypoglycaemia: 10% dextrose 5 mL/kg IV · check glucose 4-hourly (and on every change of consciousness)
  • Convulsions: diazepam 0.5 mg/kg rectal or 0.3 mg/kg IV, then phenobarbitone
  • Severe anaemia: transfuse whole blood 20 mL/kg (packed cells 10 mL/kg) over 3–4 h if Hb < 4 g/dL, or < 6 with distress or shock
  • Fluids: maintenance only; avoid overload (pulmonary oedema) · treat shock and dehydration
  • Fever: paracetamol · antibiotics (ceftriaxone) if shocked or meningitis not excluded
  • Coma care: lateral position, NG tube, turn 2-hourly, catheter
  • No steroids, no mannitol, no heparin

Uncomplicated malaria

SpeciesTreatment (Sudan national protocol)
P. falciparumArtemether–lumefantrine (Coartem, 20/120 mg tablets) twice daily for 3 days (6 doses: 0, 8, 24, 36, 48, 60 h) with fatty food or milk · 5–14 kg: 1 tab · 15–24 kg: 2 · 25–34 kg: 3 · ≥ 35 kg: 4 per dose
P. vivaxACT (or chloroquine) + primaquine 0.25 mg/kg daily for 14 days to kill hypnozoites (check G6PD first; not under 6 months)
FailureSymptoms or parasites persisting after day 3 · vomiting within 30 min: repeat the dose · if unable to take orally: treat as severe

Prevention

  • Long-lasting insecticide-treated nets (LLIN) for children and pregnant women
  • Indoor residual spraying, drain stagnant water, larvicides
  • IPTp with SP in pregnancy · seasonal malaria chemoprevention in high-risk areas
  • RTS,S / R21 malaria vaccines in selected programmes
