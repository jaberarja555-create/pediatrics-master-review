Breast milk is the ideal food for the infant. WHO and the Sudan Ministry of Health recommend early initiation within 1 hour of birth, exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, then safe complementary foods with continued breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond.

Advantages

For the baby
  • ↓ diarrhoea, pneumonia, otitis, NEC, sepsis
  • Ideal nutrition, easily digested, clean, at the right temperature
  • Better IQ · ↓ later obesity, diabetes, allergy
For the mother and family
  • Bonding · uterine involution (oxytocin) · less PPH
  • Contraception (lactational amenorrhoea, if exclusive and under 6 months)
  • ↓ breast and ovarian cancer · free, always available

Good attachment and adequate milk

Signs of good attachment
  • Chin touching the breast · mouth wide open
  • Lower lip turned out · more areola above than below
  • Slow, deep sucks with pauses; no pain
Baby is getting enough
  • ≥ 6 wet nappies a day after day 5
  • Good weight gain (back to birth weight by 2 weeks)
  • Settled after feeds · feeds 8–12 times a day on demand

Complementary feeding (weaning)

  • Start at 6 months: breast milk alone no longer meets the need for energy, iron and zinc
  • Too early (< 4 months): diarrhoea, allergy, less breast milk · too late: malnutrition, iron deficiency, feeding difficulty
  • Start with soft, thick porridge (asida, rice, potato), then mashed family food; add animal-source food (egg, liver, meat, fish), legumes (ful, lentils), oil, fruit and vegetables
  • Responsive feeding with a cup and spoon (no bottle) · good hygiene · iodised salt
  • During and after illness: more frequent feeds and continue breastfeeding

Avoid before 1 year: whole cow's milk as the main drink (iron deficiency, occult GI bleeding), honey (botulism), whole nuts (choking), tea with meals (blocks iron).

Formula feeding

  • Indications: no breast milk (maternal death or absence), galactosaemia (special formula), mother on contraindicated drugs
  • Infant formula is modified cow's milk: protein and solutes reduced, iron and vitamins added
  • Preterm formula: more energy, protein, Ca and PO₄
  • Prepare with boiled, cooled water and the exact number of scoops; clean the cup or bottle each feed
  • Over-diluted: FTT, malnutrition · over-concentrated: hypernatraemic dehydration, obesity
  • Dirty bottles → diarrhoea: cup feeding is preferred

Common breastfeeding problems

Sore, cracked nipplesPoor attachment: correct the position, apply hind-milk; continue feeding
EngorgementFrequent feeding, express milk, warm compress
MastitisContinue feeding (or express) · flucloxacillin or cloxacillin 10–14 days · abscess: drain
"Not enough milk"Usually poor attachment or infrequent feeds; check weight and wet nappies before giving formula
