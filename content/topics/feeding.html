Breast milk is the best food for every infant. WHO and IMCI advise: start breastfeeding within the first hour, give only breast milk for 6 months, then add complementary food and continue breastfeeding to 2 years or beyond.

Physiology of lactation

ReflexHormoneEffect
Milk productionProlactin (anterior pituitary)Suckling → milk secretion · more suckling = more milk
Milk ejection (let-down)Oxytocin (posterior pituitary)Milk flows out · also contracts the uterus · stress and pain inhibit it
Infant reflexesRooting, sucking, swallowingCoordinated sucking and swallowing from about 34 weeks

Types of breast milk

ColostrumFirst 3–5 days · small volume, thick, yellow · more protein, IgA, white cells and vitamin A, less fat and lactose · mild laxative (passes meconium)
Transitional milkDay 5 to 2 weeks
Mature milkFrom about 2 weeks · foremilk (start of feed): watery, quenches thirst · hindmilk (end of feed): 3 times more fat, gives satiety
Preterm mother's milkMore protein, fat and IgA than term milk · needs a fortifier for very low birth weight infants
Exam trap
  • Hindmilk (end of the feed) has more fat than foremilk, not the other way round.
  • Colostrum has more IgA and protein than mature milk.

Try it · exam-style
Which hormone produces milk and which ejects it?
ANSWERProlactin produces milk; oxytocin ejects it (let-down).
Is breast milk protein higher or lower than cow's milk?
ANSWERLower (about one third), mainly easily digested whey.

Good position and attachment (IMCI)

  • Position: infant's head and body in a straight line · body close to the mother, facing the breast · whole body supported
  • Good attachment: chin touches the breast · mouth wide open · lower lip turned out · more areola above the mouth than below
  • Effective suckling: slow deep sucks with pauses · swallowing heard · cheeks round, not pulled in
  • Feed on demand, day and night, at least 8 times in 24 hours
  • Empty one breast, then offer the other · start the next feed on the second breast
  • No water, other drinks or teats during the first 6 months
  • Burp the baby after the feed
Good attachment (left): chin on the breast, mouth wide open, lower lip out · poor (right): lips pushed forward on the nipple

Best next step · a breastfed baby gaining poorly
  • Observe a full feed: position, attachment, suckling
  • Correct the technique and feed more often · examine mother and baby for a cause
  • Add formula only if weight gain stays poor after correction

Breast problems

Sore, cracked nipplesPoor attachment (commonest) or candida · correct attachment · treat both mother and baby if candida
EngorgementFull, painful breasts from incomplete emptying · feed more often, express milk before feeds
MastitisRed, tender area with fever, usually after week 2 · Staphylococcus aureus · continue breastfeeding from that breast · oral flucloxacillin (or cloxacillin) + analgesia
Breast abscessFluctuant mass · drainage + antibiotics · feed from the other breast, express the affected side

InfantGalactosaemia (classic absolute contraindication) · PKU: limited breast milk with low-phenylalanine formula

Formula feeding

  • Indications: no breast milk (mother absent or ill) · contraindication to breastfeeding · not enough milk after correcting technique
  • Volume: about 150 ml/kg/day (≈ 100–110 kcal/kg/day) in 6–8 feeds
  • Preparation: clean, boiled bottle and water · exact scoops on the tin (1 scoop to 30 ml or 60 ml) · discard leftovers
  • Cup feeding is safer than a bottle (easier to clean)
MistakeResult
Over-diluted (too much water)Poor weight gain, failure to thrive

Exam trap
  • Whole cow's milk before 1 year causes iron deficiency anaemia (low, poorly absorbed iron and gut blood loss).
  • Goat's milk lacks folate and B12 → megaloblastic anaemia; unboiled goat's milk transmits brucellosis.

Complementary feeding (weaning)

AgeMeals a day (plus breastfeeding)Texture
6–8 months2–3 meals + 1–2 snacksThick porridge, mashed food
9–11 months3–4 meals + 1–2 snacksFinely chopped, finger foods
12–24 months3–4 meals + 1–2 snacksFamily food
  • Start at 6 months: breast milk alone no longer meets energy, iron and zinc needs
  • Not before 4 months: immature gut, more infections and allergy, less breast milk
  • Late weaning (after 6–9 months) → wasting, iron deficiency, rickets
  • One new food at a time · thick, not watery · add oil, egg, beans, meat, liver, fruit and vegetables
  • Avoid: honey under 1 year (botulism) · tea, sugary drinks · nuts and whole grapes (choking) · added salt
  • Responsive feeding, clean hands and utensils, feed more after illness
IMCI · feeding recommendations
  • Under 6 months: breastfeed as often as the child wants, at least 8 times in 24 hours; no other food or fluid
  • From 6 months: breastfeed + adequate complementary food; continue breastfeeding to 2 years
  • During and after illness: continue feeding and give one extra meal a day for 2 weeks
