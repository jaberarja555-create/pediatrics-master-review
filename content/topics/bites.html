Snake bite is a common emergency in rural Sudan (farmers, herders, children playing outside), mostly from vipers (carpet viper, Echis; puff adder) and cobras. Children receive a larger venom dose for their size. Most bites are dry (no venom), but envenomed children need antivenom early.

Effects of venom

TypeSnakesFeatures
Haemotoxic (vipers)Carpet viper (Echis), puff adderLocal swelling, pain, blistering, necrosis · bleeding: gums, bite site, haematuria, haematemesis · incoagulable blood · shock, AKI
Neurotoxic (elapids)Cobras, mambasPtosis (first sign), diplopia, difficulty swallowing and talking, drooling, respiratory paralysis · little local swelling (except spitting cobra: necrosis)

First aid

  • Reassure, keep the child still, immobilise the limb with a splint
  • Remove rings and tight clothing · transport to hospital quickly
  • Do not: cut, suck, apply tight tourniquets, ice, herbal remedies or kill the snake (a photo helps)
  • Pressure bandage only for neurotoxic elapid bites if available

Hospital assessment

  • Vital signs, airway, ptosis, swelling (mark and measure every hour), bleeding
  • 20-minute whole blood clotting test (20WBCT): 2 mL blood in a clean glass tube, leave 20 min, tilt: not clotted = coagulopathy → antivenom
  • CBC (platelets), PT/INR, fibrinogen, creatinine, K, urine (blood, myoglobin), CK, ECG
  • Observe at least 24 h, repeat 20WBCT every 6 h

Antivenom

IndicationsSystemic envenoming: incoagulable blood (20WBCT), spontaneous bleeding, neurotoxic signs, shock, AKI · severe local swelling (more than half the limb, rapid spread, bites on fingers)
DosePolyvalent antivenom (for African snakes): the same dose for children as adults (it neutralises venom, not body weight) · IV over 30–60 min in saline
ReactionsAnaphylaxis is common: adrenaline ready (0.01 mL/kg IM 1:1000) · stop, treat, restart slowly
RepeatRepeat the dose if blood is still incoagulable after 6 h, or neurotoxicity worsens after 1–2 h
SupportiveNeurotoxic: ventilation, neostigmine + atropine trial · fluids, blood products (after antivenom), tetanus prophylaxis, wound care, antibiotics if infected, surgery for necrosis (no prophylactic fasciotomy)

Scorpion sting

Very common in Sudan (northern and central states, summer nights); dangerous scorpions (Leiurus, Androctonus) release a neurotoxin that causes a massive release of catecholamines and acetylcholine. Young children are at greatest risk of death.

GradeFeatures
I · localSevere local pain, paraesthesia, little swelling
II · systemic (mild)Sweating, vomiting, salivation, restlessness, tachycardia, hypertension, priapism
III · severeCardiogenic shock, pulmonary oedema, arrhythmia, hypertension then hypotension, convulsions, coma, hyperglycaemia
Treatment
  • Local: cold compress, local anaesthetic infiltration (lidocaine), paracetamol · tetanus prophylaxis
  • Admit all young children with systemic signs · monitor HR, BP, SpO₂, ECG, glucose
  • Scorpion antivenom IV for systemic envenoming (grade II–III)
  • Prazosin 30 µg/kg orally, repeated every 3–6 h (for hypertension and to prevent pulmonary oedema)
  • Pulmonary oedema: oxygen, CPAP/ventilation, dobutamine for shock, furosemide cautiously
  • Convulsions: diazepam · avoid atropine routinely

Dog bite and rabies prevention

  • Wound care is the most important step: wash with soap and running water for 15 minutes, then povidone-iodine · do not suture deep bites early (except the face)
  • Antibiotics (co-amoxiclav) for deep wounds, hands, face · tetanus prophylaxis
  • Rabies is almost always fatal once symptoms start (hydrophobia, aerophobia, agitation, paralysis)
  • Post-exposure prophylaxis: rabies vaccine (days 0, 3, 7, 14 [and 28]) for any bite or scratch from a suspect animal · plus rabies immunoglobulin infiltrated into the wound for category III exposures (bleeding bites, licks on broken skin or mucosa)
  • Observe the dog for 10 days if possible
