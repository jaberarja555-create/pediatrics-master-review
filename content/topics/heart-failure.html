Heart failure is the inability of the heart to pump enough blood for the body's needs. In infants it is mostly due to congenital heart disease with large left-to-right shunts; in older Sudanese children rheumatic heart disease, myocarditis and severe anaemia are the main causes. Infants show it as breathlessness and sweating during feeds, not as ankle oedema.

Causes by age

AgeCauses
First weekDuct-dependent systemic lesions: HLHS, critical coarctation, critical AS · severe asphyxia, arrhythmia (SVT), sepsis, severe anaemia (hydrops)
1–3 monthsLarge L → R shunts: VSD, AVSD, PDA (as pulmonary resistance falls) · anomalous coronary artery (ALCAPA)
Older childRheumatic heart disease (mitral/aortic regurgitation), acute rheumatic carditis · myocarditis, dilated cardiomyopathy · severe anaemia (sickle cell, malaria) · hypertension (acute GN) · endocarditis · thyrotoxicosis · fluid overload (transfusion, AKI)

Clinical features

Infants
  • Tachypnoea, sweating and breathlessness on feeding, takes long to feed
  • Failure to thrive, recurrent chest infections
  • Tachycardia, gallop rhythm (S3)
  • Hepatomegaly (best sign of right failure in infants)
  • Cardiomegaly · oedema of eyelids and sacrum (late)
Older children
  • Exercise intolerance, orthopnoea, cough
  • Raised JVP, ankle oedema, tender hepatomegaly, ascites
  • Basal crackles (pulmonary oedema)
  • Cool peripheries, weak pulses, low BP (cardiogenic shock)

Investigations

  • CXR: cardiomegaly (CTR > 0.6 in infants, > 0.5 in older children), pulmonary oedema
  • Echocardiography: cause and function (key test)
  • ECG: hypertrophy, arrhythmia, ischaemia · CBC (anaemia), electrolytes, RFT, ASO/ESR (rheumatic), troponin (myocarditis), BNP

Best sign of heart failure in an infant?
ANSWERHepatomegaly (with tachypnoea and sweating on feeds).
Commonest cause of heart failure at 2 months?
ANSWERLarge left-to-right shunt (VSD).

General measures

  • Sit up (infant seat, 30–45°) · oxygen if SpO₂ low (care in duct-dependent and large shunts)
  • Feeding: high-energy feeds (150–180 kcal/kg/day), small and frequent, NG tube if tiring
  • Fluid restriction to about ⅔–¾ of maintenance in decompensated failure
  • Treat anaemia (packed cells slowly, with furosemide), infection, fever, hypertension, arrhythmia
  • Daily weight, fluid balance, liver size, RR, HR

Monitoring the response

  • Improving: RR and HR fall, liver shrinks, good diuresis, weight falls then rises as feeding improves
  • Not improving: look for the cause that needs surgery (shunt, valve), arrhythmia, anaemia, infection, endocarditis, poor adherence

Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)

  • Commonest arrhythmia in children · HR > 220 (infant) or > 180 (child), narrow QRS, no beat-to-beat variation · infants present with heart failure
  • WPW syndrome (delta wave) in some
  • Vagal manoeuvres: ice-water to the face (infants), Valsalva
  • Adenosine 0.1 mg/kg rapid IV push (then 0.2, 0.3 mg/kg)
  • Shocked: synchronised DC cardioversion 1 J/kg (then 2 J/kg)
