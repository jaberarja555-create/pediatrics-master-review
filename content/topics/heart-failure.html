Heart failure is the state in which the heart cannot pump enough blood for the body's needs. In infants the commonest cause is congenital heart disease with a large left-to-right shunt; in older children in Africa it is rheumatic heart disease, myocarditis and severe anaemia. In infants it shows mainly as breathlessness and sweating on feeding, a big liver and poor weight gain.

Causes by age

First weekDuct-dependent left-heart obstruction: hypoplastic left heart, critical coarctation or aortic stenosis · asphyxia (myocardial damage) · arrhythmia (SVT, complete heart block) · severe anaemia, sepsis, hypoglycaemia
Infancy (4–12 weeks)Large left-to-right shunts as pulmonary resistance falls: VSD, AVSD, PDA, truncus · viral myocarditis, cardiomyopathy · SVT
Older childRheumatic carditis and rheumatic valve disease · myocarditis (viral, diphtheria) · dilated cardiomyopathy · acute hypertension (post-streptococcal GN, HUS) · infective endocarditis · severe anaemia (Hb < 5 g/dl: sickle cell, malaria) · thyrotoxicosis · cor pulmonale · fluid overload (transfusion)

Clinical features

  • Infant: breathless and sweating during feeds, takes a little and stops, poor weight gain, irritability · tachypnoea, indrawing, tachycardia, gallop rhythm · enlarged, tender liver (the most reliable sign) · cool peripheries · basal crackles, wheeze
  • Oedema of the eyelids and sacrum appears late; neck veins are hard to see in infants (short neck)
  • Older child: exercise intolerance, orthopnoea, cough, abdominal pain (liver) · raised JVP, ankle oedema, hepatomegaly · gallop, murmur of the cause · ascites in severe cases
  • Cardiomegaly is almost always present (displaced apex)

Investigations

  • Chest X-ray: cardiomegaly, pulmonary congestion, effusions · echocardiography: cause and ventricular function (the key test) · ECG: rhythm, hypertrophy, low voltage (effusion)
  • CBC (anaemia, infection), electrolytes and creatinine (before diuretics and digoxin), urine and BP (nephritis), ASO, blood culture if febrile

Treatment

GeneralSit up (infant seat) · oxygen if hypoxic · fluid restriction to about 2/3 of maintenance in congestion · high-calorie feeds, small and frequent, NG feeding if tired · treat anaemia, infection, fever
DiureticsFurosemide 1 mg/kg IV or orally, 1–3 times a day (oral 1–4 mg/kg/day) · add spironolactone 1 mg/kg twice daily to keep potassium · watch hypokalaemia and alkalosis
Afterload reductionCaptopril 0.1 mg/kg test dose, then up to 0.5–1 mg/kg 8-hourly (ACE inhibitor) · for shunts, regurgitant valves, cardiomyopathy · avoid in outflow obstruction (aortic stenosis) · check BP and creatinine
InotropesDigoxin orally for chronic failure (infant maintenance 5 μg/kg twice daily) · IV dobutamine or dopamine, milrinone in ICU for shock
Treat the causeSurgery for the CHD · steroids and penicillin in rheumatic carditis · antihypertensives · transfusion of packed cells slowly with furosemide in severe anaemia · control the arrhythmia
Exam trap
  • The earliest sign of heart failure in an infant is poor feeding with breathlessness and sweating; leg oedema is not an early sign in infants.
  • Splenomegaly is not a sign of heart failure; an enlarged tender liver is.
  • Do not give digoxin in tetralogy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy or heart block.

Try it · exam-style
Give the commonest cause of heart failure in an infant and in a school child in Africa.
ANSWERInfant: congenital heart disease with a large left-to-right shunt (VSD); school child: rheumatic heart disease.

Digoxin

  • Action: increases contractility, slows AV conduction · narrow safety margin
  • Before starting: ECG, potassium, creatinine · lower doses in preterm babies and renal impairment
  • Contraindications: tetralogy of Fallot, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (outflow obstruction), heart block, WPW
  • Toxicity (made worse by hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypercalcaemia, myocarditis, renal failure): vomiting, poor feeding, bradycardia, any new arrhythmia or heart block, visual upset in older children
  • Treatment of toxicity: stop digoxin, ECG monitoring, correct potassium, atropine for bradycardia, digoxin-specific antibody (Fab) if severe

Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT)

FactsThe commonest arrhythmia in children · re-entry through the AV node or an accessory pathway (WPW: short PR, delta wave) · sudden start and stop
FeaturesInfant: heart rate 220–300, pallor, poor feeding, irritability, heart failure after 24–48 hours · older child: palpitations, chest discomfort, dizziness · ECG: narrow QRS, fixed rate, no variation with crying, P waves hidden
Treatment (stable)Vagal manoeuvres: infant: ice-water bag on the face for 10–15 seconds · child: blow into a syringe (Valsalva) · do not press on the eyes
ThenAdenosine 0.1 mg/kg rapid IV push with a saline flush (then 0.2 mg/kg, max 12 mg) into a large vein near the heart
Unstable (shock)Synchronised DC cardioversion 1 J/kg (then 2 J/kg)
PreventionPropranolol (or digoxin if no WPW) · catheter ablation for recurrent cases

Congenital complete heart block

  • Mother with lupus (anti-Ro, anti-La antibodies cross the placenta) · fetal bradycardia, newborn heart rate 40–80, may cause hydrops or heart failure · pacemaker if symptomatic or rate very low
Best next step · infant with heart rate 260 and poor feeding
  • 12-lead ECG, then ice-water bag to the face; if SVT continues give IV adenosine 0.1 mg/kg fast; if in shock, synchronised cardioversion.
