Asthma is chronic airway inflammation with reversible airflow obstruction and bronchial hyper-responsiveness, causing recurrent wheeze, cough, breathlessness and chest tightness, often worse at night and with triggers. It is the commonest chronic disease of childhood and is increasing in Sudanese cities.

Diagnosis

  • Recurrent wheeze (≥ 3 episodes), night cough, exercise symptoms, response to bronchodilator
  • Atopy: eczema, allergic rhinitis, family history
  • Triggers: viral infections, dust, smoke, pollen, cold air, exercise, animals, aspirin
  • Spirometry (> 5–6 years): FEV₁/FVC < 0.8, ↑ FEV₁ ≥ 12% after salbutamol · PEF variability
  • CXR only to exclude other causes (FB, TB, heart disease)
  • Under 5: clinical diagnosis (trial of treatment)

Treatment of acute asthma

  1. Oxygen to keep SpO₂ 94–98% · sit up
  2. Salbutamol: spacer 2–10 puffs (100 µg each) every 20 min for 1 h, or nebulised 2.5 mg (< 5 yr) / 5 mg (≥ 5 yr) every 20 min × 3
  3. Ipratropium 250 µg nebulised with salbutamol in severe attacks (first hours)
  4. Steroid: prednisolone 1–2 mg/kg orally (max 40 mg) daily for 3–5 days, or hydrocortisone 4 mg/kg IV 6-hourly if vomiting/severe
  5. Not responding: magnesium sulphate 40 mg/kg IV over 20 min (max 2 g) · IV salbutamol · aminophylline (5 mg/kg load over 20 min, then infusion) · ICU

Improving: talks, SpO₂ ≥ 94% in air, salbutamol needed only every 3–4 h. Danger: rising PaCO₂ (normal or high CO₂ in a breathless child), silent chest, exhaustion → ICU. No sedatives; antibiotics only for infection.

Stepwise long-term treatment (children ≥ 6 years, GINA-based)

Step 1As-needed salbutamol (or low-dose ICS–formoterol in older children) for infrequent symptoms
Step 2Daily low-dose inhaled corticosteroid (beclomethasone 100–200 µg/day or budesonide 100–200 µg/day) · alternative: montelukast
Step 3Low-dose ICS + LABA (salmeterol/formoterol) or medium-dose ICS
Step 4Medium-dose ICS + LABA · add montelukast · refer to a specialist
Step 5Specialist care: high-dose ICS, add-on biologics (anti-IgE), lowest possible oral steroid

Under 5 years: step 1 salbutamol as needed · step 2 daily low-dose ICS by MDI + spacer with face mask (or montelukast) · step 3 double the ICS dose · step 4 refer.

Assessing control

Well controlled (last 4 weeks)
  • Day symptoms ≤ 2 times/week
  • No night waking
  • Reliever ≤ 2 times/week
  • No activity limitation
Before stepping up check
  • Inhaler technique (commonest problem) and adherence
  • Triggers at home (smoke, dust), rhinitis, wrong diagnosis
  • Step down after 3 months of good control

Inhaler devices

< 4 yearsMDI + spacer + face mask (5–6 tidal breaths per puff)
4–6 yearsMDI + spacer with mouthpiece
> 6 yearsMDI + spacer, or dry-powder inhaler · rinse mouth after ICS (oral thrush)
Home-made spacerA 500 mL plastic bottle with the MDI fitted into the base works when a spacer is unavailable

Education and side effects

  • Written asthma action plan, trigger avoidance, no smoking at home
  • Influenza vaccine · treat allergic rhinitis
  • ICS: oral thrush, dysphonia, slight reduction in growth velocity (≈ 1 cm, not final height)
  • Salbutamol: tremor, tachycardia, hypokalaemia
