A febrile seizure is a seizure with fever (≥ 38 °C) in a child aged 6 months to 5 years, without CNS infection, metabolic cause or previous afebrile seizures. It affects 3–4% of children and is usually harmless, but meningitis and, in Sudan, cerebral malaria must be excluded.

Febrile status epilepticus: ≥ 30 minutes. Causes of the fever: viral infections (HHV-6 roseola), otitis, tonsillitis, UTI, malaria, after vaccines (DTP, MMR).

Assessment

  • Find the source of fever · malaria test (RDT/film) · glucose
  • Lumbar puncture if: signs of meningitis, < 12 months (signs unreliable), on antibiotics (masked meningitis), complex seizure, slow recovery
  • EEG, CT/MRI, electrolytes not needed in a simple febrile seizure with a clear cause
  • Admit if: first seizure under 18 months, complex seizure, unclear source, unwell, parents anxious

Management

  1. During the seizure: lie on the side (recovery position), clear airway, nothing in the mouth, time it
  2. > 5 minutes: diazepam 0.5 mg/kg rectally or midazolam 0.3 mg/kg buccal, or diazepam 0.3 mg/kg IV (13.2)
  3. Treat the cause of the fever · paracetamol for comfort (does not prevent recurrence)
  4. Reassure and educate the parents

Prognosis and parent advice

  • Recurrence about 30% (higher if first seizure < 18 months, family history, low fever, short fever before seizure)
  • Epilepsy risk: simple ≈ 1–2% (near the general population) · complex 4–6% or more
  • No brain damage, no effect on intelligence
  • Teach first aid, when to call for help (> 5 min, difficult breathing)
  • No long-term anticonvulsants for simple febrile seizures · rescue buccal midazolam / rectal diazepam at home for prolonged ones
  • Vaccines can be continued
