The systemic examination stations follow the same order in every checklist: introduction and positioning, general examination with growth and vital signs, then inspection, palpation, percussion and auscultation of the system, and at the end a summary, differential diagnosis and plan. Marks are lost more often for a missed step than for a missed sign.

Steps of the examination

StartGreet, wash hands, take permission; child sitting on the mother's lap (infant) or at 45°; expose the chest
GeneralWellbeing, consciousness, respiratory distress, pallor, cyanosis, clubbing, dysmorphism; weight, height, OFC; temperature, pulse, RR for a full minute, SpO₂
Inspection
  • Nasal flaring, grunting, head nodding
  • Chest shape: hyperinflation, Harrison's sulcus, pigeon chest, rickety rosary
  • Recession: subcostal, intercostal, suprasternal
  • Scars, chest drains; look from the foot of the bed for less movement on one side
PalpationTrachea (central or shifted), apex beat, chest expansion at three levels, tactile vocal fremitus (older child)
PercussionCompare both sides at each level: dull (consolidation), stony dull (effusion), hyper-resonant (pneumothorax, hyperinflation)
AuscultationAll areas, front and back, comparing sides: air entry, breath sounds (vesicular or bronchial), added sounds (crackles, wheeze, rub), vocal resonance
FinishOffer to examine the back and the ear, nose and throat; cover the child, thank the mother, then summarise

Severity of respiratory distress

Fast breathing (IMCI)≥ 60/min under 2 months · ≥ 50/min at 2–11 months · ≥ 40/min at 1–5 years
Chest indrawingThe lower chest wall moves in when the child breathes in; it is a sign of pneumonia in IMCI and is looked for with the child calm
Severe distressGrunting, very severe chest indrawing, head nodding, inability to feed or talk
HypoxiaCentral cyanosis or SpO₂ < 90%: give oxygen
Late signsAgitation, drowsiness, silent chest, slow breathing: impending respiratory failure

Ear, nose and throat examination

  • Done last, because it upsets the child; the mother holds the child on her lap, one arm around the arms and one hand on the forehead.
  • Throat: tonsils (size, exudate, membrane of diphtheria), palate, pharynx; a tongue depressor and a torch.
  • Ears: otoscope with the pinna pulled backwards and downwards in infants (backwards and upwards in older children); look for a red bulging drum or discharge (otitis media).
  • Nose: discharge, flaring, polyps; sinus tenderness in the older child.

Presenting the findings

Model presentation

Omar is a 3-year-old boy with fever and cough for 4 days. He is in respiratory distress with a respiratory rate of 52/min, subcostal recession and SpO₂ 92% in air. The trachea is central. Over the right lower zone there is reduced expansion, dull percussion, bronchial breathing and crackles. These findings indicate right lower lobe consolidation, most likely pneumonia; I would request a chest X-ray and CBC, give oxygen and start antibiotics.

Formula
Pleural effusion
Trachea shifted away+stony dull percussion+absent breath sounds=Pleural effusion: chest X-ray, tap
