Juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA) is arthritis of unknown cause, starting before 16 years and lasting more than 6 weeks, after other causes are excluded. It is the commonest chronic rheumatic disease of childhood. In Sudan, septic arthritis, rheumatic fever, sickle cell disease, TB and leukaemia must be excluded first.

Diagnosis and treatment

  • Clinical diagnosis of exclusion
  • Joint swelling, morning stiffness, limp, loss of movement (young children rarely complain of pain)
  • Tests: CBC, ESR/CRP, ANA, RF, anti-CCP, HLA-B27
  • Exclude: septic arthritis (joint aspirate), leukaemia (film, marrow), TB, rheumatic fever (ASO, echo), sickle cell
  • X-ray/US
  • NSAIDs (naproxen, ibuprofen) for symptoms
  • Intra-articular steroid injection (oligoarthritis)
  • Methotrexate (first-line DMARD) 10–15 mg/m² weekly + folic acid
  • Biologics (anti-TNF etanercept/adalimumab; anti-IL-6/IL-1 for systemic)
  • Physiotherapy, eye screening, growth and bone health

Kawasaki disease

  • Acute medium-vessel vasculitis of young children (< 5 years)
  • The commonest cause of acquired heart disease in children in developed countries
  • Fever ≥ 5 days + 4 of 5:
    • Conjunctivitis (bilateral, non-purulent)
    • Mouth: red cracked lips, strawberry tongue
    • Hands/feet: redness, oedema → peeling of fingertips (week 2–3)
    • Polymorphous rash
    • Cervical lymph node > 1.5 cm (unilateral)
  • Very irritable child · ↑ ESR/CRP, WBC, platelets ↑ in week 2–3, low albumin, sterile pyuria
  • Coronary artery aneurysms (15–25% untreated; < 5% treated) → MI, sudden death
  • Echocardiogram at diagnosis, 2 and 6 weeks
  • Treatment within 10 days:
    • IVIG 2 g/kg over 10–12 h + aspirin 30–50 mg/kg/day (anti-inflammatory) until afebrile, then 3–5 mg/kg/day (antiplatelet) for 6–8 weeks (longer if aneurysm)
    • Steroids/infliximab if resistant
  • Delay live vaccines 11 months after IVIG · watch for Reye with influenza/varicella on aspirin

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)

  • Multisystem autoimmune disease, adolescent girls · more severe in children (renal and CNS)
  • Malar (butterfly) rash, photosensitivity, oral ulcers, alopecia
  • Arthritis
  • Serositis
  • Nephritis (haematuria, proteinuria, hypertension)
  • Seizures, psychosis
  • Cytopenias
  • Labs: ANA (sensitive), anti-dsDNA and anti-Sm (specific), low C3/C4, Coombs-positive anaemia, ↑ ESR with normal CRP
  • Treatment: hydroxychloroquine (all), steroids, azathioprine, MMF, cyclophosphamide (severe nephritis), sun protection
  • Neonatal lupus (maternal anti-Ro): congenital heart block, rash
