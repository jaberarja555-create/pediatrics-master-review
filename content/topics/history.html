The paediatric history is usually taken from the mother (or carer), and from the child when old enough. It differs from the adult history by the perinatal, feeding, developmental and immunisation history and by attention to growth and the family's social situation. In the exam, greet, introduce yourself, take permission, and keep to a clear order.

Order of the history

1. Personal dataName, age (exact in infants), sex, address (malaria, kala-azar, schistosomiasis areas), informant and reliability, date
2. Chief complaintIn the mother's words, with duration ("fever for 3 days")
3. History of present illness
  • Analyse each complaint (onset, course, character, aggravating and relieving factors, associated symptoms)
  • Relevant negatives
  • Treatment received
  • Impact on feeding, sleep, activity, school
4. Systemic reviewCNS, CVS, respiratory, GIT, urinary, skin, musculoskeletal
5. Past historyPrevious admissions, operations, blood transfusions, chronic illness, drug allergy
6. Perinatal history
  • Antenatal: ANC visits, illness, drugs, fever, rash, BP, diabetes
  • Natal: gestation, place, mode of delivery, cried at once?
  • Postnatal: birth weight, NICU, jaundice, fits, cyanosis, feeding
7. Nutritional historyBreastfeeding (exclusive? how long?), formula (type, preparation), weaning age and foods, current diet (24-h recall)
8. Developmental history
  • Gross motor, fine motor, speech, social milestones
  • School performance
  • Regression
9. Vaccination historyBy the EPI card: up to date for age? reactions?
10. Family historyConsanguinity, siblings (alive, well, deaths and causes), similar illness, inherited disease (sickle cell, G6PD), TB contact, draw a pedigree
11. Social history
  • Parents' age, education, occupation, income
  • House (rooms, crowding, water, latrine, animals, smoking)
  • School
  • Who cares for the child

Summary and presentation

  • Present in this order, then give a summary in 2–3 lines: age, sex, main complaints with durations, key positive findings
  • Then a provisional diagnosis and differential diagnosis with reasons
Exam tip

Always mention the vaccination, growth and development of the child, even when the complaint seems unrelated: examiners mark them in every paediatric case.
