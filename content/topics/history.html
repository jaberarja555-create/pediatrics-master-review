The paediatric history is usually taken from the mother or carer, and from the child as soon as the child is old enough to answer. It differs from the adult history in four parts that are always asked: the birth, nutritional, immunization and developmental history, because each of them can explain the present illness. In the OSCE a focused history is still a complete history: every part is asked, but the questions that decide the diagnosis are asked in detail.

Starting the station

  • Greet the mother, introduce yourself and take permission; these three steps carry marks in every checklist.
  • Sit at her level, keep eye contact and use simple words, not medical terms.
  • Begin with open questions and let her tell the story in her own words; use closed questions later to fill the gaps.
  • Before going on, ask yourself two questions: what is the differential diagnosis of this complaint, and which part of the history decides between them?

Order of the history

1. Personal dataName, age (in days or months for infants), sex, residence (malaria, kala-azar and schistosomiasis areas), tribe, informant and reliability, date of admission
2. Presenting complaintsIn the mother's words, in chronological order, each with its duration ("fever for 3 days"). A known disease is stated first: "a known case of sickle cell anaemia since the age of one year, now presenting with…"
3. History of presenting illness
  • Analyse each complaint: onset, course, character, aggravating and relieving factors, associated symptoms
  • Ask the relevant negatives that exclude the other diagnoses
  • Treatment received and the response
  • Complications and the effect on feeding, sleep, activity and school
4. Systemic reviewCNS, respiratory, cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, urinary, skin, musculoskeletal
5. Birth historyAntenatal, natal and postnatal (next page)
6. Nutritional historyBreastfeeding, formula, weaning and the present diet (next page)
7. ImmunizationFrom the EPI card: up to date for age? reactions? the BCG scar
8. DevelopmentGross motor, fine motor, speech and language, social milestones; school performance; any regression
9. Past historyPrevious similar attacks, admissions, operations, blood transfusions, chronic illness
10. Family historyConsanguinity, number of siblings and their health, deaths and their causes, similar illness, inherited disease (sickle cell, G6PD deficiency), contact with a chronic cough (TB)
11. Drug historyCurrent drugs and doses, drug allergy, traditional remedies (cautery, herbs)
12. Social historyParents' age, education, occupation and income; housing (rooms, crowding, water supply, latrine, animals); smoking at home; who cares for the child
Best next step

Whatever the complaint, always state the child's growth, immunization and development: examiners give marks for them in every paediatric station, even when they seem unrelated to the complaint.

Birth history

Antenatal
  • Antenatal visits, maternal age, planned pregnancy
  • Illness: fever with rash (TORCH), hypertension, diabetes, anaemia, bleeding
  • Drugs, irradiation, maternal malnutrition
Natal
  • Place (hospital or home) and attendant
  • Gestational age: term or preterm; single or multiple
  • Mode: vaginal, assisted or caesarean section, and why
  • Prolonged labour, prolonged rupture of membranes (sepsis risk)
Postnatal
  • Cried immediately or needed resuscitation
  • Birth weight, birth injury, congenital anomaly
  • Admission to the nursery: jaundice, convulsions, cyanosis, breathing difficulty
  • Time of the first breastfeed, meconium passed in 24 hours

Nutritional history

Breastfeeding
  • When started; exclusive for the first 6 months or not
  • Frequency, day and night; one breast emptied before the other
  • When and why stopped
Formula or animal milkType, amount of powder and water (over-dilution causes undernutrition), measured or guessed, bottle hygiene, fresh cow or goat milk
WeaningAge at the start (6 months), first foods, how prepared, response
Present dietA 24-hour recall: type, amount and frequency of meals; feeding during illness; food taboos

Developmental history

  • Ask the age of the key milestones in each field: gross motor (head control, sitting, walking), fine motor (reaching, pincer grasp), speech (first word, sentences) and social (smiling, playing with others).
  • Older child: school class and performance.
  • Ask about regression (loss of skills already gained), which points to a progressive neurological disease and not to cerebral palsy.

Writing the summary

The summary is the last mark in every history checklist. It is two or three sentences that give the age and sex, the residence when it matters, the main complaints with their durations, and the positive findings and important negatives that support the diagnosis.

Model summary

Ahmed is a 3-year-old boy from Gedaref who presented with fever for 3 weeks, abdominal distension for 2 weeks and weight loss. The fever is intermittent, with sweating and no rigors. He has received two courses of antimalarials without improvement. He is fully immunized and his development is normal. A neighbour's child had a similar illness. My provisional diagnosis is visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar); the differential diagnosis is chronic malaria, typhoid fever, brucellosis and leukaemia.

After the summary

  1. Give the provisional diagnosis with the two or three facts that support it.
  2. Give the differential diagnosis, each with one reason for and one against.
  3. State the investigations that confirm the diagnosis, starting with the simplest.
  4. Outline the management, including any life-threatening problem that needs treatment first.
  5. Thank the mother and ask whether she has any questions.

The long case

  • Present the history in the same order you took it, without notes where possible.
  • Present the examination as general examination, growth, then the system involved, then the other systems briefly.
  • End with the problem list: medical, nutritional, developmental and social problems, because the management plan answers each one.
Exam trap
  • Leading questions ("Is the stool black?") lose marks; ask open questions first ("What is the colour of the stool?").
  • A summary that repeats the whole history is wrong: give only the facts that support the diagnosis.
  • The duration of every complaint must be stated; "fever" without "for 3 days" is incomplete.
