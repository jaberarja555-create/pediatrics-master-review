Measles (rubeola) is a highly contagious paramyxovirus infection spread by droplets, still causing outbreaks and deaths in Sudan, mostly in malnourished, unvaccinated children under 5. It suppresses immunity for months, so most deaths come from pneumonia, diarrhoea and malnutrition. Vitamin A and vaccination are the key interventions.

Clinical course

Incubation10–12 days · infectious from 4 days before to 4 days after the rash
Prodrome (3–4 days)High fever + the "3 Cs": cough, coryza, conjunctivitis · Koplik spots (white spots on red buccal mucosa opposite the lower molars) 1–2 days before the rash: pathognomonic
Rash (day 4)Maculopapular, starts behind the ears and on the face, spreads down to the trunk and limbs over 3 days, becomes confluent; fever peaks with the rash
RecoveryRash fades in the same order leaving brown staining and fine desquamation · fever settles by day 3 of the rash

Complications

  • Pneumonia (viral or secondary bacterial): commonest cause of death · croup
  • Diarrhoea, dehydration, malnutrition (kwashiorkor after measles)
  • Otitis media (commonest complication)
  • Mouth ulcers, stomatitis (poor feeding)
  • Eyes: keratitis, xerophthalmia → blindness (vitamin A deficiency)
  • Encephalitis (1/1000, days after rash)
  • SSPE (subacute sclerosing panencephalitis): years later, progressive dementia, myoclonus, death
  • Reactivation of TB (Mantoux becomes negative)

Management

  • Diagnosis is clinical (measles IgM in outbreaks) · isolate for 4 days after the rash
  • Vitamin A to every child: day 1 and day 2 (< 6 mo 50 000 IU · 6–12 mo 100 000 IU · > 1 yr 200 000 IU); third dose at 2–4 weeks if eye signs
  • Fever, fluids, ORS, continue feeding · mouth care (gentian violet), eye care (tetracycline ointment)
  • Antibiotics for pneumonia, otitis, eye infection · severe complicated measles (with pneumonia, SAM, clouding of the cornea, deep mouth ulcers): admit
  • Prevention: measles vaccine at 9 and 18 months · post-exposure vaccine within 72 h (from 6 months)

Varicella: treatment and prevention

  • Calamine, antihistamine for itch · short nails · paracetamol (no aspirin; avoid ibuprofen: skin infection)
  • Aciclovir 20 mg/kg orally 5 times daily × 5–7 days in adolescents and complicated cases · IV 10 mg/kg 8-hourly if immunosuppressed, neonatal or pneumonia
  • VZIG within 10 days of exposure for immunosuppressed children, susceptible pregnant women, and newborns of mothers with rash from 5 days before to 2 days after delivery
  • Live varicella vaccine (2 doses) where available
