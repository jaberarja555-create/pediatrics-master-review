Measles is a highly contagious RNA virus (paramyxovirus) spread by droplets and air, infectious from 4 days before to 4 days after the rash. It kills through pneumonia, diarrhoea and malnutrition, mainly in unvaccinated and malnourished children. Prevented by measles vaccine (MR) at 9 and 18 months (EPI).

Clinical stages

Prodrome (3–4 days)High fever + the 3 Cs: cough, coryza, conjunctivitis · Koplik spots: grey-white dots on red buccal mucosa opposite the lower molars (pathognomonic), gone when the rash appears
Rash (day 4)Red maculopapular rash starting behind the ears and on the face, spreading down to the trunk and limbs over 3 days, becoming blotchy · fever peaks with the rash
RecoveryRash fades in the same order, leaving brown staining and fine peeling · fever falls by day 3 of the rash (persisting fever = complication)

Complications

  • Pneumonia (viral or bacterial; the commonest cause of death) · otitis media (commonest) · laryngotracheobronchitis
  • Diarrhoea, mouth ulcers → malnutrition (can precipitate kwashiorkor)
  • Eye: corneal ulcer, blindness (vitamin A deficiency)
  • Acute encephalitis (1 in 1000) · SSPE: fatal degenerative brain disease 7–10 years later (high measles antibody in CSF)
  • Immune suppression for weeks (TB flares, false-negative Mantoux) · myocarditis

Management (WHO, IMCI)

  • Vitamin A to every child with measles on day 1 and day 2: < 6 m 50,000 IU · 6–11 m 100,000 IU · ≥ 12 m 200,000 IU (third dose after 2–4 weeks if eye signs)
  • Supportive: fluids, feeding, eye and mouth care (tetracycline eye ointment, gentian violet for mouth ulcers) · antibiotics for pneumonia, ear or eye infection · isolate
  • Severe complicated measles (clouding of cornea, deep mouth ulcers, pneumonia, not able to drink): admit

Mumps and infectious mononucleosis

MumpsParamyxovirus · fever, painful parotid swelling (one then both sides) that lifts the ear lobe, pain on chewing · complications: aseptic meningitis (commonest), orchitis (after puberty; rarely sterility), pancreatitis, sensorineural deafness · supportive · prevented by MMR
Infectious mononucleosisEpstein–Barr virus · fever, exudative tonsillitis, large cervical nodes, splenomegaly (50%), hepatitis · rash after amoxicillin (80%) · blood: lymphocytosis with atypical lymphocytes, Monospot (heterophile) or EBV IgM · supportive · avoid contact sports (splenic rupture)
Exam trap
  • A child with "tonsillitis" who gets a widespread rash after amoxicillin most likely has infectious mononucleosis, not penicillin allergy.
  • Chickenpox lesions are seen at all stages at once; smallpox lesions are all at the same stage.
