About 1 in 10 newborns needs help to start breathing and 1 in 100 needs more than bag-and-mask ventilation. The most important action is effective ventilation within the "Golden Minute": most babies who do not breathe at birth recover with ventilation alone. Sudan follows the Helping Babies Breathe (HBB) and newborn life support approach.

Preparation

  • A skilled person at every birth · warm room, warm towels, radiant warmer
  • Equipment checked: bag and mask (sizes 0 and 1), suction (bulb or catheter), oxygen, clock
  • Ask: gestation? amniotic fluid clear? how many babies? risk factors?
  • Risk: preterm, meconium, fetal distress, breech, maternal sedation, multiple pregnancy

The steps (HBB / NLS algorithm)

Effective ventilation

  • Head in the neutral ("sniffing") position, a roll under the shoulders · mask covers mouth and nose, sealed on the chin
  • Room air for term babies (21%); 21–30% for preterm · increase O₂ only if compressions are needed or SpO₂ stays low
  • Rate 40–60 breaths/min ("breathe, two, three") · the sign of success is a rising heart rate and visible chest movement
  • No chest rise: correct in order: reposition the mask, reposition the head, suction, open the mouth, increase the pressure, then an alternative airway (intubation or laryngeal mask)

Chest compressions and drugs

CompressionsWhen HR < 60/min after 30 s of effective ventilation · two-thumb technique on the lower third of the sternum, depth ⅓ of the chest · 3 compressions : 1 breath · reassess every 60 s
AdrenalineHR still < 60: 1:10 000 adrenaline 0.1–0.3 mL/kg IV (10–30 µg/kg) via umbilical vein, every 3–5 min · ET route 0.5–1 mL/kg while IV access is obtained
VolumeSuspected blood loss (pale, weak pulses): normal saline or O-negative blood 10 mL/kg over 5–10 min
GlucoseAfter resuscitation: 10% dextrose 2 mL/kg IV if hypoglycaemic
NaloxoneNot used routinely; ventilate first (only for maternal opioid with persistent apnoea despite good ventilation)

Meconium-stained liquor

  • Vigorous baby (crying, good tone): routine care; no suction
  • Non-vigorous baby: start ventilation within the first minute; do not delay for routine tracheal suction
  • Suction the mouth then nose only if secretions obstruct the airway
  • Watch for meconium aspiration syndrome (3.4)

When to stop

  • No detectable heart rate after 10–20 minutes of full, effective resuscitation: discuss stopping with the senior
  • Do not start at the limits of viability as defined locally (e.g. < 25 weeks or < 500 g) or with lethal malformations (anencephaly)
  • After resuscitation: keep warm, check glucose, watch for HIE (3.7), seizures, breathing; explain to the parents and record the times

Post-resuscitation care

  • Admit to the nursery if prolonged ventilation was needed
  • Monitor temperature, glucose, SpO₂, urine output
  • Avoid hyperthermia (worsens brain injury)
Best next step

Baby not breathing after drying and stimulation → bag-and-mask ventilation with room air, not suction, oxygen by mask, or more stimulation.
