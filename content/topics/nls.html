About 1 in 10 newborns needs help to start breathing and fewer than 1 in 100 needs chest compressions or drugs. Ventilation of the lungs is the single most important step. The steps follow the 2025 neonatal resuscitation guidelines (ILCOR, AHA–AAP, ERC) and Helping Babies Breathe.

Deliveries that need a person trained in resuscitation

  • Preterm or post-term birth · multiple pregnancy
  • Meconium-stained liquor · fetal distress (abnormal heart rate)
  • Caesarean section under general anaesthesia
  • Maternal diabetes, pre-eclampsia, chorioamnionitis
  • Abnormal presentation, instrumental delivery, cord prolapse
  • Known fetal anomaly (diaphragmatic hernia, hydrops)

Prepare: a warm room and radiant warmer, dry towels, a bag and mask (sizes 0 and 1), suction, oxygen with a blender, pulse oximeter, laryngoscope and tubes (2.5–3.5 mm), umbilical catheter, adrenaline 1:10,000.

Helping Babies Breathe · the golden minute
  • Dry the baby thoroughly and keep warm; clear the airway only if secretions block it; rub the back to stimulate.
  • Not breathing or gasping by 60 seconds → start bag-and-mask ventilation at 40–60 breaths per minute.

Newborn resuscitation algorithm

Exam trap
  • Meconium-stained liquor: a vigorous baby gets routine care; a non-vigorous baby gets ventilation within the first minute. Routine tracheal suction is no longer done; suction only if the airway is blocked.
  • Term babies are resuscitated in air (21%), not 100% oxygen; oxygen goes up to 100% only with chest compressions.
  • The compression ratio in the newborn is 3 : 1 (90 compressions + 30 breaths per minute), not 15 : 2.

How are chest compressions given in a newborn?
ANSWERTwo thumbs on the lower third of the sternum, one third of chest depth, 3 compressions to 1 breath.
When should resuscitation in a term baby usually stop?
ANSWERWhen there is no heart rate after about 20 minutes of full resuscitation.

Care after resuscitation

  • Admit to the neonatal unit · keep temperature 36.5–37.5 °C and avoid overheating
  • Check blood glucose, blood gas (cord pH), and watch for seizures
  • Moderate or severe encephalopathy at ≥ 36 weeks → consider therapeutic hypothermia within 6 hours (see 3.7)
  • Look for causes: pneumothorax, diaphragmatic hernia, sepsis, blood loss, maternal sedation
  • Record the times, Apgar scores and actions · talk to the parents
  • Diaphragmatic hernia: intubate at once, do not use bag and mask (it fills the gut in the chest)
Best next step · HR stays low and the chest does not move despite repositioning
  • Secure the airway with an endotracheal tube or laryngeal mask, confirm chest movement, then reassess the heart rate.
  • Chest compressions and adrenaline work only after the lungs are being inflated.

What is the IV dose of adrenaline in neonatal resuscitation?
ANSWER10–30 µg/kg (0.1–0.3 ml/kg of 1:10,000) via the umbilical vein.
Why is bag-and-mask ventilation avoided in diaphragmatic hernia?
ANSWERIt blows air into the stomach and bowel in the chest and compresses the lungs; intubate instead.
