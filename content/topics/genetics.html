About 1 in 150 newborns has a chromosomal abnormality. The commonest are the trisomies (Down, Edwards, Patau) from non-disjunction in meiosis, which rises with maternal age, and the sex chromosome disorders (Turner, Klinefelter). Recognising the dysmorphic pattern and the associated problems is what the exam tests.

Down syndrome (trisomy 21)

Genetics
  • 1 in 700 births; risk ↑ with maternal age (1 in 30 at 45)
  • Non-disjunction 94% (recurrence ≈ 1%)
  • Robertsonian translocation 4% (parent may carry it: higher recurrence, karyotype parents)
  • Mosaic 2%
Face and body
  • Upslanting palpebral fissures, epicanthic folds, Brushfield spots, flat face and occiput (brachycephaly), small ears, protruding tongue
  • Single palmar crease, short broad hands, clinodactyly, sandal gap · hypotonia (floppy newborn)
Associated problems
  • Congenital heart disease (40–50%): AVSD, VSD, TOF → echo in all
  • GI: duodenal atresia, Hirschsprung, coeliac
  • Intellectual disability, delayed development
  • Hypothyroidism, short stature, obesity
  • Hearing loss (glue ear), cataract, squint
  • Leukaemia (ALL, AML; transient myeloproliferation)
  • Atlanto-axial instability, early Alzheimer, sleep apnoea, infections

Single-gene inheritance

PatternFeaturesExamples
Autosomal dominant
  • Every generation
  • 50% risk to each child
  • Both sexes
  • Variable expression, new mutations
Achondroplasia, neurofibromatosis, tuberous sclerosis, Marfan, hereditary spherocytosis, vWD, polycystic kidney (adult)
Autosomal recessive
  • Parents are carriers (unaffected)
  • 25% risk each pregnancy
  • Consanguinity (common in Sudan)
  • Both sexes
Sickle cell, thalassaemia, CAH, cystic fibrosis, galactosaemia, PKU, SMA, Wilson, most metabolic disorders
X-linked recessive
  • Boys affected, carrier mothers
  • No male-to-male transmission
  • Sons of carrier: 50% affected, daughters 50% carriers
Haemophilia A/B, Duchenne, G6PD deficiency, colour blindness, fragile X (X-linked, variable)

Other important syndromes

Fragile X
  • Commonest inherited cause of intellectual disability in boys
  • Long face, large ears, macro-orchidism, autism
  • CGG repeat
Achondroplasia
  • AD (mostly new mutation, older father)
  • Short limbs (rhizomelic), large head, frontal bossing, normal intelligence

  • Thymic aplasia (T-cell defect)
  • Cleft palate
  • Hypocalcaemia (no parathyroids)

Noonan"Male Turner" (normal karyotype) · webbed neck, short, pulmonary stenosis

Prenatal diagnosis and counselling

  • Screening: combined test (nuchal translucency + PAPP-A, β-hCG) at 11–14 weeks · cell-free fetal DNA
  • Diagnostic:
    • Chorionic villus sampling (11–14 weeks), amniocentesis (15+ weeks)
    • Genetic counselling for families and consanguineous couples
