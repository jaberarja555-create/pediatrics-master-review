A preterm baby is born before 37 completed weeks (about 10% of births). Grades: extremely preterm < 28 weeks · very preterm 28 to < 32 weeks · moderate to late preterm 32 to < 37 weeks. Prematurity is the leading cause of neonatal death.

Causes

  • Idiopathic spontaneous preterm labour (the commonest)
  • Maternal: infection (chorioamnionitis, UTI, malaria), pre-eclampsia, diabetes, extremes of age, poverty, smoking
  • Uterine and placental: preterm rupture of membranes, cervical incompetence, placenta praevia, abruption, polyhydramnios
  • Fetal: multiple pregnancy, anomalies, fetal distress

Features of a preterm baby

Skin and hairThin, red, shiny skin · little subcutaneous fat · plenty of lanugo · few sole creases
Ears and breastsSoft ear cartilage with slow recoil · breast nodule < 5 mm or absent
GenitaliaBoys: smooth scrotum, undescended testes · girls: prominent clitoris and labia minora
Tone and reflexesHypotonic, extended posture · weak cry, weak suck and swallow (until 34 weeks)

The New Ballard score estimates gestation (± 2 weeks) from 6 physical and 6 neuromuscular signs (posture, square window, arm recoil, popliteal angle, scarf sign, heel to ear).

Care of the preterm baby

Before birthAntenatal corticosteroids (dexamethasone or betamethasone) at 24–34 weeks when preterm birth is expected (WHO) · magnesium sulfate before 32 weeks (brain protection) · antibiotics for preterm rupture of membranes
WarmthKangaroo mother care (KMC): continuous skin-to-skin on the mother's chest, started immediately after birth for every baby < 2000 g once able to breathe (WHO 2022) · incubator if unstable
FeedingMother's own breast milk (donor milk next) · by NGT before 32–34 weeks, cup or breast later · small trophic feeds first, increase daily · human milk fortifier for < 1500 g
Fluids10% glucose 60–80 ml/kg/day on day 1, increase by 20 ml/kg/day to 150 ml/kg/day · add sodium and potassium from day 2–3
SupplementsVitamin D · iron from 2–6 weeks · vitamin K at birth
DischargeFeeding well by breast or cup · gaining weight · stable temperature · mother confident (usually about 1800 g)

Complications by system

RespiratoryRDS (surfactant deficiency, see 3.4) · apnoea of prematurity (pause ≥ 20 s, or shorter with bradycardia or cyanosis; treat with caffeine citrate 20 mg/kg load, then 5–10 mg/kg once daily) · bronchopulmonary dysplasia (still needs oxygen at 36 weeks' corrected age)
TemperatureHypothermia: large surface area, thin skin, little brown fat · cold stress 36–36.4 °C · moderate 32–35.9 °C · severe < 32 °C
BrainIntraventricular haemorrhage (fragile germinal matrix; in the first 72 h; cranial ultrasound) · periventricular leukomalacia → spastic diplegia
HeartPatent ductus arteriosus: bounding pulses, continuous murmur, heart failure · echo · ibuprofen or paracetamol, surgery if they fail
GutFeeding intolerance, reflux · necrotising enterocolitis (below)
EyesRetinopathy of prematurity: abnormal retinal vessels (immaturity + too much oxygen) → detachment, blindness · screen every baby < 32 weeks or < 1500 g · laser or anti-VEGF injection
Metabolic, bloodHypoglycaemia, jaundice (immature liver), anaemia of prematurity, osteopenia (low calcium and phosphate), vitamin K deficiency
InfectionLow maternal IgG (crosses after 32 weeks), immature immunity, lines and tubes · early (GBS) and late (staphylococci, Gram-negatives) sepsis
LaterCerebral palsy, learning difficulty, hearing and visual loss, poor growth, rickets

Necrotising enterocolitis (NEC)

  • Bowel wall necrosis in preterm babies, usually 1–2 weeks after starting feeds · risk: prematurity, formula feeds, fast feed increase, asphyxia, polycythaemia, PDA
  • Features: feed intolerance, bile-stained vomiting, abdominal distension, blood in the stool, then shock, apnoea, abdominal wall redness
  • Abdominal X-ray: pneumatosis intestinalis (gas in the bowel wall) · gas in the portal vein · free air (perforation)
  • Treatment: nil by mouth, NGT on free drainage, IV fluids, IV antibiotics (ampicillin + gentamicin + metronidazole) for 7–14 days · surgery for perforation
  • Prevention: breast milk, antenatal steroids, careful feed increase

Problems: birth asphyxia, meconium aspiration · hypoglycaemia (low glycogen) · hypothermia · polycythaemia (chronic fetal hypoxia) · hypocalcaemia · infection. Care: warmth, early and frequent feeds, glucose checks.

Large for gestational age

  • Birth weight > 90th centile; macrosomia > 4 kg (or 4.5 kg)
  • Causes: maternal diabetes, large parents, Beckwith–Wiedemann syndrome, hydrops
  • Problems: shoulder dystocia, birth injury (Erb palsy, fractured clavicle), asphyxia, hypoglycaemia, polycythaemia

Post-term baby (42 weeks or more)

  • Alert, open eyes · dry, peeling, wrinkled skin · long nails · meconium staining · little fat
  • Problems: meconium aspiration, birth asphyxia, hypoglycaemia, polycythaemia, persistent pulmonary hypertension
Exam trap
  • SGA and preterm are different: a term baby of 2.2 kg is SGA but not preterm.
  • Both SGA and large babies of diabetic mothers get hypoglycaemia, for different reasons (low stores against high insulin).
  • Asymmetrical IUGR (head sparing) has the better prognosis.
