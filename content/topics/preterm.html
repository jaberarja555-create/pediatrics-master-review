A preterm baby is born before 37 completed weeks. Prematurity is the leading cause of neonatal death in Sudan. Most deaths are from hypothermia, infection, respiratory distress and feeding problems, and can be prevented with simple care: warmth (kangaroo mother care), breast milk and infection control.

Classification

By gestation
  • Late preterm 34–36⁺⁶ weeks
  • Moderate 32–33⁺⁶ weeks
  • Very preterm 28–31⁺⁶ weeks
  • Extremely preterm < 28 weeks
LBW has two causes
  • Preterm (appropriate for dates)
  • SGA / IUGR (term but small): placental insufficiency, pre-eclampsia, smoking, infection, chromosomal · symmetric (early insult, head small too) or asymmetric (late placental, head spared)

Causes of preterm birth

  • Maternal: infection (UTI, chorioamnionitis, malaria), pre-eclampsia, antepartum haemorrhage, cervical incompetence, young or old age, poor nutrition, short birth interval
  • Fetal: multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios, congenital anomalies · often unknown
  • Prevention of complications: antenatal corticosteroids to the mother 24–34 weeks (dexamethasone 6 mg IM 12-hourly × 4 or betamethasone 12 mg × 2): ↓ RDS, IVH, NEC and death

Problems of the preterm baby

TemperatureHypothermia: large surface area, little brown fat, thin skin

Thermal care

  • Keep axillary temperature 36.5–37.5 °C
  • Hypothermia: mild 36–36.4 · moderate 32–35.9 · severe < 32 °C
  • Dry at birth, cap, skin-to-skin, delay bath, warm room (25–28 °C)
  • Incubator or radiant warmer for the sick or very small baby
Kangaroo mother care (KMC)

Baby naked except a nappy and cap, upright skin-to-skin on the mother's chest, day and night, with exclusive breastfeeding. For stable babies < 2000 g. It reduces hypothermia, infection and death, and helps breastfeeding and bonding.

Feeding the preterm baby

Gestation / weightMethod

32–34 weeksCup or spoon feeding of expressed breast milk, then breast
< 32 weeks / too sickNasogastric tube feeds of expressed breast milk · IV fluids if very sick or < 1.2 kg
Fluid volume (mL/kg/day)
  • Day 1: 60–80 · rise by about 20 mL/kg/day · day 7: 150 (up to 180 in very small babies)
  • IV fluid day 1: 10% dextrose; add Na and K from day 2–3
Supplements
  • Breast milk is best (expressed mother's milk; fortifier if available)
  • Vitamin D 400 IU/day · iron 2–3 mg/kg/day from 2–6 weeks to 1 year · multivitamins

Apnoea of prematurity

  • Pause in breathing ≥ 20 seconds, or shorter with bradycardia (< 100) or desaturation · common < 34 weeks (immature respiratory centre)
  • Exclude secondary causes: sepsis, hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, anaemia, seizures, IVH
  • Treatment: gentle stimulation, caffeine citrate 20 mg/kg loading, then 5–10 mg/kg once daily (or aminophylline 6 mg/kg load, then 2.5 mg/kg 12-hourly), CPAP if frequent

Necrotising enterocolitis (NEC)

  • Ischaemic, infected inflammation of the bowel wall in preterm babies after starting feeds (week 1–2) · risk: formula feeding, asphyxia, PDA
  • Features: feed intolerance, abdominal distension, bilious aspirates, bloody stool, apnoea, shock
  • X-ray: pneumatosis intestinalis (gas in the bowel wall), portal gas, free air (perforation)
  • Treatment: stop feeds (NPO 7–10 days), NG tube on free drainage, IV fluids
  • Antibiotics: ampicillin + gentamicin + metronidazole for 7–10 days
  • Surgery for perforation or worsening
  • Prevention: breast milk, antenatal steroids, slow increase of feeds

Discharge and follow-up

  • Discharge: stable temperature in a cot or KMC, breastfeeding well, gaining weight (15–20 g/kg/day), mother confident
  • Usually weight ≥ 1.8–2 kg
  • Growth on corrected age · development (CP risk) · eyes (ROP) · hearing · anaemia · immunise by chronological age
