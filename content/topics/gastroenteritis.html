Acute diarrhoea is the passage of 3 or more loose or watery stools in 24 hours, lasting less than 14 days. It is a leading cause of death in Sudanese children under 5, almost always from dehydration. Most episodes are viral; the key treatments are ORS, zinc and continued feeding, not antibiotics.

Non-gut causes of diarrhoea and vomiting: malaria, UTI, otitis media, pneumonia, meningitis, appendicitis, intussusception (bloody "redcurrant jelly" stool), DKA.

Other assessment

  • Shock: cold hands, CRT > 3 s, weak fast pulse, low BP
  • Signs of hypernatraemia: doughy skin, irritability, high-pitched cry, thirst out of proportion
  • Blood in stool, duration ≥ 14 days, malnutrition (SAM changes the plan: 2.2)
  • Investigations are not needed in most cases · in severe: Na, K, urea, creatinine, glucose, blood gas
  • Stool microscopy and culture in dysentery or outbreaks · malaria test with fever

Plan A: no dehydration (treat at home)

  1. Give extra fluid: ORS after each loose stool: < 2 years 50–100 mL · 2–10 years 100–200 mL · older: as much as wanted · plus breast milk, soup, rice water
  2. Zinc for 10–14 days: 10 mg/day (< 6 months) · 20 mg/day (≥ 6 months)
  3. Continue feeding: breastfeed more often; normal food
  4. Return at once if: not able to drink, becomes sicker, blood in stool, drinking poorly, fever

Plan B: some dehydration (ORS in the clinic over 4 hours)

  • ORS 75 mL/kg over 4 hours (by cup and spoon, small frequent sips; NG tube if not drinking)
  • Vomiting: wait 10 minutes, then continue more slowly
  • Continue breastfeeding · give zinc
  • Reassess after 4 hours and choose the plan again: no dehydration → Plan A · still some → repeat Plan B and start food · severe → Plan C

  • Fluid: Ringer's lactate (Hartmann's) or normal saline · total 100 mL/kg
  • Repeat the first 30 mL/kg once if the radial pulse is still weak
  • Reassess every 1–2 hours; give ORS (5 mL/kg/h) as soon as the child can drink

  • No IV access: ORS by NG tube 20 mL/kg/h for 6 hours (120 mL/kg) and refer
  • Shock (cold hands, CRT > 3 s, weak fast pulse) in a child without SAM: ETAT bolus 20 mL/kg crystalloid quickly (13.1)
  • SAM: different, slower regimen (ReSoMal; 2.2)

  • How it works: sodium–glucose co-transport (SGLT1) in the small bowel stays intact in diarrhoea: glucose carries sodium, and water follows
  • Low osmolarity reduces stool volume and vomiting by about a third
  • Mix one packet in 1 litre of clean water; use within 24 h
  • Home fluids: rice water, soup, yoghurt drinks · avoid soft drinks and sweet juices (osmotic diarrhoea)

Prevention of diarrhoea

  • Exclusive breastfeeding to 6 months
  • Hand washing with soap, safe water, latrines
  • Safe food preparation and storage
  • Rotavirus vaccine and measles vaccine
  • Vitamin A supplementation, zinc in diarrhoea
  • Cholera vaccine in outbreaks
