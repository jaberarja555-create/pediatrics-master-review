In a counselling station the examiner plays the mother and marks communication as much as content. Every checklist follows the same order: find out what she already knows, correct and add information in simple words, check that she has understood, answer her questions, and arrange follow-up. Praise what she does well before correcting what is wrong.

Steps of every counselling station

  1. Greet, introduce yourself, confirm who she is and the child's name; sit at her level.
  2. Ask what she knows about the condition and what worries her most.
  3. Explain the condition in simple words, in small pieces, without medical terms; use a drawing if it helps.
  4. Give the practical advice (what to do, what to avoid, when to return).
  5. Check understanding: ask her to repeat the key points in her own words.
  6. Invite her questions, give written information if available, and arrange follow-up.

Immunization counselling

What she knowsAsk which vaccines she has heard of and look at the EPI card
Why vaccinateVaccines protect against serious diseases (measles, polio, tetanus, pertussis, TB, pneumonia, diarrhoea) that can kill or disable; vaccines are safe

Febrile convulsion

What she knowsHow she finds out the child has fever, what she does (medicine, sponging), how she recognises a convulsion and what she did; praise what was right
Detecting feverFeel the forehead or the covered parts of the body with the back of the hand; best with a thermometer (axillary ≥ 37.5 °C)
Reducing feverParacetamol 15 mg/kg every 6 hours (syrup or suppository); remove extra clothes; sponge with lukewarm (tepid) water, not cold water or alcohol; plenty of fluids
During a fitStay calm; lay the child on the side on a safe surface; nothing in the mouth; loosen clothes; note the time; if it lasts more than 5 minutes, give rectal diazepam 0.5 mg/kg if prescribed and go to hospital
PrognosisAbout 1 in 3 children have another febrile convulsion; it does not damage the brain or intelligence; epilepsy is rare (slightly more than other children)
Return at onceFit longer than 5 minutes, repeated fits, drowsiness after the fit, neck stiffness, rash
Exam trap

Antipyretics make the child comfortable but do not prevent febrile convulsions; regular antiepileptic drugs are not given for simple febrile convulsions.

Epilepsy

NatureA tendency to repeated fits from abnormal electrical activity in the brain; common, controllable, not contagious, not caused by spirits
MedicineGive it every day at the same time; never stop suddenly (fits return, status epilepticus); usually continued until 2 years without a fit; bring the medicine to every visit
PrecautionsNo swimming or bathing alone, no climbing heights, shower rather than bath, avoid sleep loss; school and play are encouraged
Seizure diaryRecord each fit (date, time, type, duration) for the follow-up visit
Formula
First aid in a fit
On the side, safe place+nothing in the mouth+time it: > 5 min=Rectal diazepam and hospital

Mother puts a spoon in the mouth during a fitWrong: nothing in the mouth
Mother sponges with cold water and alcoholWrong: lukewarm water only
Epileptic child stopped the drug because fits stoppedRestart: never stop suddenly
Chance of another febrile convulsionAbout 1 in 3

When is rectal diazepam given at home?

Type 1 diabetes

The diabetes station is usually a mother of a newly diagnosed child with type 1 diabetes. She needs to hear that the disease is common and controllable, that insulin is life-long and cannot be replaced by tablets or diet, and how to recognise and treat hypoglycaemia.

Points to cover

NatureThe pancreas has stopped making insulin, the hormone that moves sugar from the blood into the body; it is not her fault and not caused by sweets
InsulinLife-long; never miss a dose, even when the child eats less (sick-day rules)
StorageSpare vials in the fridge (not the freezer); the vial in use at room temperature for up to 4 weeks; where there is no fridge, a clay pot (zeer) keeps it cool
InjectionClean hands, correct dose drawn, pinch the skin, inject subcutaneously at 90°; rotate the sites (abdomen, thighs, upper arms, buttocks) to avoid lumps (lipohypertrophy)
MonitoringBlood glucose before meals and at bedtime; a diary of readings and doses
Diet and activityRegular meals and snacks with complex carbohydrates; avoid sugary drinks except to treat a low; sport and school are encouraged, with a snack before exercise; tell the teacher
Sick daysKeep giving insulin, give fluids, check glucose and urine ketones more often; come at once with vomiting, abdominal pain or fast breathing (DKA)

Hypoglycaemia

  • Signs: sweating, shaking, hunger, pallor, headache, irritability or odd behaviour, drowsiness, fits
  • Causes: missed meal, extra exercise, too much insulin
  • Awake: give sugar at once (juice, sugar in water, sweets), then a meal or snack
  • Drowsy or fitting: nothing by mouth; glucagon IM or sugar gel on the gums; go to hospital
Formula
Hypoglycaemia at home
Sweaty, shaky, irritable child on insulin+missed meal or extra play=Sugar by mouth now, then a snack
Clue switch · the detail that flips the answer
Mother stops insulin because the child is vomitingWrong: continue insulin, come to hospital (DKA risk)
Lumps at the injection sitesLipohypertrophy: rotate the sites
Unconscious child on insulin at homeGlucagon IM, nothing by mouth
Try it · exam-style
How is insulin stored at home?
ANSWERSpare vials in the fridge (not the freezer); the vial in use at room temperature for up to 4 weeks.
Why are injection sites rotated?
ANSWERTo prevent fatty lumps (lipohypertrophy) that make insulin absorption unpredictable.

Down syndrome (breaking the news)

  • Choose a quiet room, both parents if possible, the baby present; ask what they have noticed.
  • Explain that the baby has an extra chromosome 21; it happened at conception and is not the parents' fault; a blood test (karyotype) confirms it and tells the risk for the next pregnancy.
  • Children with Down syndrome are usually happy and affectionate; they learn more slowly (developmental delay) and most attend school with support.
  • Some have problems that are checked and treated: heart defect (echo now), thyroid, hearing, vision, infections.
  • Offer support (early stimulation, physiotherapy, parent groups); questions; follow-up.

Inhaler with a spacer

  1. Remove the cap and shake the inhaler; fit it into the spacer.
  2. Child under 4 years: put the mask over the nose and mouth; older child: lips around the mouthpiece.
  3. Press one puff, then let the child take 5–6 normal breaths (or one deep breath held for 10 seconds).
  4. Wait 30–60 seconds, shake again before the next puff; rinse the mouth after a steroid inhaler.
  5. Wash the spacer monthly in soapy water and let it dry in air.
Spacer with mask

Preparing and giving ORS

  1. Wash hands; pour the whole packet into 1 litre of clean (boiled and cooled) water; stir well.
  2. Give by cup and spoon in small frequent sips; if the child vomits, wait 10 minutes and continue more slowly.
  3. After each loose stool: 50–100 ml under 2 years, 100–200 ml at 2 years or more.
  4. Use within 24 hours, then discard; continue breastfeeding and food; give zinc.

Asthma counselling

NatureThe airways are sensitive and inflamed; with a trigger they narrow, causing cough, wheeze and breathlessness; it is common and controllable, not contagious
Two kinds of inhalerReliever (salbutamol) opens the airways within minutes during symptoms · controller (inhaled steroid) is taken every day, even when well, to prevent attacks
Steroid inhaler worriesThe dose is small and safe for growth; rinse the mouth after use to prevent thrush
TriggersAvoid smoke (cigarettes, wood and charcoal fires), dust, strong perfumes; colds and exercise are not avoided but planned for (reliever before sport)
Written action planDuring an attack give salbutamol 2–10 puffs by spacer every 20 minutes; come to hospital at once if the reliever does not help, the child cannot talk or feed, the lips turn blue, or the reliever is needed more often than every 4 hours
Follow-upBring the inhalers and spacer to every visit to check the technique; control is reviewed and treatment stepped down when well controlled
