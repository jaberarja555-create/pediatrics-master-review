Vomiting in infancy ranges from harmless posseting to surgical emergencies. Three questions guide the approach: Is the vomit bile-stained? (green = obstruction below the ampulla until proved otherwise) · Is the infant unwell or dehydrated? · Is the infant gaining weight?

Gastro-oesophageal reflux (GOR) and GORD

  • GOR: effortless regurgitation after feeds, happy, thriving infant · lax lower oesophageal sphincter, liquid diet, lying flat · peaks at 4 months, gone by 12–18 months
  • Treatment: reassurance, upright after feeds, avoid overfeeding, thickened feeds
  • GORD (disease): FTT, oesophagitis (irritability, arching, haematemesis, iron-deficiency anaemia), aspiration pneumonia, apnoea, wheeze · Sandifer syndrome (neck twisting)
  • Higher risk: CP, preterm, repaired oesophageal atresia, diaphragmatic hernia
  • Treatment: omeprazole 1 mg/kg/day · surgery (fundoplication) if severe

Infantile hypertrophic pyloric stenosis

WhoFirst-born boys (M : F 4 : 1), age 2–8 weeks (peak 3–6), family history, erythromycin in early infancy
FeaturesProjectile non-bilious vomiting after feeds, baby hungry after vomiting · constipation, weight loss, dehydration · visible gastric peristalsis (left to right) · olive-shaped mass in the right upper quadrant during a test feed
BiochemistryHypochloraemic hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis (loss of HCl) · paradoxical aciduria late
DiagnosisUltrasound: pyloric muscle thickness > 3–4 mm, length > 15–17 mm · barium: "string sign"
TreatmentCorrect fluids and electrolytes first (0.45% saline + 5% dextrose + KCl until Cl ≥ 100 and bicarbonate < 28) — not an emergency operation · then Ramstedt pyloromyotomy

Intussusception

  • Telescoping of a proximal bowel segment into the distal one, usually ileocolic · commonest cause of intestinal obstruction from 3 months to 3 years (peak 6–9 months)
  • Lead point: hypertrophied Peyer's patches after a viral infection · in older children: Meckel's diverticulum, polyp, lymphoma, HSP
  • Paroxysmal colicky pain: the infant screams, draws up the legs, goes pale, then is quiet between attacks
  • Vomiting (bilious later) · "redcurrant jelly" stool (blood and mucus, late)
  • Sausage-shaped mass (RUQ), empty right iliac fossa (Dance's sign) · lethargy, shock
DiagnosisUltrasound: target (doughnut) sign · X-ray: obstruction, soft-tissue mass
TreatmentResuscitate, NG tube, IV fluids, antibiotics · air (or contrast) enema reduction under imaging (success 80–90%) · surgery if peritonitis, perforation, shock or failed reduction
RecurrenceAbout 5–10% after enema reduction
