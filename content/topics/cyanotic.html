Cyanotic CHD sends deoxygenated blood into the systemic circulation (right-to-left shunt or mixing). Central cyanosis appears when deoxygenated Hb exceeds about 5 g/dL. The commonest cyanotic lesion overall is tetralogy of Fallot; the commonest presenting in the first day is transposition of the great arteries.

Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF)

Four components
  • Pulmonary (RV outflow) stenosis (infundibular): decides the severity
  • VSD (large)
  • Overriding aorta
  • Right ventricular hypertrophy

Associations: DiGeorge (22q11), Down syndrome.

Clinical features
  • Cyanosis from a few weeks–months (as the stenosis worsens) · hypercyanotic spells
  • Squatting after exercise (older child) · clubbing · polycythaemia
  • Ejection systolic murmur (pulmonary stenosis, not the VSD) at the upper left sternal edge · single S2 · murmur shortens during a spell
  • CXR: boot-shaped heart (coeur en sabot: uplifted apex, concave pulmonary bay), oligaemic lungs, right aortic arch (25%)
  • ECG: RVH, right axis · echo confirms
  • Treatment: complete repair at 4–12 months (VSD closure + relief of RVOT obstruction) · Blalock–Taussig shunt (subclavian → pulmonary artery) if too small or severe early
  • Propranolol to reduce spells while waiting

Hypercyanotic (tet) spell

What happensSpasm of the RV outflow → more R → L shunt · triggered by crying, feeding, defecation, waking, dehydration · peak 2–6 months
FeaturesIrritability, deepening cyanosis, hyperpnoea, murmur becomes softer or disappears, limp, seizures, death
Treatment (in order)1. Calm, mother holds the baby in the knee–chest position (↑ systemic resistance) · 2. Oxygen · 3. morphine 0.1–0.2 mg/kg IM/IV/SC · 4. normal saline 10–20 mL/kg · 5. propranolol 0.1 mg/kg IV (relaxes the infundibulum) · 6. sodium bicarbonate for acidosis · 7. phenylephrine, ventilation · urgent surgery
Exam trap

In TOF the murmur is from the pulmonary stenosis, not the VSD (pressures in the ventricles are equal). A softer murmur during a spell means less blood crosses the outflow: the child is worse, not better. Digoxin is contraindicated (increases infundibular contraction).

Transposition of the great arteries (TGA)

  • Aorta arises from the right ventricle, pulmonary artery from the left: two parallel circulations · survival depends on mixing (PFO, PDA, VSD)
  • Commoner in boys and infants of diabetic mothers
  • Severe cyanosis on day 1–2 (as the duct closes), often without a murmur, little distress
  • CXR: "egg on a string" (narrow superior mediastinum), plethoric lungs
  • Treatment: PGE₁ to keep the duct open · balloon atrial septostomy (Rashkind) to improve mixing · arterial switch operation in the first 2–3 weeks

Complications of chronic cyanosis

  • Polycythaemia → hyperviscosity, stroke (worse with dehydration and iron deficiency)
  • Brain abscess (> 2 years; R → L bypasses lung filtering)
  • Clubbing, FTT, delayed development
  • Infective endocarditis (7.6)
  • Paradoxical emboli · bleeding tendency · gout, renal disease (adults)
  • Keep hydrated; treat iron deficiency (microcytic RBCs are stiff)
