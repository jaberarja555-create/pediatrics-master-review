Central cyanosis is a blue tongue and lips, seen when there is more than 5 g/dl of deoxygenated haemoglobin in the capillaries. In heart disease it comes from right-to-left shunting or mixing. Cyanotic heart disease is harder to see in anaemia and easier in polycythaemia; pulse oximetry confirms it.

Causes of cyanosis in a newborn

  • Heart: TGA, tetralogy, pulmonary or tricuspid atresia, TAPVR, truncus, hypoplastic left heart · no or little respiratory distress · hyperoxia test: PaO2 stays low
  • Lung: RDS, pneumonia, meconium aspiration, persistent pulmonary hypertension · respiratory distress, improves with oxygen
  • Other: methaemoglobinaemia (chocolate blood, normal PaO2), polycythaemia, hypoglycaemia, sepsis, shock
  • Peripheral cyanosis (blue hands and feet, pink tongue): cold, early newborn period (acrocyanosis), poor perfusion

Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF)

Four parts1. Pulmonary (right ventricular outflow) stenosis, mainly infundibular · 2. large VSD · 3. overriding aorta · 4. right ventricular hypertrophy · DiGeorge (22q11) in some · right aortic arch in 25%
Key ideaThe degree of pulmonary stenosis decides everything: severe → cyanosis at birth (duct-dependent) · moderate → cyanosis develops over months as the infundibulum thickens · mild → pink ("acyanotic") Fallot
FeaturesThe commonest cyanotic CHD after infancy · cyanosis appearing in the first year · hypercyanotic spells · squatting after exercise (older child) · clubbing · exercise intolerance · heart failure is rare
SignsEjection systolic murmur at the upper left sternal edge (from the pulmonary stenosis, not the VSD) · single S2 · RV heave · normal heart size
InvestigationsEchocardiography (diagnostic) · X-ray: boot-shaped heart (uplifted apex, concave pulmonary bay), oligaemic lungs · ECG: right axis, RVH · CBC: polycythaemia, relative iron deficiency

Hypercyanotic (tet) spells

  • Peak age 2–4 months (first 2 years) · often in the morning after crying, feeding or defecation, or with fever or dehydration
  • Infundibular spasm + fall in systemic resistance → more right-to-left shunt → deeper cyanosis, rapid deep breathing, irritability, then limpness
  • The murmur becomes soft or disappears (less blood crosses the pulmonary valve)
  • Prolonged spell → hypoxia, acidosis, convulsions, stroke, death
Treatment of a spell (in order)
  1. Calm the child; knee–chest position (on the mother's shoulder) · oxygen
  2. Morphine 0.1 mg/kg SC, IM or IV · 0.9% saline 10 ml/kg bolus
  3. Sodium bicarbonate 1 mmol/kg IV for acidosis
  4. Propranolol 0.1 mg/kg IV slowly (relaxes the infundibulum) or phenylephrine (raises systemic resistance)
  5. Not settling: sedation, intubation and ventilation · urgent shunt surgery

Try it · exam-style
Why does squatting help a child with tetralogy?
ANSWERKinking the leg arteries raises systemic resistance, so less blood shunts right-to-left and more goes to the lungs.

Long-term management of tetralogy

  • Oral propranolol to prevent spells · treat iron deficiency (iron-deficient red cells are stiff → stroke) · avoid dehydration · dental care and endocarditis prevention
  • Total repair (close the VSD, relieve the outflow obstruction) at 4–12 months · modified Blalock–Taussig shunt (subclavian to pulmonary artery) if the infant is small or very cyanosed · newborn with severe obstruction: PGE1 to keep the duct open
  • Complications (unrepaired): spells, polycythaemia, stroke, brain abscess (headache, fever, focal signs in a child over 2 years), infective endocarditis, poor growth

Transposition of the great arteries (TGA)

DefectAorta from the right ventricle, pulmonary artery from the left → two separate circuits; life depends on mixing through the foramen ovale, duct or a VSD · boys, infant of a diabetic mother
FeaturesThe commonest cyanotic CHD at birth · severe cyanosis in the first hours or days, worse as the duct closes · little distress at first · no murmur (intact septum), single loud S2 · acidosis, hypoglycaemia
InvestigationsEcho · X-ray: "egg on its side" with a narrow mediastinum, plethoric lungs · hyperoxia test fails
TreatmentPGE1 infusion at once · balloon atrial septostomy (Rashkind) to improve mixing · arterial switch operation in the first 2–3 weeks

Other cyanotic lesions

Pulmonary atresiaNo outflow to the lungs · cyanosis at birth, duct-dependent · single S2 · PGE1, shunt
Tricuspid atresiaSmall right ventricle · cyanosis at birth · left axis deviation and LVH on ECG (unusual for cyanotic CHD)
Ebstein anomalyTricuspid valve displaced into the right ventricle → huge right atrium, tricuspid regurgitation, arrhythmias (WPW) · maternal lithium · "box-shaped" giant heart
TAPVRPulmonary veins drain to the right side · cyanosis + heart failure · obstructed type: severe distress, white lungs · snowman heart (supracardiac type)
Truncus arteriosusOne artery for both circulations over a VSD · mild cyanosis, early heart failure · DiGeorge syndrome
Hypoplastic left heartTiny left ventricle and aorta · shock when the duct closes in the first week · PGE1, staged surgery
Exam trap
  • TGA with an intact septum has no murmur; a severely cyanosed newborn with a normal-sounding heart may still have serious heart disease.
  • In a cyanosed newborn with suspected duct-dependent disease, giving high oxygen can help the duct close; start PGE1 and aim for SpO2 75–85%.
  • Tetralogy causes oligaemic lungs and rarely heart failure; TGA causes plethoric lungs.
