Tuberculosis is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, spread by droplets from an adult with smear-positive pulmonary TB. Sudan has a high TB burden. Children usually get primary TB from a household contact; young children (< 5 years) and those with HIV or malnutrition progress quickly to severe forms (miliary TB, TB meningitis). Diagnosis is difficult because children rarely produce sputum and have few bacilli.

Forms of TB in children

Primary complex (Ghon)Lung focus + enlarged hilar lymph nodes · often silent · nodes may compress bronchi (collapse, wheeze)
Pulmonary TBChronic cough > 2 weeks, fever, weight loss · adolescent: adult-type cavitation
TB lymphadenitisCommonest extrapulmonary form · painless cervical nodes, matted, cold abscess, sinus
Miliary TBBlood spread · very ill, fever, HSM · CXR: millet-seed nodules · choroid tubercles
TB meningitisSubacute (weeks): personality change, headache, vomiting → cranial nerve palsies (VI), coma · hydrocephalus, basal enhancement
OthersSpine (Pott's disease): gibbus, paraplegia · abdominal (ascites, "doughy" abdomen) · pleural and pericardial effusion · bones and joints

Diagnosis

Suspect TB with
  • Contact with an adult with TB (most important clue)
  • Cough > 2 weeks, fever > 2 weeks, weight loss / FTT not responding to nutrition
  • Painless neck nodes, gibbus, unexplained effusion
  • Pneumonia not responding to antibiotics
Tests
  • Mantoux (TST): 5 TU PPD intradermal, read at 48–72 h: ≥ 10 mm positive (≥ 5 mm if HIV or SAM) · false negative: SAM, HIV, measles, miliary TB, steroids
  • CXR: hilar nodes, consolidation, miliary, cavity, effusion
  • GeneXpert MTB/RIF on gastric aspirate (morning, fasting), induced sputum or stool: detects TB and rifampicin resistance
  • Node aspirate/biopsy (caseating granuloma), CSF, pleural fluid

The diagnosis is often clinical: contact + symptoms + CXR ± positive Mantoux, without bacteriological proof. A positive Mantoux shows infection, not disease; BCG causes only a small reaction (< 10 mm).

  • Steroids: prednisolone 2 mg/kg/day × 4 weeks, then taper: TB meningitis, pericarditis, airway compression by nodes
  • Pyridoxine 5–10 mg/day with isoniazid in malnourished or HIV children
  • Test every child with TB for HIV · nutritional support
  • Response: fever falls in 2–4 weeks, weight gain, appetite returns · failure: poor adherence, resistance (GeneXpert), HIV

Prevention

  • BCG at birth: protects against TB meningitis and miliary TB
  • Contact tracing: every child contact of a smear-positive adult is examined
  • TB preventive therapy for contacts < 5 years and HIV-positive children without active disease: isoniazid 10 mg/kg daily × 6 months (or 3 months rifampicin + isoniazid)
  • Baby of a mother with TB: breastfeed; if mother smear-positive, give isoniazid for 6 months, then BCG
