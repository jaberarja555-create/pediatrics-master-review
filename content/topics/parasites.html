Intestinal parasites are very common in Sudanese children, spread by faecal contamination of soil, water and food. Heavy infection causes anaemia, malnutrition, poor growth and poor school performance. Routine deworming every 6 months from 1 year is part of child health care.

Protozoa

Giardia lamblia
  • Cysts in water · upper small bowel
  • Chronic diarrhoea, steatorrhoea, bloating, flatulence, malabsorption and FTT (lactose intolerance after)
  • Stool for cysts / trophozoites (3 samples), antigen test
  • Metronidazole 5–7.5 mg/kg 8-hourly × 5–7 days or tinidazole 50 mg/kg once
Entamoeba histolytica
  • Amoebic dysentery: bloody mucoid stool, tenesmus, less fever than shigella · flask-shaped ulcers in the colon
  • Amoebic liver abscess: fever, tender hepatomegaly, right shoulder pain, "anchovy sauce" pus
  • Stool: trophozoites with ingested RBCs
  • Metronidazole 10 mg/kg 8-hourly × 7–10 days, then diloxanide for cysts

Schistosomiasis (bilharzia) is caught by contact with fresh water containing cercariae released by snails; they penetrate the skin. Sudan has both forms, with heavy transmission in the Gezira irrigation scheme and along the Nile. School-age children who swim and play in canals are most affected.

The two forms

FeatureS. haematobium (urinary)S. mansoni (intestinal)
Adult wormsVesical venous plexusMesenteric veins (portal)
Main symptomTerminal haematuria, dysuria, frequencyAbdominal pain, bloody diarrhoea
ChronicBladder fibrosis and calcification, obstructive uropathy, hydronephrosis, squamous cell carcinoma of the bladderPeriportal (Symmers') fibrosis → portal hypertension: huge spleen, oesophageal varices, haematemesis with normal liver function
DiagnosisEggs with a terminal spine in midday urine (filtered)Eggs with a lateral spine in stool (Kato–Katz) · rectal snip

Other features

  • Swimmer's itch (cercarial dermatitis) hours after exposure
  • Katayama fever (acute): fever, urticaria, cough, eosinophilia, 3–8 weeks after first exposure
  • Anaemia, growth retardation · eosinophilia
  • Ultrasound: bladder wall thickening, periportal fibrosis

Treatment and prevention

  • Praziquantel 40 mg/kg orally once (both species) · repeat after 4–6 weeks in heavy infection
  • Variceal bleeding: resuscitation, endoscopic band ligation, propranolol (5.5)
  • Mass school-based treatment with praziquantel · avoid canal water, safe water supply, latrines, snail control
