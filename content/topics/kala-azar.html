Visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar) is caused by Leishmania donovani, spread by the sandfly (Phlebotomus). Eastern Sudan (Gedaref, Sennar, Blue Nile) is one of the main foci in the world, and children are most often affected. Untreated, it is almost always fatal. Treatment follows the WHO guidelines for East Africa (2026 update).

Clinical features

  • Prolonged fever (> 2 weeks), often with two peaks a day · weight loss, wasting
  • Massive splenomegaly (soft, non-tender) · hepatomegaly · lymphadenopathy
  • Pallor, bleeding (epistaxis, gums) · darkening of the skin ("black fever")
  • Labs: pancytopenia (anaemia, leucopenia, low platelets) · high ESR · hypergammaglobulinaemia with low albumin
  • Deaths from bleeding, secondary infection (pneumonia, TB), severe anaemia, malnutrition
  • HIV co-infection: atypical, more relapses

Diagnosis

Clinical caseFever > 2 weeks + splenomegaly (or lymphadenopathy) + weight loss in a person from an endemic area
SerologyrK39 rapid test (first test) · DAT (direct agglutination) · stay positive after cure, so they cannot diagnose relapse
ParasitologyLeishman–Donovan bodies in an aspirate: spleen (most sensitive, ~95%; risk of bleeding — check platelets and clotting), bone marrow (~70%), lymph node · needed for relapse and test of cure

Treatment (WHO, East Africa)

Primary VL, age ≥ 4 yearsParomomycin 20 mg/kg/day IM + oral miltefosine (dose by weight band), both for 14 days · not in pregnancy
Primary VL, othersSodium stibogluconate (SSG) 20 mg/kg/day IM or IV + paromomycin 15 mg/kg/day IM for 17 days · ECG (SSG cardiotoxicity), pancreatitis, renal and hearing checks (paromomycin)
Severe disease, relapse, HIV, very youngLiposomal amphotericin B (AmBisome) by IV infusion (total about 30 mg/kg) · with miltefosine in HIV co-infection
SupportiveNutrition (RUTF), blood transfusion, treat infections (TB, pneumonia), treat malaria, iron and folate

After treatment

  • Cure: no fever, spleen smaller, weight gain, Hb rising · follow up at 6 months for relapse
  • Post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis (PKDL): pale or raised papules starting around the mouth, spreading to face and body, during or after treatment · most heal alone · severe or lasting: paromomycin + miltefosine
  • Prevention: insecticide-treated bed nets, early diagnosis and treatment
