Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) is the severe end of protein-energy malnutrition (PEM): a lack of protein and energy, usually with infection and vitamin and mineral deficiencies. It is a leading cause of death under 5 years. Diagnosis and treatment follow the WHO 2023 guideline on wasting and nutritional oedema and the CMAM programme.

Exam trap
  • Kwashiorkor is not defined by a weight below 60% of expected: that is marasmus. In kwashiorkor the weight is 60–80% because the oedema hides the loss.
  • Underweight is not the same as marasmus: marasmus means wasting (loss of fat and muscle), not just a low weight.
  • Bilateral pitting oedema makes the diagnosis SAM whatever the weight or MUAC.

Aetiology and risk factors

Primary (dietary)Poverty, food shortage, displacement · failure of breastfeeding or over-diluted formula (marasmus) · late or poor complementary feeding · abrupt weaning onto a starchy, low-protein diet (kwashiorkor) · large family, short birth spacing
Secondary (disease)Recurrent diarrhoea, measles, TB, HIV, worms, malaria · congenital heart disease, chronic kidney disease · malabsorption · cleft palate, cerebral palsy (feeding difficulty)

Clinical features of kwashiorkor

ConstantOedema (starts on the dorsum of the feet and hands) · growth failure (masked by oedema) · mental changes (apathy, irritability when handled) · muscle wasting with preserved fat
UsualHair: thin, sparse, straight, red or grey, easily plucked; flag sign (pale and dark bands from periods of poor and better nutrition) · moon face · anaemia · diarrhoea
OccasionalFlaky-paint dermatosis (dark patches that peel, leaving raw skin; legs, buttocks, napkin area) · hepatomegaly · angular stomatitis · vitamin A deficiency (eye signs)
Oedema gradeExtent
+ (mild)Both feet
++ (moderate)Feet + lower legs, hands or lower arms
+++ (severe)Generalised, including the face

Test: press both feet with the thumbs for 3 seconds; a pit on both feet = nutritional oedema.

  • Causes of oedema: low albumin (low oncotic pressure) · leaky capillaries (free radicals, infection) · salt and water retention (renin–angiotensin–aldosterone, ADH)
  • Fatty liver: low apolipoprotein → fat cannot leave the liver
  • Marasmic kwashiorkor: severe wasting plus oedema
Exam trap
  • "Kwashiorkor is caused by calorie deficiency" is false: it is mainly protein deficiency; marasmus is the adaptation to starvation.
  • Serum albumin is low in kwashiorkor; "raised albumin" is the false option.
  • Hepatomegaly in kwashiorkor is due to fatty infiltration, and a large liver is a bad sign.

Complications and causes of death

HypoglycaemiaBlood glucose < 3 mmol/L (54 mg/dL) · often with hypothermia and infection
HypothermiaAxillary temperature < 35 °C (rectal < 35.5 °C) · more common in marasmus
InfectionBronchopneumonia (commonest cause of death), sepsis, diarrhoea, TB, skin infection · usually no fever and few signs
Dehydration, electrolytesDiarrhoea → dehydration · low potassium and magnesium, total body sodium high
Heart failureFrom severe anaemia, too much IV fluid or sodium, or too fast refeeding
OthersSevere anaemia · vitamin A deficiency (corneal ulcer, blindness) · bleeding (purpura)
Dehydration signs in SAM
Reliable: history of watery diarrhoea · thirst · recently sunken eyes (mother says) · weak or absent radial pulse · little or no urine
Unreliable (also present without dehydration): skin pinch, sunken eyes, dry mouth, lethargy
  • Poor prognostic signs: hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, jaundice, large liver, purpura, deep corneal ulcer, very low albumin, shock
  • Refeeding syndrome: feeding too fast → low phosphate, potassium and magnesium → heart failure, death
Exam trap
  • A child with SAM who has an infection often has no fever; hypothermia and hypoglycaemia are signs of infection, so every child gets antibiotics.
  • Skin pinch and sunken eyes overestimate dehydration in marasmus; treating them as normal children gives fluid overload and heart failure.

The WHO ten steps

Steps 1–4: emergency treatment

HypoglycaemiaConscious: 50 ml of 10% glucose (or sugar water) orally or by NGT, then F-75 · Unconscious: 10% glucose 5 ml/kg IV, then 50 ml by NGT · antibiotics · prevent: feed 2-hourly day and night
HypothermiaFeed at once · skin-to-skin (kangaroo) with the mother, cover the head, warm room or heater · no hot-water bottles · antibiotics · check temperature every 30 minutes
Dehydration (no shock)ReSoMal orally or by NGT, slowly: 5 ml/kg every 30 min for 2 h, then 5–10 ml/kg/h for 4–10 h, alternating with F-75 · after each loose stool 50–100 ml · stop if pulse and breathing rise, eyelids puffy or oedema increases
ShockThe only indication for IV fluid: 15 ml/kg over 1 h of Ringer's lactate with 5% glucose (or half-strength saline with 5% glucose) · improves → repeat, then ReSoMal · no improvement → septic shock: maintenance fluid + blood 10 ml/kg over 3 h
ElectrolytesExtra potassium and magnesium (mineral mix in F-75, ReSoMal) · low-sodium feeds · never diuretics for the oedema
Severe anaemiaHb < 4 g/dL (or < 6 g/dL with distress) → packed cells or whole blood 10 ml/kg over 3 h in the first 24 h
Exam trap
  • Standard ORS is not used in SAM: it has too much sodium and too little potassium. ReSoMal has Na 45 and K 40 mmol/L (ORS: Na 75, K 20).
  • IV fluid only for shock; the oedema of kwashiorkor is not treated with diuretics and not a reason for IV albumin.

Outpatient therapeutic programme (OTP)

  • RUTF (ready-to-use therapeutic food): peanut paste with milk, sugar, oil, vitamins and minerals · 500 kcal per sachet (92 g) · energy like F-100
  • Needs no water or cooking → no contamination at home
  • Dose: about 200 kcal/kg/day (sachets by weight band) · give with plenty of clean water · continue breastfeeding
  • On admission: amoxicillin 15 mg/kg 8-hourly for 5 days · measles vaccine · deworming (albendazole) at the second visit
  • Weekly visits: weight, MUAC, oedema, appetite test, health check
  • Refer to the stabilisation centre: complication, failed appetite test, increasing oedema, weight loss

Discharge and follow-up

Stabilisation centre → OTPAppetite returned (passes the appetite test) · oedema reducing · complications treated · clinically well
Cured (discharge from SAM care)The criterion used at admission is normal: MUAC ≥ 12.5 cm and/or weight-for-height ≥ −2 SD · no oedema · both on 2 consecutive visits
Recovery orderAppetite and smile return first (days 3–4) · oedema goes · skin heals (about 10 days) · weight gain
Step 9–10Play and emotional stimulation, mother stays with the child · teach the mother feeding, hygiene and vaccination · follow up in the community

Moderate acute malnutrition (MAM)

  • MUAC 11.5 to < 12.5 cm or weight-for-height −3 to < −2 SD, no oedema
  • Targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP): ready-to-use supplementary food (RUSF) or fortified blended flour (corn-soya blend), visits every 2 weeks · counselling · treat illness
  • Discharged when MUAC is ≥ 12.5 cm or weight-for-height ≥ −2 SD
CMAM · screening and referral
  • Community workers screen children 6–59 months with the MUAC tape and check both feet for oedema.
  • Red MUAC or oedema → OTP (or the stabilisation centre if complicated) · yellow MUAC → TSFP.
  • Every sick child seen with IMCI is checked for acute malnutrition.

Prevention

  • Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, then good complementary feeding with animal protein and oil
  • Vaccination (measles), vitamin A every 6 months from 6 months to 5 years · ORS and zinc for diarrhoea · birth spacing · growth monitoring
