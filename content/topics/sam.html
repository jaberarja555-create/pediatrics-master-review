Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) is a major cause of death in Sudanese children under 5. It is managed by the CMAM programme (community-based management of acute malnutrition): uncomplicated cases at home with RUTF, complicated cases in a stabilisation centre with F-75 and F-100, following the WHO 10 steps.

Definition of SAM (6–59 months)

Any one of:
  • Weight-for-height < −3 SD (WHZ)
  • MUAC < 11.5 cm
  • Bilateral pitting oedema (nutritional)
Moderate acute malnutrition (MAM):
  • WHZ −3 to −2 SD, or
  • MUAC 11.5–12.5 cm
  • Treated by the supplementary feeding programme

Marasmic–kwashiorkor: severe wasting + oedema (worst prognosis). Grading of oedema: + feet only · ++ feet and legs (and hands) · +++ generalised including face.

Pathophysiology of the dangers

The body slows every system to survive ("reductive adaptation"): the cells pump out less sodium (↑ total body Na, ↓ K, ↓ Mg), the heart is small and weak, and infection gives no fever. Too much fluid, sodium or protein too fast causes heart failure and death.

Where to treat: the CMAM pathway

Inpatient (stabilisation centre)SAM with medical complications (danger signs, pneumonia, dehydration, fever, hypothermia, severe anaemia, skin lesions), oedema +++, failed appetite test, or age < 6 months
Outpatient (OTP)SAM, alert, passes the appetite test, oedema + or ++, no complications → RUTF at home, weekly visit
Appetite testGive RUTF in a quiet place for up to 30 min: child eats at least the target amount (about ⅓ of a packet for a 4–7 kg child) = pass

The 10 steps of inpatient care

Steps 1 and 2: hypoglycaemia and hypothermia

Hypoglycaemia (glucose < 3 mmol/L, 54 mg/dL)
  • Alert: 50 mL of 10% glucose orally or by NG tube, then F-75
  • Unconscious: 10% dextrose 5 mL/kg IV, then 50 mL by NG tube
  • Feed 2-hourly day and night + start antibiotics
Hypothermia (axillary < 35 °C, rectal < 35.5 °C)
  • Feed at once · kangaroo skin-to-skin with the mother, cover head, warm room
  • No hot-water bottles (burns)
  • Hypothermia and hypoglycaemia are signs of infection: give antibiotics

Step 3: dehydration

  • Signs of dehydration are unreliable in SAM (sunken eyes, poor skin turgor are present anyway): rely on the history of watery diarrhoea, thirst, recent sunken eyes, weak pulse, no urine
  • Use ReSoMal (less Na, more K than standard ORS): 5 mL/kg every 30 min for 2 h, then 5–10 mL/kg/h for up to 10 h, alternating with F-75 · after each loose stool: 50–100 mL
  • No IV fluids except in shock: 15 mL/kg over 1 h of half-strength Darrow's or Ringer's lactate with 5% dextrose; repeat once if improving, else blood 10 mL/kg over 3 h
  • Stop fluids if pulse ↑ by 15–25, RR ↑ by 5, liver enlarges, puffy eyelids: heart failure

Steps 4 and 5: electrolytes and infection

  • Give extra potassium and magnesium (electrolyte-mineral solution, already in F-75, F-100 and ReSoMal)
  • Never treat oedema with diuretics · low-sodium foods
  • Antibiotics for every child: uncomplicated: amoxicillin 25 mg/kg 12-hourly × 5 days
  • Complicated: ampicillin 50 mg/kg IV 6-hourly + gentamicin 7.5 mg/kg daily × 7 days
  • Measles vaccine if ≥ 6 months and not given

Step 6: micronutrients

  • Vitamin A on day 1 only if eye signs or recent measles (F-75/F-100/RUTF contain it): < 6 months 50 000 IU · 6–12 months 100 000 IU · > 12 months 200 000 IU
  • Folic acid 5 mg day 1, then 1 mg/day · zinc and copper are in the feeds
  • Iron: never in the stabilisation phase (feeds bacteria, free radicals); start in rehabilitation when the child gains weight (3 mg/kg/day)

F-75: 75 kcal and 0.9 g protein per 100 mL (low protein, low sodium). Severe oedema: 100 mL/kg/day. Feed by cup and spoon; NG tube if the child takes < 80% of the feed. Continue breastfeeding.

Step 8: catch-up growth (rehabilitation)

  • Ready when: appetite returns, oedema reduced (usually day 3–7)
  • Transition: replace F-75 by the same volume of F-100 (100 kcal, 2.9 g protein/100 mL) or RUTF for 2 days, then increase by 10 mL each feed
  • Then 150–220 kcal/kg/day
  • RUTF (Plumpy'Nut): peanut paste, 500 kcal per 92 g sachet, needs no water (no contamination) · ≈ 200 kcal/kg/day
  • Watch for heart failure during transition: pulse ↑ 25 or RR ↑ 5 → reduce the feed
  • Target gain > 10 g/kg/day · < 5 g/kg/day = look for infection (TB, HIV, UTI)

Steps 9 and 10: stimulation and follow-up

  • Play, mother involvement, cheerful environment: malnourished children have delayed development
  • Discharge to OTP when appetite is good, complications resolved, oedema settling · cure when WHZ ≥ −2 SD or MUAC ≥ 12.5 cm and no oedema for 2 weeks
  • Teach the mother to prepare energy-dense food · complete immunization · deworm (albendazole 400 mg once, ≥ 2 yr; 200 mg at 1–2 yr) in rehabilitation
