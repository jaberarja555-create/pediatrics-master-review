The neonatal period is the first 28 days of life. It carries the highest risk of death in childhood: in Sudan most neonatal deaths come from prematurity, birth asphyxia and sepsis. Good routine care at birth (warmth, breathing, breastfeeding, cord and eye care, vitamin K, vaccines) prevents most of them.

  • Scored at 1 and 5 minutes (and every 5 min to 20 min if < 7)
  • 7–10 normal · 4–6 moderately depressed · 0–3 severely depressed
  • Heart rate is the most important sign; colour the least
  • Resuscitation is never delayed to score the Apgar (3.2); a low 5-minute score predicts outcome better

Assessment of gestational age

  • Best: early ultrasound dating · then LMP
  • New Ballard score after birth: 6 neuromuscular + 6 physical signs (accurate ± 2 weeks)
  • Preterm signs: thin, red, gelatinous skin, plenty of lanugo, flat breast areola, soft ear cartilage, few sole creases, undescended testes, prominent labia minora, poor tone (extended limbs)

Common normal findings (reassure)

  • Caput succedaneum: scalp oedema, crosses suture lines, gone in days
  • Mongolian blue spots: sacral, fade by school age (not bruises)
  • Erythema toxicum: red blotches with white centres, day 2–3, eosinophils
  • Milia (white spots on the nose) · Epstein pearls (palate)
  • Stork mark (salmon patch) on the nape
  • Breast enlargement (both sexes) and vaginal bleeding (girls, day 3–7): maternal oestrogen withdrawal
  • Physiological jaundice after 24 h (3.5)
  • Sneezing, hiccups, irregular breathing
  • Umbilical hernia · hydrocele · acrocyanosis
  • Subconjunctival haemorrhage

The full newborn examination (head to toe)

  • General: colour (pink, pale, jaundice, cyanosis), cry, tone, posture, dysmorphism · weight, length, OFC
  • Head: fontanelles, sutures, cephalhaematoma (does not cross sutures) · eyes: red reflex (cataract, retinoblastoma) · palate (cleft) · ears
  • Chest and heart: respiratory distress, murmurs, femoral pulses (coarctation)
  • Abdomen: organs, masses, umbilical stump (2 arteries + 1 vein), anus patent
  • Genitalia: testes descended, hypospadias, ambiguous genitalia
  • Hips: Ortolani (relocates a dislocated hip, "clunk") and Barlow (dislocates an unstable hip) for DDH
  • Spine (dimple, tuft, swelling) · limbs (talipes, extra digits, clavicle) · reflexes (Moro, grasp, suck)

Routine care at birth (essential newborn care)

  1. Dry and keep warm immediately; skin-to-skin with the mother; delay bathing for 24 h
  2. Check breathing; delayed cord clamping (1–3 min) in a baby who breathes; clean cord care with chlorhexidine 4% to the stump
  3. Breastfeed within 1 hour, exclusively
  4. Eye prophylaxis: tetracycline 1% eye ointment once (ophthalmia neonatorum)
  5. Vitamin K 1 mg IM (0.5 mg if < 1.5 kg)
  6. Vaccines: BCG and OPV 0 (1.4) · weigh, examine, identify LBW and danger signs
Neonatal danger signs (IMCI): refer

Not feeding well · convulsions · fast breathing ≥ 60 · severe chest indrawing · temperature ≥ 37.5 or < 35.5 °C · movement only when stimulated · jaundice in the first 24 h or on the palms and soles · umbilical redness spreading to the skin.
