The neonatal period is the first 28 days of life: early (days 0–7) and late (days 8–28). Most neonatal deaths happen in the first week, from prematurity, birth asphyxia and infection.

Normal values of a term newborn

Weight · length · OFC2.5–4 kg · about 50 cm · about 35 cm
Weight changeLoses up to 10% in the first 3–5 days · regains birth weight by 10–14 days · then gains 25–30 g/day
Heart rate120–160/min
Respiratory rate40–60/min · abdominal, nose breathing, may be periodic
TemperatureAxillary 36.5–37.5 °C
Blood glucose≥ 2.6 mmol/L (47 mg/dL) after the first hours
Haemoglobin14–20 g/dL at birth · falls to a low of 10–11 g/dL at 8–12 weeks (physiological anaemia)
Meconium · urineMeconium within 24–48 h (most in 24 h) · urine within 24 h
Exam trap
  • A term baby who has not passed meconium by 48 hours needs examination for imperforate anus, Hirschsprung disease and meconium ileus (cystic fibrosis).
  • No urine by 24 hours: look for a palpable bladder (posterior urethral valves) or renal agenesis, and check perfusion.
  • Weight loss of more than 10% suggests poor feeding or dehydration.

Normal findings that need only reassurance

Erythema toxicumCommonest rash (50%): red blotches with small pustules on the trunk and face, days 2–3 · baby well · pustules contain eosinophils · fades in days
Milia · miliariaMilia: tiny white papules on the nose (blocked sebaceous glands) · miliaria: sweat rash from overheating
Mongolian spotsBlue-grey patches over the lower back and buttocks · common in dark-skinned babies · fade in years · not bruises
Salmon patchPink macules on the eyelids, forehead ("angel's kiss") and nape ("stork bite") · fade
OthersAcrocyanosis (blue hands and feet) in the first day · caput succedaneum · moulding of the skull · breast enlargement and witch's milk in both sexes · vaginal mucus or small bleed (maternal hormone withdrawal) · Epstein pearls on the palate · umbilical hernia · liver 1–2 cm below the costal margin · innocent soft murmur in the first day
Best next step · a girl newborn has bilateral breast swelling with a little milk
  • Reassure: it is due to maternal hormones and settles in a few weeks.
  • Do not squeeze the breasts: squeezing causes mastitis and abscess.

Abnormal findings that need action

Central cyanosisBlue tongue and lips at any time · look for heart or lung disease
Jaundice in the first 24 hAlways pathological (haemolysis, infection)
Sacral dimple, tuft of hairMidline lesion over the spine: spina bifida occulta, tethered cord → ultrasound
Absent red reflexCongenital cataract (rubella), retinoblastoma → urgent eye review
Port-wine stainPresent from birth, does not fade · on the face (ophthalmic area) → Sturge–Weber syndrome
Strawberry naevusInfantile haemangioma: appears in the first weeks, grows to about 1 year, then regresses · propranolol if near the eye or airway
OtherCleft palate (feel the palate) · choanal atresia (cyanosis at rest, pink when crying; NGT will not pass) · single umbilical artery (renal anomalies) · weak femoral pulses (coarctation) · unstable hip (DDH) · ambiguous genitalia

Routine care at birth (WHO essential newborn care)

  1. Dry and skin-to-skin with the mother · keep warm
  2. Delayed cord clamping (at least 1 minute) · cut with a sterile blade
  3. Breastfeeding within the first hour, exclusive
  4. Vitamin K 1 mg IM (prevents vitamin K deficiency bleeding)
  5. Eye prophylaxis: tetracycline 1% or erythromycin ointment (ophthalmia neonatorum)
  1. Dry cord care; chlorhexidine 7.1% to the cord stump where home births and cord infection are common
  2. BCG, OPV 0 and hepatitis B at birth (EPI)
  3. Full examination within 24 h · weight
  4. Bath delayed to 24 hours
  5. Teach the mother the danger signs

Clinical examinationRed reflex (cataract) · palate · hips (Barlow and Ortolani) · femoral pulses · testes · spine · anus
Pulse oximetryRight hand and a foot after 24 h: critical congenital heart disease
HearingOtoacoustic emissions before discharge or by 1 month
Heel-prick blood spotDay 2–7: congenital hypothyroidism (TSH), phenylketonuria, sickle cell disease, cystic fibrosis, other metabolic diseases
IMCI · danger signs in a young infant (refer urgently)
  • Not feeding well · convulsions · fast breathing (60 or more per minute) · severe chest indrawing
  • Fever (37.5 °C or more) or low temperature (below 35.5 °C) · movement only when stimulated or no movement
  • Also: jaundice in the first 24 h or on the palms and soles, umbilical redness spreading to the skin, many skin pustules.

Primitive reflexes at birth

  • Present at birth: Moro, rooting, sucking, palmar and plantar grasp, stepping, placing, ATNR · parachute is not present (appears at 8–9 months)
  • Moro absent on both sides: CNS depression (HIE, sepsis, kernicterus, drugs) · absent on one side: Erb palsy or fractured clavicle or humerus (details in 1.2 Development)
