Thyroid hormone is essential for brain development in the first 2–3 years and for growth throughout childhood. Congenital hypothyroidism is the commonest preventable cause of intellectual disability; treatment started in the first 2 weeks gives normal intelligence, so many countries screen all newborns.

Congenital hypothyroidism

  • Incidence 1 in 2000–4000 · causes: thyroid dysgenesis (absent or ectopic gland, 85%) · dyshormonogenesis (goitre, familial) · iodine deficiency (endemic) · maternal antithyroid drugs (transient)
  • Most look normal at birth (maternal T4)
  • Features (weeks–months): prolonged jaundice, poor feeding, constipation, lethargy, sleepy, hoarse cry, large tongue, umbilical hernia, large anterior (and posterior) fontanelle, dry skin, cold, hypotonia, coarse face
  • Later: developmental delay, short stature (delayed bone age)
ScreeningHeel-prick TSH on day 2–5 (↑)
ConfirmSerum TSH ↑, free T4 ↓ · thyroid ultrasound/scan · knee X-ray (absent distal femoral epiphysis)
TreatmentLevothyroxine 10–15 µg/kg/day once daily, start immediately (crushed tablet, not with soy or iron) · keep T4 high-normal, TSH normal · monitor every 1–2 months in infancy · lifelong in most

Acquired hypothyroidism

  • Hashimoto (autoimmune) thyroiditis: commonest in older children, girls, associated with type 1 DM, Down and Turner syndromes · iodine deficiency (endemic goitre in Sudan)
  • Features: growth failure (short and overweight, delayed bone age), fatigue, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, poor school performance, goitre, delayed (or rarely precocious) puberty
  • Labs: ↑ TSH, ↓ free T4, anti-TPO antibodies
  • Treatment: levothyroxine 2–5 µg/kg/day (by age) · iodised salt for iodine deficiency

Hyperthyroidism

  • Graves disease (TSH-receptor stimulating antibodies): commonest, adolescent girls
  • Features: weight loss with good appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, tachycardia, tremor, anxiety, poor concentration, accelerated growth, diarrhoea, goitre (diffuse, bruit), exophthalmos (milder than adults)
  • Labs: ↓ TSH, ↑ free T4/T3, TRAb positive
  • Treatment: carbimazole 0.25–1 mg/kg/day (methimazole) for 2–3 years · propranolol for symptoms · watch for agranulocytosis (sore throat, fever: stop and check CBC) · propylthiouracil not used in children (liver failure)
  • Relapse: radioiodine or thyroidectomy
  • Neonatal thyrotoxicosis: baby of a mother with Graves (antibodies cross the placenta): irritability, tachycardia, poor weight gain · transient
