Acute flaccid paralysis (AFP) is the sudden onset of weakness with low tone and reduced reflexes in a child under 15 years. Every case is reported to the EPI surveillance system and two stool samples are sent to exclude polio (4.9). The commonest cause today is Guillain–Barré syndrome.

Causes of AFP

  • Guillain–Barré syndrome (commonest)
  • Poliomyelitis (wild or vaccine-derived)
  • Transverse myelitis, spinal cord compression (TB spine, tumour, trauma)
  • Traumatic neuritis (sciatic nerve from IM injection in the buttock)
  • Hypokalaemia, botulism, tick paralysis, snake bite (neurotoxic), myositis

Guillain–Barré syndrome (GBS)

  • Acute immune demyelinating polyneuropathy 1–3 weeks after a respiratory or GI infection (Campylobacter, CMV, EBV)
  • Ascending symmetrical weakness (legs first), areflexia, mild sensory symptoms (paraesthesia, pain), cranial nerves (facial), autonomic instability
  • Danger: respiratory muscle weakness and bulbar palsy (aspiration)
  • CSF: albuminocytological dissociation (↑ protein, normal cells) after the first week
  • Nerve conduction: slowed / blocked
  • Treatment: monitor vital capacity, swallowing, BP, HR · IVIG 2 g/kg over 2–5 days (or plasmapheresis) · ventilation if VC < 15–20 mL/kg · DVT prevention, physiotherapy · no steroids
  • Most recover fully over weeks–months

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD)

  • X-linked recessive · boys · absent dystrophin (one third new mutations)
  • Delayed walking, frequent falls, waddling gait, toe walking, difficulty climbing stairs (2–5 years)
  • Gowers' sign: "climbs up his legs" to stand (proximal hip weakness)
  • Calf pseudohypertrophy (fat and fibrous tissue)
  • Learning difficulty in some
  • CK very high (10–100 × normal, even at birth) · genetic testing (deletion) · muscle biopsy (absent dystrophin)
  • Course: wheelchair by ~12 years, scoliosis, contractures, cardiomyopathy, respiratory failure → death in the 20s–30s (longer with care)
  • Treatment: corticosteroids (prednisolone/deflazacort) slow progression · physiotherapy, night splints, spinal surgery, non-invasive ventilation, cardiac drugs · genetic counselling

Becker muscular dystrophy: reduced (not absent) dystrophin, later onset, slower course, walking beyond 16 years.

Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA)

  • Autosomal recessive, SMN1 deletion · degeneration of anterior horn cells
  • Type 1 (Werdnig–Hoffmann): before 6 months, never sits, death by 2 years without treatment
  • Alert floppy baby, tongue fasciculations, areflexia, bell-shaped chest
  • New gene therapies (nusinersen, onasemnogene) · supportive care
