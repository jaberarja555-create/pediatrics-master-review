About 60% of term and 80% of preterm babies become jaundiced in the first week. Most is physiological, but high unconjugated bilirubin can cross the blood–brain barrier and cause kernicterus (permanent brain damage), and some jaundice is the first sign of sepsis, haemolysis or liver disease. Jaundice is visible when serum bilirubin exceeds about 5 mg/dL (85 µmol/L) in the newborn.

Clinical assessment

  • Examine in daylight: press the skin to blanch it · jaundice spreads head to toe (cephalocaudal, Kramer zones)
  • Face ≈ 5 · chest ≈ 10 · abdomen ≈ 12 · legs ≈ 15 · palms and soles > 15 mg/dL: measure at once
  • Visual estimate is unreliable in dark skin: always measure serum (or transcutaneous) bilirubin if in doubt
  • Look for: pallor, cephalhaematoma, hepatosplenomegaly, signs of sepsis, stool and urine colour

Investigations

  • Total and direct (conjugated) bilirubin · blood groups of mother and baby · direct Coombs test
  • CBC with reticulocytes and blood film (spherocytes, fragmented cells) · G6PD screen
  • If sick: blood culture, CRP, urine · prolonged: TSH, urine culture, LFT, stool colour, ultrasound (biliary atresia: absent gallbladder, triangular cord sign), HIDA scan

Use lower thresholds (by 2–3 mg/dL) with risk factors: haemolysis, G6PD, sepsis, acidosis, asphyxia, low albumin. Preterm: phototherapy at (gestation in weeks × 10 − 100) µmol/L and exchange at (gestation × 10) µmol/L (÷ 17 for mg/dL); e.g. 32 weeks: phototherapy 220 µmol/L (13 mg/dL).

Phototherapy

  • Blue light (430–490 nm) changes bilirubin to water-soluble isomers (lumirubin) excreted in bile and urine without conjugation
  • Baby naked, eyes covered, turned regularly, light 30–50 cm away
  • Continue breastfeeding; extra fluid 10–20 mL/kg/day
  • Check bilirubin every 6–12 h; stop when 2–3 mg/dL below the start line
  • Side effects: dehydration, loose green stool, rash, hyper/hypothermia, separation from mother
  • Bronze baby syndrome if conjugated bilirubin is high: phototherapy is contraindicated in cholestatic jaundice
  • IV immunoglobulin 0.5–1 g/kg in Rh/ABO haemolysis rising despite phototherapy

Exchange transfusion

  • Indications: bilirubin at the exchange line despite intensive phototherapy, signs of acute bilirubin encephalopathy (at any level), severe haemolysis with anaemia (cord Hb < 11 g/dL)
  • Double-volume exchange: 2 × 85 mL/kg (≈ 170 mL/kg) via the umbilical vein, in 10–20 mL aliquots · removes 85% of RBCs and 50% of bilirubin
  • Blood: fresh, O Rh-negative cross-matched with the mother (Rh disease) or O with baby's Rh (ABO)
  • Complications: hypocalcaemia, hypoglycaemia, hyperkalaemia, thrombocytopenia, infection, NEC, portal vein thrombosis, air embolism

Bilirubin encephalopathy and kernicterus

  • Free unconjugated bilirubin enters the brain and stains the basal ganglia · risk ↑ with prematurity, acidosis, hypoalbuminaemia, sepsis, drugs that displace bilirubin (ceftriaxone, sulphonamides)
  • Acute: lethargy, poor suck, hypotonia → hypertonia, opisthotonus, retrocollis, high-pitched cry, fever, seizures
  • Chronic (kernicterus): choreoathetoid cerebral palsy, sensorineural deafness, upward-gaze palsy, dental enamel dysplasia, intellectual disability
  • Prevention is the goal: treat by the threshold charts; every baby with severe jaundice gets a hearing test

Biliary atresia

  • Progressive obliteration of the extrahepatic bile ducts · commonest cause of conjugated jaundice needing surgery and the commonest reason for liver transplant in children
  • Prolonged jaundice + pale (acholic) stool + dark urine in a baby who is otherwise well and gaining weight · later hepatomegaly, splenomegaly
  • Ultrasound (absent or small gallbladder), HIDA (no excretion), liver biopsy, operative cholangiogram
  • Kasai portoenterostomy best before 60 days of age: success falls sharply after 8 weeks
Best next step

Any baby jaundiced at 2 weeks (3 weeks if preterm) needs a split (total and conjugated) bilirubin, stool colour check, TSH and urine culture. A conjugated level above 1 mg/dL is a surgical emergency until biliary atresia is excluded.
