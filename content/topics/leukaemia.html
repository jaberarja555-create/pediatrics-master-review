Leukaemia is the commonest childhood cancer (about one third). Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL) makes up about 80%, peak age 2–5 years; AML about 15%. Malignant blasts fill the marrow and cause bone marrow failure (anaemia, infection, bleeding) and infiltrate organs. With modern treatment about 90% of children with ALL are cured in high-income centres.

Clinical features

Bone marrow failure
  • Anaemia: pallor, fatigue
  • Neutropenia: fever, infections
  • Thrombocytopenia: petechiae, bruising, epistaxis
Infiltration
  • Bone pain, limp, refusal to walk (may mimic arthritis)
  • Hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy
  • CNS (headache, cranial nerve palsy), testes (painless swelling), mediastinal mass (T-cell: stridor, SVC obstruction), gum hypertrophy (AML)

Investigations

  • CBC: anaemia, thrombocytopenia, WBC low, normal or very high · blasts on the film
  • Bone marrow aspirate (diagnostic): > 20–25% blasts · immunophenotyping (B or T), cytogenetics
  • CXR (mediastinal mass) · LP (CNS disease) · uric acid, K, PO₄, Ca, creatinine, LDH · coagulation · HIV, hepatitis

Treatment

InductionSteroid (dexamethasone/prednisolone), vincristine, asparaginase (± daunorubicin) · goal: remission in 4 weeks
CNS prophylaxisIntrathecal methotrexate (± cranial radiotherapy in high risk)
Consolidation / intensificationFurther combination chemotherapy
MaintenanceDaily 6-mercaptopurine + weekly methotrexate, total treatment about 2–3 years
SupportiveBlood and platelet transfusions, cotrimoxazole (PCP prophylaxis), treat febrile neutropenia, nutrition, psychosocial support · no live vaccines

Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML)

  • ≈ 15% of childhood leukaemia · infants and adolescents · Down syndrome (megakaryoblastic), previous chemotherapy, Fanconi anaemia
  • Auer rods on film · gum hypertrophy, skin nodules, chloroma (orbit: proptosis), DIC (promyelocytic, APL)
  • Treatment: intensive chemotherapy (cytarabine + anthracycline), bone marrow transplant in high risk · APL: all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA)
  • Cure about 60–70%

Tumour lysis syndrome

  • Rapid breakdown of tumour cells (high-count leukaemia, Burkitt lymphoma) on starting treatment, or spontaneously
  • ↑ uric acid, ↑ K, ↑ PO₄, ↓ Ca → AKI, arrhythmia, seizures
  • Prevention: hyperhydration (2–3 L/m²/day, no K), allopurinol 10 mg/kg/day (or rasburicase), monitor electrolytes 6–8-hourly
  • Treat hyperkalaemia (5.2), dialysis if AKI

Febrile neutropenia

DefinitionFever ≥ 38.3 °C once (or ≥ 38 °C for 1 h) with neutrophils < 500/µL
Why urgentNo pus or signs of focus; Gram-negative sepsis can kill within hours
ManagementBlood cultures (peripheral + line) and start broad-spectrum IV antibiotics within 1 hour: piperacillin–tazobactam or ceftazidime / cefepime (± amikacin) · add vancomycin for line infection or shock · antifungal if fever persists after 4–7 days

When to suspect cancer in a child

  • Unexplained pallor, bruising, bone pain, persistent fever
  • Lymph nodes > 2 cm, hard, fixed, supraclavicular, growing over 2–4 weeks
  • Abdominal mass
  • White pupillary reflex (leukocoria), new squint
  • Morning headache with vomiting, ataxia, cranial nerve palsies (brain tumour)
  • Unexplained weight loss, night sweats
