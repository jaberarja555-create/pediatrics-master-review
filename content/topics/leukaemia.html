Leukaemia is the commonest childhood cancer (about one third). Blast cells fill the marrow (marrow failure) and spread to other organs (infiltration). Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL) is about 75–80%, peak age 2–5 years, boys more; acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) about 15–20%; chronic myeloid leukaemia is rare.

  • Risk factors: Down syndrome, Fanconi anaemia, ataxia telangiectasia, neurofibromatosis · previous chemotherapy or radiation · the history is short and may look like a viral illness, arthritis or ITP

Clinical features

Marrow failurePallor, tiredness (anaemia) · petechiae, bruises, nosebleeds (low platelets) · fever and infections that do not settle (few working neutrophils)
InfiltrationBone pain, limp, refusal to walk (child wakes at night) · hepatosplenomegaly · generalised lymphadenopathy · mediastinal mass (T-cell ALL: cough, stridor, superior vena cava obstruction) · testis: painless swelling · CNS: headache, vomiting, cranial nerve palsy · AML: gum hypertrophy, chloroma (green mass, proptosis), skin nodules

Investigations

  • CBC and film: anaemia (normocytic), low platelets, WBC low, normal or very high · blasts on the film (absent in some) · may be pancytopenia
  • Bone marrow aspirate (diagnostic): ≥ 25% blasts (20% for AML) · immunophenotyping (flow cytometry: B or T cell, myeloid) · cytogenetics (prognosis) · cytochemistry: AML blasts are myeloperoxidase positive and may contain Auer rods
  • Lumbar puncture: CNS disease · chest X-ray: mediastinal mass (before any sedation) · uric acid, potassium, phosphate, calcium, creatinine (tumour lysis) · LFT, coagulation (DIC in AML), blood culture if febrile · echocardiography before anthracyclines · blood group

Differential diagnosis

ITPOnly platelets low, well child, no organomegaly, no bone pain
Aplastic anaemiaPancytopenia, no organomegaly or nodes, empty marrow
Kala-azar (4.7)Long fever, huge spleen, pancytopenia, wasting · rK39, marrow or splenic aspirate shows Leishman–Donovan bodies
Juvenile idiopathic arthritisFever, joint pain, anaemia · platelets high (low in leukaemia) · night bone pain suggests leukaemia
Infectious mononucleosisFever, nodes, spleen, atypical lymphocytes · monospot or EBV IgM
Leukaemoid reactionVery high WBC of mature cells with sepsis, pertussis, TB · no blasts · settles with the illness
Marrow infiltration by tumourNeuroblastoma (8.6) · abdominal mass, raised urinary catecholamines

Try it · exam-style
A child has fever, joint pain, anaemia and a very high platelet count. Leukaemia or JIA?

Treatment of ALL

Induction (4 weeks)Vincristine (IV weekly) + steroid (prednisolone or dexamethasone) + asparaginase ± anthracycline (daunorubicin) in high risk · intrathecal methotrexate · aim: remission (< 5% blasts in a normal marrow) in about 98%
CNS treatmentIntrathecal methotrexate repeated through treatment (the CNS is a sanctuary site) · cranial radiation only for some with CNS disease
ConsolidationSeveral drugs over months · minimal residual disease (MRD) guides intensity
Maintenance (2–3 years total)Daily oral 6-mercaptopurine + weekly oral methotrexate, with vincristine and steroid pulses · co-trimoxazole against Pneumocystis pneumonia
RelapseMarrow, CNS or testis · intensive chemotherapy, stem cell transplant
  • AML: shorter, more intensive chemotherapy (cytarabine + anthracycline) · stem cell transplant for high risk · acute promyelocytic leukaemia (t(15;17)): all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) + arsenic · cure about 60–70% · chronic myeloid leukaemia: Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22), huge spleen, very high WBC · imatinib

Oncological emergencies

Tumour lysis syndromeRapid cell death at the start of chemotherapy (high-count ALL, Burkitt lymphoma): high uric acid, potassium and phosphate, low calcium → acute kidney injury, arrhythmia, tetany · prevention: hyperhydration (2–3 L/m²/day, no potassium), allopurinol or rasburicase (high risk), monitor electrolytes 6–12-hourly · treat hyperkalaemia; dialysis if needed
Febrile neutropeniaFever ≥ 38.3 °C once (or ≥ 38 °C for 1 hour) with neutrophils < 0.5 × 10⁹/L · cultures, then broad-spectrum IV antibiotic within 1 hour (piperacillin–tazobactam or an antipseudomonal cephalosporin) · no rectal temperature
Mediastinal massAirway and SVC compression · avoid general anaesthesia and lying flat · steroids, urgent diagnosis

Side effects of chemotherapy

  • All: marrow suppression, infection, mucositis, vomiting, hair loss · vincristine: neuropathy, constipation, jaw pain (never intrathecal: fatal) · anthracyclines: cardiomyopathy · asparaginase: allergy, pancreatitis, thrombosis · methotrexate: mucositis, liver and kidney damage · steroids: hyperglycaemia, avascular necrosis · cyclophosphamide: haemorrhagic cystitis, infertility · no live vaccines during treatment
Best next step · child on chemotherapy with fever
  • Examine, take blood cultures (and from the central line) and start IV broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour, without waiting for the neutrophil count.
