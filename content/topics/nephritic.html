Acute nephritic syndrome is glomerular inflammation causing haematuria (cola-coloured urine), oedema, hypertension and oliguria, with some proteinuria. In Sudanese children the commonest cause is post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (PSGN), following throat or skin (impetigo, scabies) infection. It usually heals completely.

Investigations

  • Urine: RBCs, red cell casts, protein · UPCR
  • Urea, creatinine, K, Na · CBC
  • C3 low, C4 normal
  • Evidence of streptococcus: ASO (throat) or anti-DNase B (skin), throat/skin swab
  • CXR (pulmonary oedema, cardiomegaly) if breathless

Management

  1. Fluid restriction: insensible losses (400 mL/m²/day) + previous day's urine output · salt restriction · daily weight, input–output chart, BP 4-hourly
  2. Furosemide 1–2 mg/kg for oedema and hypertension
  3. Hypertension: nifedipine 0.25–0.5 mg/kg or amlodipine · hypertensive encephalopathy (headache, vomiting, seizures, blurred vision): IV labetalol/hydralazine, lower BP by ≤ 25% in 8 h
  4. Penicillin for 10 days to clear streptococci (does not change the nephritis)
  5. Dialysis for severe AKI, hyperkalaemia, refractory fluid overload
  • Prognosis: excellent (> 95%): diuresis in 1 week, BP normal in 2–3 weeks, C3 normal by 8 weeks, microscopic haematuria may last 1–2 years
  • Biopsy if: C3 still low after 8 weeks, nephrotic-range proteinuria, rising creatinine (RPGN), no evidence of strep

Henoch–Schönlein purpura (IgA vasculitis)

  • Commonest vasculitis of childhood
  • 3–10 years
  • After an upper respiratory infection
  • IgA deposits in small vessels
  • Skin: palpable purpura on the buttocks and extensor legs (gravity-dependent), with normal platelets
  • Joints: arthralgia/arthritis of knees and ankles (no damage)
  • Gut: colicky abdominal pain, GI bleeding, intussusception (ileo-ileal)
  • Kidney (30–50%): haematuria ± proteinuria, nephritis/nephrotic, rarely CKD · most within 4–6 weeks
  • Scrotal swelling (orchitis) in boys
  • Diagnosis:
    • Clinical
    • Platelets, clotting normal
    • Urine and BP at every visit for 6–12 months
  • Treatment: supportive, paracetamol/NSAID (if kidneys normal)
  • Prednisolone for severe abdominal pain
  • Nephritis with heavy proteinuria: steroids/immunosuppression, biopsy
