Acute rheumatic fever (ARF) is an immune reaction 2–4 weeks after a group A streptococcal throat infection: antibodies against the bacteria cross-react with heart, joint, brain and skin tissue (molecular mimicry). It affects children 5–15 years. ARF and its sequel, rheumatic heart disease (RHD), remain common in Sudan and are the leading cause of acquired heart disease in children.

Revised Jones criteria

Major criteriaMinor criteria
  • Carditis (clinical or on echo): pancarditis; mitral regurgitation (apical pansystolic), aortic regurgitation, Carey Coombs murmur, pericarditis, heart failure
  • Polyarthritis: large joints, migratory, very painful, dramatic response to aspirin, no deformity
  • Sydenham chorea: late (months), involuntary movements, emotional lability, girls
  • Erythema marginatum: pink rings on the trunk, not itchy
  • Subcutaneous nodules: painless, over extensor surfaces, with carditis
  • Fever ≥ 38.5 °C
  • Arthralgia (if arthritis is not a major criterion)
  • ↑ ESR (≥ 60 mm/h) or CRP (≥ 3 mg/dL)
  • Prolonged PR interval on ECG (if carditis is not counted)
Diagnosis

Evidence of recent streptococcal infection (raised or rising ASO / anti-DNase B titre, positive throat culture or rapid antigen) plus 2 major, or 1 major + 2 minor criteria. Chorea alone is enough (no evidence of strep needed). Recurrence in a patient with RHD: 3 minor criteria are enough.

Investigations

  • ASO titre (> 200–333 IU in children) · throat swab
  • ESR, CRP, CBC (leucocytosis, anaemia)
  • ECG: prolonged PR, heart block · CXR: cardiomegaly
  • Echocardiography in every case: valve regurgitation (subclinical carditis)

Treatment of acute rheumatic fever

Eradicate strepBenzathine penicillin G IM once (600 000 U < 30 kg · 1.2 million U ≥ 30 kg) or oral penicillin V 10 days
ArthritisAspirin 50–60 mg/kg/day (up to 80–100) in 4 doses for 2 weeks, then taper over 2–4 weeks (or naproxen 10–20 mg/kg/day)
CarditisBed rest · heart failure: furosemide, captopril · prednisolone 2 mg/kg/day (max 60 mg) for 2–3 weeks then taper, in severe carditis with heart failure
ChoreaQuiet environment · haloperidol, valproate or carbamazepine if severe
Follow-upEcho, ESR/CRP to monitor · start secondary prophylaxis at once

Secondary prophylaxis (prevents recurrence)

  • Benzathine penicillin G IM every 3–4 weeks (every 3 weeks in high-risk areas)
  • Penicillin allergy: erythromycin 250 mg 12-hourly orally

Primary prevention: treat every streptococcal sore throat with penicillin for 10 days (6.1).

Chronic rheumatic heart disease

LesionSigns
Mitral regurgitationCommonest in children · apical pansystolic murmur radiating to the axilla, displaced apex
Mitral stenosisLater (adolescents) · loud S1, opening snap, low-pitched mid-diastolic rumble at the apex (left lateral position), AF, pulmonary hypertension, haemoptysis
Aortic regurgitationEarly diastolic murmur at the left sternal edge (sitting forward, expiration) · collapsing pulse, wide pulse pressure
  • Complications: heart failure, infective endocarditis, atrial fibrillation, stroke, recurrences
  • Treatment: prophylaxis, heart failure drugs, dental care, valve repair or replacement (warfarin for mechanical valves)
