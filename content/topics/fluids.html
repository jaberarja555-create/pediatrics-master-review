Children have a higher water turnover than adults, so they become dehydrated or overloaded quickly. IV fluid is a drug: prescribe the type, volume and rate, and check weight, urine output and electrolytes. Use isotonic fluids for maintenance to avoid hospital-acquired hyponatraemia.

Body water

  • Total body water: preterm ≈ 80% · term ≈ 75% · 1 year ≈ 65% · adult ≈ 60% of weight
  • Infants have more extracellular fluid, a large surface area and immature kidneys
  • Normal urine output: infant 1–2 mL/kg/h · child ≥ 1 mL/kg/h · oliguria < 1 mL/kg/h (infants), < 0.5 (children)

Example: 25 kg → 1000 + 500 + 100 = 1600 mL/day (≈ 65 mL/h). Maximum usually 2–2.5 L/day. Newborns: see 3.3 (60–80 mL/kg on day 1, rising to 150).

Choice of fluid

Maintenance (> 1 month)Isotonic: 0.9% saline with 5% dextrose (or Ringer's lactate with dextrose) · add KCl 20 mmol/L once passing urine
Neonates10% dextrose day 1, then add Na and K
Resuscitation / deficit0.9% saline or Ringer's lactate · never dextrose-only fluids for boluses
AvoidHypotonic fluids (0.18% saline, 5% dextrose alone): hyponatraemia and cerebral oedema, because illness raises ADH

Fluid restriction (⅔ maintenance)

  • Meningitis / encephalitis with SIADH, raised ICP, HIE · AKI (insensible losses 400 mL/m²/day + urine output) · heart failure · post-operative · bronchiolitis and pneumonia (ADH ↑)

Deficit replacement

  • Deficit (mL) = % dehydration × weight (kg) × 10
  • e.g. 10 kg child, 5% dehydrated → 500 mL
  • Give deficit + maintenance over 24 h (48 h if hypernatraemic), minus boluses
  • Replace ongoing losses (stool, vomit, NG) mL for mL
  • Reassess weight, urine output, Na and K every 6–12 h
  • Diarrhoea: prefer the IMCI plans (5.1)
