Convulsive status epilepticus is a seizure lasting ≥ 5 minutes (operational definition) or repeated seizures without recovery in between. Brain injury becomes likely after 30 minutes, so treatment follows a timed protocol. In Sudan the commonest causes are febrile seizures, cerebral malaria, meningitis, hypoglycaemia and stopping anticonvulsants.

Treatment protocol

TimeAction
0–5 min
  • ABC, recovery position, oxygen, suction
  • Time the fit
  • Check glucose: if low give 10% dextrose 2–5 mL/kg
  • IV/IO access, bloods
5 min: first dose
  • Diazepam 0.3 mg/kg IV (max 10 mg) over 2 min, or 0.5 mg/kg rectally
  • Or midazolam 0.3 mg/kg buccal / 0.15 mg/kg IV
  • Or lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV
10 min: second doseRepeat the benzodiazepine once (no more than 2 doses: respiratory depression)
15–20 min: second line
  • Phenytoin 20 mg/kg IV over 20 min (ECG, not in dextrose)
  • Or phenobarbitone 20 mg/kg IV over 20 min (first choice in neonates; where phenytoin is unavailable)
  • Or levetiracetam 40 mg/kg / valproate 40 mg/kg
35–40 min: refractorySecond second-line drug, then ICU: rapid sequence intubation with thiopental (4 mg/kg) or midazolam infusion

Investigations and care

  • Glucose, Na, Ca, Mg, CBC, malaria film/RDT, blood culture, blood gas, drug levels (on AEDs), toxicology
  • LP when stable and no raised ICP (meningitis) · CT/MRI if focal or trauma
  • Treat the cause: fever (paracetamol), antibiotics (ceftriaxone), artesunate, correct electrolytes
  • Monitor breathing (benzodiazepines), BP, temperature, GCS

Assessing consciousness

  • AVPU: P or U ≈ GCS ≤ 8 (airway at risk)
  • Glasgow coma scale (modified for children < 5): eyes 4, verbal 5, motor 6 · GCS ≤ 8 = coma
  • Blantyre coma scale (young children, malaria):
    • Eye movement 0–2, verbal 0–2, motor 0–1
    • ≤ 2 = cerebral malaria
  • Check pupils, posture, neck stiffness, fontanelle, fundi, focal signs, rash, smell (ketones)

Raised intracranial pressure

  • Signs: headache, vomiting, falling GCS, unequal or dilated pupils, abnormal posturing (decorticate/decerebrate), papilloedema, bulging fontanelle
  • Cushing triad (late): bradycardia, hypertension, irregular breathing → herniation
Management
  • Head up 30°, midline, avoid neck compression
  • Intubate if GCS ≤ 8 · normal CO₂ (brief hyperventilation only for herniation)
  • Mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg IV over 20 min or 3% saline 3–5 mL/kg
  • Avoid hypotonic fluids, fever, hypoxia, seizures
  • Treat cause
  • No LP
