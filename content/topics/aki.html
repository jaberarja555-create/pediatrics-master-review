Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a sudden fall in kidney function: creatinine rising (≥ 1.5 × baseline) and/or urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours. In Sudanese children the commonest causes are dehydration from gastroenteritis, sepsis, severe malaria, haemolysis and HUS. Most AKI is reversible if the cause is treated early.

Management

  1. Assess volume: hypovolaemic → fluid bolus 10–20 mL/kg normal saline and reassess urine output · catheter to measure output and relieve obstruction
  2. Once euvolaemic: fluid = insensible losses (400 mL/m²/day) + urine output + other losses · daily weight
  3. Trial of furosemide 2 mg/kg IV if overloaded (only after volume is restored)
  4. Hyperkalaemia (5.2): stop K, calcium gluconate, salbutamol, insulin–dextrose · acidosis: bicarbonate · hyponatraemia: restrict water · hypocalcaemia, hyperphosphataemia
  5. Hypertension: nifedipine, amlodipine · adjust drug doses for kidney function, avoid nephrotoxins
  6. Nutrition: adequate calories, moderate protein, low K and PO₄
Indications for dialysis (peritoneal dialysis in most Sudanese centres)

Refractory hyperkalaemia (> 6.5–7 mmol/L) · fluid overload with pulmonary oedema or hypertension not responding · severe acidosis · uraemia (encephalopathy, pericarditis, bleeding) · prolonged anuria · removal of toxins (tumour lysis).

Haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS)

  • Commonest cause of intrinsic AKI in young children
  • Shiga toxin from E. coli O157 (EHEC) or Shigella, after bloody diarrhoea (5–10 days later)
  • Triad: microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia (fragmented RBCs/schistocytes) + thrombocytopenia + AKI
  • Pallor, jaundice, oliguria, oedema, hypertension, petechiae, seizures (CNS)
  • Coombs negative, ↑ LDH, normal clotting (unlike DIC)
  • Treatment: supportive, transfusion, dialysis
  • No antibiotics or antidiarrhoeals (more toxin release)
  • Atypical HUS: eculizumab

Chronic kidney disease (CKD)

  • Kidney damage or GFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m² for > 3 months · stage 5 (GFR < 15) = end-stage
  • Causes in children:
    • Congenital anomalies (renal dysplasia, posterior urethral valves, reflux nephropathy)
    • Glomerular disease (FSGS, chronic GN)
    • Hereditary (polycystic, Alport, cystinosis)
  • Estimate GFR: Schwartz formula = 36.5 × height (cm) ÷ creatinine (µmol/L)
  • Features: growth failure, anorexia, vomiting, anaemia, polyuria (concentrating defect) or oliguria, renal osteodystrophy (rickets), hypertension, acidosis, delayed puberty, school problems
  • Labs: ↑ urea, creatinine, ↑ PO₄, ↓ Ca, ↑ PTH, ↑ K, acidosis, normocytic anaemia
  • US: small kidneys (except polycystic)

Hypertension in children

  • BP ≥ 95th centile for age, sex and height on 3 occasions (≥ 130/80 in adolescents)
  • Use the right cuff (bladder covers 80–100% of the arm)
  • In children hypertension is usually secondary, mostly renal: GN, reflux nephropathy, CKD, renal artery stenosis
  • Also coarctation, phaeochromocytoma, CAH, Cushing, drugs (steroids)
  • Adolescents with obesity: primary (essential) hypertension
  • Hypertensive emergency (encephalopathy, heart failure): IV labetalol or hydralazine, reduce by ≤ 25% in the first 8 h
  • Long-term: amlodipine, ACE inhibitor (enalapril), β-blocker
