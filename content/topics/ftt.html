Failure to thrive (FTT) is inadequate physical growth in a young child: weight below the 3rd centile, or weight that crosses two major centile lines downwards. It is a sign, not a diagnosis. In Sudan the commonest cause is inadequate intake (poor feeding practice, poverty) often with recurrent infection (diarrhoea, TB, malaria).

Approach

History (the most useful tool)
  • Detailed feeding history: breast or formula, how the formula is prepared, amount, weaning foods
  • Vomiting, diarrhoea, stool type, cough, fever, urinary symptoms
  • Birth weight, gestation, previous growth on the card
  • Social: income, carer, mother's health, other children
Examination
  • Plot weight, length and OFC and look at the pattern
  • Signs of malnutrition (2.2), dysmorphism
  • Murmur, cyanosis, hepatomegaly, abdominal distension, wasted buttocks
  • Signs of neglect: dirty, bruises, withdrawn
  • Watch a feed

Investigations (guided by the history)

  • First line: CBC, urinalysis and urine culture (silent UTI), stool exam (giardia, ova, fat), RFT and electrolytes, LFT, blood glucose
  • Second line: TSH, coeliac serology (anti-tTG IgA), HIV test, Mantoux and CXR, sweat test, karyotype (girls: Turner), echocardiogram
  • The best diagnostic step in most cases is admission and a trial of adequate feeding: weight gain in hospital confirms inadequate intake at home

Management

  • Treat the cause · nutritional rehabilitation (about 150 kcal/kg/day or more for catch-up)
  • Feeding education for the mother: correct formula preparation, energy-dense weaning food (2.1)
  • Social support; suspected neglect → social worker
  • Follow up weight monthly on the growth chart

Short stature is height below the 3rd centile (−2 SD) for age and sex. Most short children are normal: familial short stature and constitutional delay. The height velocity separates normal from pathological: a normal-variant child grows at a normal speed.

Mid-parental height (target height)

  • Boys: (father's height + mother's height + 13) ÷ 2
  • Girls: (father's height + mother's height − 13) ÷ 2
  • The child's final height should lie within ± 8.5 cm of the target
  • Child far below the parents' centiles = pathological

Investigations

  • Bone age (left wrist X-ray): the key first test
  • CBC, ESR, RFT, electrolytes, bicarbonate, Ca/PO₄/ALP, LFT, urinalysis · TSH, free T4 · coeliac serology
  • Karyotype in every short girl without an obvious cause (Turner)
  • GH stimulation test (after normal screening) · MRI pituitary if GH low or headache, visual field defect (craniopharyngioma)
Exam trap

In malnutrition, weight falls before height (the child is thin); in endocrine short stature, height falls first and the child is plump. Tall stature in childhood: familial, obesity, Marfan, Klinefelter, precocious puberty (tall child, short adult).
