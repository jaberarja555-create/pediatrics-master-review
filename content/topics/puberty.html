Puberty is driven by the hypothalamic GnRH pulse generator, which stimulates LH and FSH and then the gonads. The first sign in girls is breast budding (thelarche, average 10–11 years) and in boys testicular enlargement ≥ 4 mL (average 11–12 years). Puberty that is too early or too late needs assessment.

Normal sequence and Tanner staging

Girls
  • Breast bud (B2) → pubic hair → growth spurt (early, peak at B2–B3) → menarche (≈ 2–2.5 yr after thelarche, B4; average 12–13 yr)
Boys
  • Testes ≥ 4 mL (G2, Prader orchidometer) → penile growth, pubic hair → growth spurt (late, G4) → voice change, facial hair

Tanner stages 1–5 for breasts (B), genitalia (G) and pubic hair (P): 1 = prepubertal, 5 = adult. Pre-pubertal testes are 1–3 mL; adult 15–25 mL.

Precocious puberty

DefinitionSecondary sexual characteristics before 8 years in girls and before 9 years in boys
Central (GnRH-dependent, true)
  • Normal sequence, ↑ LH/FSH
  • Girls: usually idiopathic
  • Boys: often CNS lesion (hypothalamic hamartoma, tumour, hydrocephalus, post-meningitis) → MRI brain
  • Treatment: GnRH agonist (leuprorelin)
Peripheral (GnRH-independent)
  • Low LH/FSH
  • CAH (virilisation), adrenal or gonadal tumours, McCune–Albright (café-au-lait, bone dysplasia), exogenous sex steroids, severe hypothyroidism
Benign variants
  • Premature thelarche (girls 6 months–2 years, isolated breast, no growth spurt: observe)
  • Premature adrenarche (pubic hair, body odour, 6–8 yr)
Investigations
  • Bone age (advanced)
  • LH/FSH (± GnRH test), oestradiol/testosterone, 17-OHP, TSH
  • Pelvic US
  • MRI brain

Delayed puberty

  • No signs by 13 years in girls (or no menarche by 15) and 14 years in boys
  • Constitutional delay (commonest, especially boys, family history, delayed bone age)
  • Chronic disease (CKD, IBD, coeliac, SCD, malnutrition), hypothyroidism
  • Hypogonadotropic (↓ LH/FSH): Kallmann (anosmia), pituitary tumour, anorexia
  • Hypergonadotropic (↑ LH/FSH: gonadal failure): Turner (girls), Klinefelter (boys), chemotherapy
  • Investigate: bone age, LH/FSH, karyotype, TSH · treat cause or short course of sex steroids

Growth hormone deficiency

  • Congenital: midline defects (septo-optic dysplasia, cleft palate), genetic
  • Neonates: hypoglycaemia, micropenis, prolonged jaundice
  • Acquired: craniopharyngioma, cranial irradiation, head injury, infection
  • Features: short but proportionate, chubby ("cherubic" face, central fat), slow height velocity, delayed bone age, high-pitched voice, normal intelligence
  • Investigations (after excluding commoner causes 1.3): IGF-1, IGF-BP3 (low), GH stimulation tests (insulin, glucagon, clonidine, arginine: peak GH low on 2 tests), MRI pituitary, other pituitary hormones
  • Treatment: daily recombinant GH injections until growth stops · replace other deficient hormones
  • GH also used in Turner, Prader–Willi, CKD, SGA without catch-up

Craniopharyngioma

  • Commonest supratentorial tumour of childhood (from Rathke's pouch) · benign but locally invasive
  • Headache, vomiting (raised ICP), bitemporal hemianopia, short stature (GH), diabetes insipidus, delayed puberty, obesity
  • X-ray/CT: suprasellar calcification, cystic mass · MRI
  • Surgery ± radiotherapy · lifelong hormone replacement

Diabetes insipidus (DI)

Types
  • Central (↓ ADH: craniopharyngioma, head injury, meningitis, Langerhans cell histiocytosis)
  • Nephrogenic (kidney does not respond: X-linked, lithium, hypokalaemia, hypercalcaemia)
Features
  • Polyuria, polydipsia (drinks even dirty water, at night), dehydration, hypernatraemia, FTT
  • Urine dilute (osmolality < 300) with high plasma osmolality
Tests
  • Water deprivation test then desmopressin: urine concentrates after DDAVP = central
  • No response = nephrogenic
TreatmentCentral: desmopressin (nasal or oral) · nephrogenic: free water, low-salt diet, thiazide, NSAID

Polyuria and polydipsia differential: diabetes mellitus (glucose), DI, psychogenic polydipsia (urine concentrates on water deprivation), CKD, hypercalcaemia, hypokalaemia.
