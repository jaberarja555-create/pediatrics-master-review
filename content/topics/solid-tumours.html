After leukaemia, the commonest childhood cancers are brain tumours, lymphomas, neuroblastoma, Wilms tumour and retinoblastoma. In Africa, Burkitt lymphoma (linked to EBV and malaria) is common. Most present as a mass; early referral improves cure.

Retinoblastoma

  • Malignant tumour of the retina, < 3 years · RB1 gene (chromosome 13q14): hereditary (40%, bilateral, earlier, later osteosarcoma) or sporadic (unilateral)
  • Leukocoria (white pupillary reflex, "cat's eye") · squint · red painful eye, proptosis (late)
  • Diagnosis: examination under anaesthesia, US/MRI (calcification) · no biopsy
  • Treatment: chemotherapy, laser, cryotherapy, enucleation for advanced disease · screen siblings and children of survivors

Brain tumours

  • Commonest solid tumours of childhood · most are infratentorial (posterior fossa): medulloblastoma, cerebellar astrocytoma, brainstem glioma, ependymoma
  • Raised ICP: morning headache and vomiting, papilloedema, VI nerve palsy, enlarging head in infants
  • Cerebellar signs: ataxia, nystagmus · brainstem: cranial nerve palsies · craniopharyngioma: short stature, visual field defect, diabetes insipidus, calcified suprasellar mass
  • MRI · surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy · dexamethasone for oedema

Exam trap

Night pain in a bone that wakes the child, or "growing pains" in one leg only, need an X-ray. A child with a white pupil in a photograph needs urgent eye referral. Never biopsy a suspected Wilms tumour or retinoblastoma (seeding).
