The cardiovascular station is usually a child with a murmur, cyanosis or heart failure. After the general examination, the examiner expects the pulses (radial and femoral together), the precordium in order (inspection, palpation, auscultation) and the signs of heart failure, then a diagnosis of the lesion from the murmur and its associated signs.

Steps of the examination

GeneralDysmorphism (Down, Turner, Williams), breathlessness, sweating, cyanosis, pallor, growth
HandsClubbing, splinter haemorrhages and Osler's nodes (endocarditis), capillary refill
Pulse
  • Rate, rhythm, volume and character: collapsing (PDA), weak (aortic stenosis, shock)
  • Radial and femoral together: delay or weak femorals = coarctation
  • Offer the BP in the right arm and a leg
Face and neckConjunctival pallor, tongue for central cyanosis, JVP (older child)
InspectionChest bulge (enlarged heart), scars (midline sternotomy, left lateral thoracotomy: PDA ligation, coarctation, shunt), visible pulsations
PalpationApex beat: site and character (heaving: LV; displaced: cardiomegaly) · parasternal heave (RV) · thrills · palpable P2 (pulmonary hypertension)
AuscultationFour areas with diaphragm and bell; heart sounds (S2 splitting); murmur: timing, site of maximum intensity, grade, radiation; back for coarctation
Heart failureTender enlarged liver (the most useful sign in infants), basal crackles, oedema (late in infants)

Describing a murmur

  • Timing: systolic (with the pulse), diastolic or continuous
  • Site of maximum intensity and radiation (axilla, neck, back)
  • Character: pansystolic, ejection, machinery
  • Change with posture or breathing
GradeMeaning
1–2Soft, heard with care
3Loud, no thrill
4Loud with a thrill
5–6Heard with the stethoscope partly or fully off the chest

Presenting the findings

Model presentation

Hiba is a 5-month-old girl with poor feeding and sweating on feeds. She is underweight and breathless (RR 60/min). The pulses are normal in volume with no radio-femoral delay. The apex is displaced and there is a thrill at the lower left sternal edge with a grade 4/6 pansystolic murmur and a loud P2. The liver is 4 cm below the costal margin. These findings suggest a large VSD with heart failure and pulmonary hypertension; I would request an echocardiogram, chest X-ray and ECG, and start furosemide and captopril.

Coarctation of the aorta
Radio-femoral delay+high BP in the arms+murmur between the scapulae=Coarctation: echo

Try it · exam-style
Why are the radial and femoral pulses felt together?
ANSWERTo detect radio-femoral delay or weak femorals of coarctation of the aorta.
Give three features of an innocent murmur.
ANSWERSoft and systolic with no thrill, varies with posture, in a well child with normal heart sounds.
