Nerve injuries

Erb palsy (C5–C6)The commonest · arm adducted, internally rotated, elbow extended, forearm pronated, wrist flexed: "waiter's tip" · Moro absent on that side, grasp present · may have phrenic palsy (C4)
Klumpke palsy (C8–T1)Rare · claw hand, weak small hand muscles · grasp absent, Moro present · Horner syndrome (ptosis, small pupil) if T1 is injured
ManagementGentle passive physiotherapy from 1 week (after swelling settles) to prevent contractures · most recover by 3 months · refer for nerve surgery if no recovery of biceps by 3–6 months
Facial palsyPressure from forceps or the sacrum · the eye does not close, flat nasolabial fold, the mouth pulls to the normal side when crying · artificial tears, recovers in weeks
Phrenic nerve (C3–C5)Respiratory distress · raised hemidiaphragm on X-ray (paradoxical movement on ultrasound)

Fractures and other injuries

  • Clavicle: the commonest fracture (shoulder dystocia) · baby does not move the arm, Moro absent on that side, crepitus, a lump (callus) at 1 week · heals alone: gentle handling or pin the sleeve to the vest for 1–2 weeks
  • Humerus or femur fracture (breech) · skull fracture, intracranial haemorrhage (subdural, subarachnoid) after difficult delivery
  • Sternomastoid tumour: firm lump in the sternomastoid at 2–3 weeks → torticollis (head tilts to the same side) · stretching physiotherapy

Developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH)

  • Unstable or dislocated hip from a shallow acetabulum · left more often
  • Risk factors: female (6 : 1), breech, first baby, family history, oligohydramnios, tight swaddling with legs straight
  • Newborn tests: Barlow (pushes a dislocatable hip out, backwards) · Ortolani (brings a dislocated hip back in with a "clunk")
  • Later signs: limited abduction, asymmetrical thigh creases, short leg (Galeazzi sign: one knee lower), limp or waddling gait when walking
  • Ultrasound of the hip up to 4–6 months (screen babies at risk at 6 weeks) · X-ray after 4–6 months
  • Treatment: Pavlik harness (hips flexed and abducted) under 6 months · closed reduction and hip spica at 6–18 months · open surgery later

Congenital talipes equinovarus (club foot)

  • About 1 in 1000 · boys 2 : 1 · half bilateral · may go with spina bifida, DDH, arthrogryposis
  • Foot points down (equinus) and inwards (varus), sole faces inwards · cannot be corrected to neutral
  • Positional talipes (from intrauterine position) corrects fully with gentle pressure: physiotherapy only
  • Fixed club foot: Ponseti method from the first weeks: weekly plaster casts, small Achilles tenotomy, then a foot-abduction brace to 4 years
Exam trap
  • A hip click without movement of the head is usually a harmless ligament click; the abnormal sign is a clunk (Ortolani).
  • Flexible flat feet, positional talipes and mild tibial torsion resolve on their own; fixed club foot does not.
  • Hip X-ray is unreliable before 4–6 months (the femoral head is not yet ossified): use ultrasound.
