The newborn's glucose falls after the cord is cut and rises again as feeding starts. Hypoglycaemia is the commonest metabolic problem in newborns and, if prolonged or symptomatic, causes permanent brain injury (occipital lobes). Babies at risk are screened before they show signs.

Definition

  • Blood glucose < 2.6 mmol/L (45 mg/dL): the operational threshold for treatment in any newborn
  • < 2.0 mmol/L (36 mg/dL) with symptoms needs IV dextrose
  • Target after treatment: ≥ 2.6 mmol/L (≥ 3.3 after 48 h)
  • Strip results must be confirmed by a laboratory sample, but do not delay treatment

Babies at risk (screen before feeds, for 24–48 h)

MechanismBabies
↓ StoresPreterm, SGA / IUGR, LBW, the smaller twin

Clinical features

  • Often none (hence screening)
  • Jitteriness, tremor, irritability, high-pitched cry
  • Poor feeding, lethargy, hypotonia, hypothermia
  • Apnoea, cyanosis, tachypnoea, sweating
  • Seizures, coma (late, dangerous)

Management

Asymptomatic, glucose 2.0–2.6Feed at once (breastfeed or expressed milk/formula 10 mL/kg by cup or NG) · recheck in 30–60 min
Symptomatic or < 2.0, or still low after a feed10% dextrose 2 mL/kg IV over 5 min, then an infusion of 10% dextrose at 60–80 mL/kg/day (GIR 4–6 mg/kg/min) · recheck in 30 min
No IV accessBuccal dextrose gel 40% 0.5 mL/kg, or 10% dextrose by NG tube
Persistent (needs GIR > 8–10)Hyperinsulinism likely: raise the infusion concentration (central line) · glucagon 0.1–0.2 mg/kg IM/IV · hydrocortisone · diazoxide · send insulin, cortisol, ketones at the time of hypoglycaemia

Glucose infusion rate (GIR, mg/kg/min) = % dextrose × mL/kg/day ÷ 144. Never give 25% or 50% dextrose boluses to a newborn (rebound hypoglycaemia, vein damage).

Care of the IDM

Early feeding (within 1 hour) and glucose checks before feeds for 24 h · watch for respiratory distress, jaundice, plethora · check Ca if jittery · examine for anomalies and murmurs · echo if a murmur or distress.
