Poisoning in young children (1–5 years) is usually accidental: they swallow medicines, kerosene or chemicals stored in drink bottles. In adolescents it is often deliberate. Most children recover with supportive care; a few poisons need a specific antidote. In Sudan the common ones are kerosene, organophosphate insecticides, caustics, iron, paracetamol and hair dye (paraphenylenediamine).

General management

  1. ABCD and glucose · treat seizures · identify the poison: what, how much (mg/kg), when · bring the container
  2. Decontamination: activated charcoal 1 g/kg (max 50 g) within 1 hour for most drugs · not for hydrocarbons (kerosene), caustics, alcohols, iron, lithium, or with a poor airway
  3. Do not induce vomiting (aspiration, caustic re-exposure) · skin: remove clothes, wash · eyes: irrigate
  4. Antidote where available · enhanced elimination (urine alkalinisation for salicylates, dialysis) · observe 6–24 h
  5. Prevention: safe storage, child-resistant containers, never store kerosene in drink bottles · assess for neglect or self-harm
