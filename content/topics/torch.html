Congenital infections are passed from mother to fetus across the placenta (or at birth). The group is remembered as TORCH: Toxoplasma, Other (syphilis, varicella, parvovirus B19, HIV, Zika), Rubella, CMV and Herpes. Many share IUGR, microcephaly, hepatosplenomegaly, jaundice, thrombocytopenia (petechiae, "blueberry muffin" rash), eye disease and deafness; a few features point to the organism.

Investigations

  • Baby IgM (specific; IgG may be maternal) · PCR (CMV in urine/saliva within 3 weeks)
  • Syphilis: VDRL/RPR (baby titre ≥ 4× mother's), TPHA, long-bone X-ray
  • Cranial ultrasound / CT (calcifications), eye examination, hearing test, echo
  • CBC (platelets), LFT, bilirubin

Neonatal tetanus

  • Clostridium tetani spores enter the umbilical stump (cut with an unclean instrument, dung or ash applied) in a baby of an unvaccinated mother
  • Day 3–14 (usually day 7): a baby who sucked well stops sucking (trismus), then spasms to touch and noise, opisthotonus, clenched fists, risus sardonicus, fully conscious
  • Mortality 50–90%; worse if onset < 7 days
  • Treatment: quiet dark room, minimal handling · human tetanus immunoglobulin 500 IU IM (or antitoxin) · benzylpenicillin 100 000 U/kg/day or metronidazole 7.5 mg/kg 8-hourly × 10 days · diazepam 0.1–0.2 mg/kg IV for spasms (± phenobarbitone) · clean the cord · NG feeds of breast milk
  • Prevention: Td to all women of childbearing age, clean delivery, clean cord care
