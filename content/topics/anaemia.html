Anaemia is a haemoglobin below the normal range for age. It is very common in Sudanese children because of iron deficiency, malaria, hookworm, sickle cell disease and malnutrition. The MCV (red cell size) and the reticulocyte count (marrow response) divide the causes and guide the tests.

Physiological anaemia of infancy: after birth, high O₂ switches off erythropoietin and fetal RBCs are short-lived, so Hb falls to its lowest at 8–12 weeks (earlier and deeper in preterm babies). IMCI: palmar pallor = some pallor (iron, deworm), severe palmar pallor = refer.

Clues from the history and examination

  • Diet: prolonged milk only, tea with meals, pica (eating soil)
  • Blood loss: worms, menorrhagia, epistaxis
  • Ethnicity and family history (sickle cell, thalassaemia), drugs or fava beans (G6PD)
  • Jaundice + splenomegaly = haemolysis
  • Bleeding, bruising, fever, nodes, bone pain = leukaemia, aplasia
  • Koilonychia, angular stomatitis, glossitis (iron) · frontal bossing (thalassaemia)

Causes of iron deficiency

  • Poor intake: prolonged milk feeding, late or poor weaning, cow's milk before 1 year, tea
  • ↑ Need: prematurity and LBW (low stores), rapid growth (infancy, adolescence)
  • Blood loss: hookworm, schistosomiasis, cow's milk enteropathy, Meckel's, menstruation, epistaxis
  • Malabsorption: coeliac disease, giardia, H. pylori

Clinical features

  • Pallor, fatigue, irritability, poor appetite, pica, breath-holding spells
  • Poor concentration and developmental delay (may not fully reverse if < 2 years)
  • Koilonychia, angular stomatitis, glossitis
  • Severe: tachycardia, flow murmur, heart failure
  • ↑ Febrile seizures, reduced immunity

Film: microcytic hypochromic cells, pencil cells, target cells. Mentzer index (MCV ÷ RBC count) > 13 favours iron deficiency, < 13 thalassaemia trait.

Treatment

  • Oral iron: elemental iron 3–6 mg/kg/day (ferrous sulphate) in 1–2 doses between meals, with juice (vitamin C), not with milk or tea
  • Response: reticulocytes rise in 5–10 days, Hb rises ≈ 1–2 g/dL per month · continue for 3 months after Hb is normal to refill stores
  • Treat the cause: deworm (albendazole), diet counselling (meat, liver, beans, green leaves), limit milk to 500 mL/day, stop tea with meals
  • Failure to respond: poor adherence (commonest), continued loss, wrong diagnosis (thalassaemia), malabsorption (coeliac)
  • Transfusion only for heart failure or Hb < 4 g/dL with distress · side effects of iron: black stool, constipation, epigastric pain, teeth staining · iron poisoning if overdosed (13.3)
  • Prevention: iron supplements for preterm/LBW infants, iron-rich weaning foods, deworming
