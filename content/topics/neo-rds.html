Respiratory distress is one of the commonest reasons for neonatal admission. The signs are tachypnoea ≥ 60/min, grunting, nasal flaring, chest (subcostal) retraction and cyanosis. The cause depends mainly on gestation and the timing: RDS in the preterm, TTN after caesarean section, meconium aspiration in the post-term, and pneumonia or sepsis at any age.

Causes

Pulmonary
  • Transient tachypnoea (TTN): commonest overall
  • RDS (hyaline membrane disease)
  • Meconium aspiration · pneumonia
  • Pneumothorax · congenital diaphragmatic hernia
Non-pulmonary
  • Sepsis, hypothermia, hypoglycaemia, acidosis
  • Cyanotic heart disease, heart failure
  • Birth asphyxia (HIE) · polycythaemia
  • Airway: choanal atresia (cyanosis relieved by crying), oesophageal atresia

Respiratory distress syndrome (RDS)

  • Cause: surfactant deficiency (produced by type II pneumocytes from ~ 24 weeks, enough after 34–35 weeks) → alveoli collapse at the end of expiration
  • Risk: prematurity (most important), infant of a diabetic mother (insulin delays surfactant), male, caesarean without labour, asphyxia, second twin
  • Reduced by antenatal steroids and labour
  • Onset within 4–6 hours of birth, worsens over 48–72 h, then improves
  • Grunting (keeps alveoli open), retraction, cyanosis
  • CXR: ground-glass (reticulogranular) lungs, air bronchograms, low volume ("white-out" if severe)
  • ABG: hypoxia, hypercapnia, acidosis

Management of RDS

  • Prevention: antenatal corticosteroids 24–34 weeks
  • Warmth, minimal handling, IV fluids (10% dextrose 60–80 mL/kg/day), monitor glucose
  • Oxygen to keep SpO₂ 90–95% (not higher: ROP) · bubble CPAP (5–7 cm H₂O) early: the key treatment in Sudanese nurseries
  • Exogenous surfactant via the endotracheal tube (INSURE, LISA) in moderate–severe RDS: the most effective treatment where available
  • Antibiotics (ampicillin + gentamicin) until sepsis is excluded: GBS pneumonia looks the same
  • Mechanical ventilation if CPAP fails (apnoea, rising CO₂, FiO₂ > 0.4–0.6)

Complications

  • Pneumothorax, air leak (sudden deterioration)
  • PDA · IVH
  • Bronchopulmonary dysplasia (O₂ need at 36 weeks corrected)
  • Retinopathy of prematurity (O₂ toxicity)

General supportive care of the baby in distress

  1. Airway and oxygen: nasal prongs (0.5–1 L/min) or head box; SpO₂ 90–95%; CPAP if distress persists
  2. Warmth (incubator or KMC when stable) · monitor temperature
  3. Fluids: IV 10% dextrose if RR > 70 or severe distress; NG feeds when RR settles · check glucose
  4. Antibiotics: ampicillin 50 mg/kg 12-hourly + gentamicin 5 mg/kg daily IV, stop at 48–72 h if cultures negative and CRP normal
  5. Investigations: CXR, CBC, CRP, blood culture, glucose, blood gas

Monitoring response

  • Improving: RR and grunting fall, less retraction, SpO₂ maintained with less O₂, tolerating feeds
  • Failing: apnoea, rising O₂ need (FiO₂ > 0.4 on CPAP), PaCO₂ > 60, acidosis → ventilation / refer to NICU
