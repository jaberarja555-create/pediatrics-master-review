Respiratory distress in a newborn means RR > 60/min with any of: grunting, nasal flaring, chest recession (subcostal, intercostal, sternal) or cyanosis. The cause is often outside the lungs.

Causes of respiratory distress in the newborn

LungsTTN (the commonest at term) · RDS (the commonest in preterm) · meconium aspiration · pneumonia · pneumothorax · pulmonary hypoplasia
AirwayBilateral choanal atresia · Pierre Robin sequence · oesophageal atresia with fistula (frothy saliva, NGT coils in the pouch)
SurgicalCongenital diaphragmatic hernia · congenital lobar emphysema
HeartCyanotic congenital heart disease · heart failure · PPHN
OthersSepsis · hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, metabolic acidosis · anaemia, polycythaemia · birth asphyxia, intracranial bleed

Respiratory distress syndrome (hyaline membrane disease)

  • Cause: surfactant deficiency (made by type II pneumocytes, mature at 35 weeks) → alveoli collapse at the end of expiration
  • Risk ↑: prematurity (the main one), infant of a diabetic mother, male, second twin, elective caesarean section, asphyxia, hypothermia
  • Risk ↓: antenatal steroids, prolonged rupture of membranes, IUGR, maternal hypertension (fetal stress ↑ cortisol)
  • Clinical: distress within 4 hours of birth, worsening over 48–72 h, then improving · grunting · reduced air entry
  • Chest X-ray: diffuse ground-glass (reticulogranular) lungs with air bronchograms, small lung volume · "white-out" if severe
  • Sepsis looks the same: take blood culture and start antibiotics
Respiratory supportCPAP (6–8 cm H2O) from birth is the first choice · ventilation if CPAP fails · target SpO2 90–94%
SurfactantNatural surfactant (poractant 200 mg/kg) by tube or thin catheter (LISA) when oxygen need rises above 30% on CPAP · may repeat
SupportiveThermal care, IV fluids and glucose, ampicillin + gentamicin until cultures are negative, caffeine in very preterm
ComplicationsPneumothorax, PDA, IVH, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, retinopathy (oxygen)

Pneumothorax

  • After ventilation or bag-and-mask, RDS or meconium aspiration · sudden deterioration, reduced air entry on one side, shift of the apex beat
  • Bright transillumination of the chest · X-ray: black lung field without markings, mediastinal shift
  • Tension pneumothorax: needle aspiration (2nd intercostal space, midclavicular line), then chest drain

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia

  • Bowel herniates into the chest, usually left posterolateral (Bochdalek) · pulmonary hypoplasia decides survival
  • Severe distress at birth, scaphoid abdomen, heart sounds on the right, bowel sounds in the chest · often seen on antenatal ultrasound
  • X-ray: bowel loops in the left chest, mediastinum pushed right
  • Intubate at once (no bag and mask) · NGT to decompress the stomach · stabilise, then surgical repair

Cyanosis

  • Central: blue tongue and lips · reduced Hb > 5 g/dL · always abnormal
  • Peripheral (acrocyanosis): blue hands and feet with a pink tongue · normal in the first day, or cold, shock
  • Causes of central cyanosis: lungs (RDS, MAS, pneumonia, pneumothorax, CDH) · cyanotic heart disease (TGA, TOF, pulmonary atresia, TAPVR) · PPHN · polycythaemia · methaemoglobinaemia
Hyperoxia testGive 100% oxygen for 10 minutes and measure right radial PaO2 · PaO2 rises above 150 mmHg → lung disease · stays below 100 mmHg → cyanotic heart disease (or severe PPHN) → echocardiography
Clues to heart diseaseCyanosis without much distress · murmur, weak femoral pulses · no rise in SpO2 with oxygen · baby gets worse as the duct closes (day 2–5)
Duct-dependent lesionProstaglandin E1 infusion 5–10 ng/kg/min (higher if the duct is closing) keeps the duct open · side effect: apnoea (be ready to ventilate) · refer to a cardiac centre
PPHNHigh pulmonary pressure after birth → right-to-left shunt · after MAS, asphyxia, sepsis, CDH · labile oxygen, pre-ductal SpO2 higher than post-ductal · ventilation, inhaled nitric oxide

Apnoea

  • Pause in breathing ≥ 20 seconds, or shorter with bradycardia or cyanosis
  • Apnoea of prematurity: immature respiratory centre, from day 2, a diagnosis of exclusion
  • Secondary: sepsis, meningitis, hypoglycaemia, hypocalcaemia, hypothermia, IVH, seizures, anaemia, reflux, maternal drugs
  • Gentle stimulation, clear the airway, bag-and-mask if not breathing
  • Look for and treat the cause (septic screen, glucose)
  • Caffeine citrate for apnoea of prematurity · CPAP or ventilation if recurrent
Exam trap
  • Apnoea in a term baby is never "apnoea of prematurity": look for sepsis, seizures or hypoglycaemia.
  • Acrocyanosis with a pink tongue on day 1 is normal; a blue tongue is not.
