The newborn examination is done in the first 72 hours to find congenital anomalies and problems that need early treatment (cataract, hip dysplasia, heart disease, undescended testes). It is done head to toe in a warm room, with the mother present, starting with what can be seen while the baby is quiet (colour, breathing, heart sounds).

Exam trap
  • Caput succedaneum is oedema that crosses suture lines and goes in days; a cephalhaematoma is blood under the periosteum, limited by a suture, and may cause jaundice.
  • An asymmetrical Moro reflex points to a brachial plexus injury or a fractured clavicle or humerus on the side that does not move.
  • A single umbilical artery is linked with renal and other anomalies.

The resuscitation station

The resuscitation station is done on a newborn manikin. Marks are given for preparation, the steps of the golden minute, correct bag-and-mask ventilation, and the decision at each heart-rate check. The full algorithm is in 3.2; the station follows Helping Babies Breathe and the 2025 neonatal resuscitation guidelines.

Steps of the station

  1. Prepare: warm area and radiant warmer, two dry towels, bag and masks (sizes 0 and 1), suction, oxygen, stethoscope, pulse oximeter, laryngoscope and tubes.
  2. At birth: dry the baby thoroughly, remove the wet towel, keep warm; assess breathing, tone and heart rate. A baby who cries and breathes well stays with the mother (routine care).
  3. Not breathing: position the head in the neutral position, clear the airway only if secretions block it, rub the back to stimulate.
  4. Still not breathing or gasping, or HR < 100/min, by 60 seconds: start bag-and-mask ventilation in air at 40–60 breaths/min; watch the chest rise.
  5. Check the heart rate after 30 seconds of effective ventilation (stethoscope at the apex or the umbilical cord pulse).
  6. HR < 60/min despite good ventilation: chest compressions with two thumbs on the lower third of the sternum, one third of the chest depth, 3 compressions to 1 breath (90 + 30 per minute), oxygen 100%.
  7. HR still < 60/min: adrenaline 1:10,000, 0.1–0.3 ml/kg IV via the umbilical vein, every 3–5 minutes; volume 0.9% saline 10 ml/kg if blood loss is suspected.
  8. HR > 100/min and breathing: stop ventilation, monitor, keep warm, start breastfeeding, explain to the mother.

Bag-and-mask technique

  • Mask covers the chin, mouth and nose, not the eyes; seal it with the thumb and index finger (C-grip) and lift the jaw into the mask with the other fingers
  • Squeeze to see a gentle chest rise; say "breathe, two, three" to keep the rate; the first sign of success is a rising heart rate
  • Chest not rising: reposition the head, re-apply the mask, suction, open the mouth, increase the pressure, then tube or laryngeal mask

Indications for endotracheal intubation

  • Bag-and-mask ventilation does not give a good chest rise.
  • Chest compressions are needed (better coordination of breaths).
  • To give surfactant, or adrenaline down the tube while IV access is being placed.
  • Suspected congenital diaphragmatic hernia (bag-and-mask ventilation inflates the bowel in the chest).
  • Extreme prematurity; suction of the trachea when meconium blocks the airway.

Procedure

  1. Baby supine, head in the neutral ("sniffing") position; give free-flow oxygen during the attempt.
  2. Hold the laryngoscope in the left hand, slide the straight blade over the right side of the tongue into the vallecula, and lift.
  3. See the cords; insert the tube with the right hand to the vocal-cord mark; light cricoid pressure may help.
  4. Limit each attempt to about 30 seconds; if it fails, go back to bag and mask.
  5. Confirm the position: chest rises equally, breath sounds on both sides, no air entry over the stomach, rising heart rate, CO₂ detector colour change; then fix the tube and note the depth.
Neonatal resuscitation guidelines 2025
  • Resuscitation is not started when survival is not possible: gestation below about 22–23 weeks, birth weight below about 400 g, anencephaly, confirmed trisomy 13 or 18.
  • After about 20 minutes of full resuscitation with no heart rate, the team considers stopping.
