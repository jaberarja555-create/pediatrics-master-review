Typhoid fever is caused by Salmonella Typhi (and Paratyphi A, B, C), spread by the faeco-oral route from cases and carriers through food and water. Incubation about 2 weeks. Increasing drug resistance has changed treatment: azithromycin and ceftriaxone are now the main drugs (WHO).

Clinical features

Week 1Fever rising step-wise to 39–40 °C, headache, malaise, abdominal pain, dry cough · diarrhoea (young children) or constipation (older)
Week 2Sustained high fever, toxic, apathetic ("typhoid state") · coated tongue · relative bradycardia · tender soft splenomegaly, hepatomegaly · rose spots on the trunk (hard to see on dark skin)
Week 3 complicationsIntestinal perforation and bleeding (ileal Peyer patches) · encephalopathy · myocarditis · hepatitis · osteomyelitis (sickle cell disease) · relapse · chronic carrier (gallbladder)
InfantsNon-specific fever, vomiting, diarrhoea, like gastroenteritis or sepsis

Investigations

  • Blood culture: the test of choice in the first week (positive in 40–60%) · bone marrow culture is the most sensitive (positive even after antibiotics) · stool and urine culture from the second week
  • CBC: leucopenia (relative lymphocytosis), anaemia, low platelets (severity)
  • Widal test: poor value in endemic areas (false positives and negatives); never diagnose typhoid on Widal alone

Treatment (WHO)

UncomplicatedOral azithromycin 20 mg/kg once daily (max 1 g) for 7 days · cefixime where sensitive
Severe or complicatedCeftriaxone 80 mg/kg/day IV (max 4 g) for 10–14 days · meropenem for extensively drug-resistant typhoid · dexamethasone for typhoid encephalopathy or shock
PerforationResuscitation, broad-spectrum antibiotics (add metronidazole), urgent surgery
PreventionSafe water, sanitation, hand washing, food hygiene · typhoid conjugate vaccine (TCV), one dose from 6 months

Try it · exam-style
Why is the Widal test unreliable in Sudan?
ANSWERPast infection, vaccination and cross-reacting antibodies give false positives; early disease gives false negatives.
Name two serious complications of typhoid.
ANSWERIntestinal perforation and bleeding (also encephalopathy, myocarditis).

Brucellosis

  • Brucella melitensis (goats, sheep, camels) and abortus (cattle) · caught by drinking raw (unboiled) milk, fresh cheese, or contact with animals
  • Fever (often undulant, sweats at night), malaise, joint pain, back pain · hepatosplenomegaly, lymph nodes
  • Complications: arthritis (hip, knee; sacroiliitis), spondylitis, osteomyelitis, orchitis, endocarditis (main cause of death), neurobrucellosis
  • A common cause of prolonged fever in children from herding families
DiagnosisBlood culture (slow; tell the laboratory) · serology: standard agglutination test titre ≥ 1:160, Rose Bengal screen · CBC: leucopenia, anaemia
Treatment under 8 yearsRifampicin 15–20 mg/kg/day + cotrimoxazole (TMP 10 mg/kg/day in 2 doses) for 6 weeks
Treatment 8 years and overDoxycycline 4 mg/kg/day (max 200 mg) + rifampicin for 6 weeks
ComplicatedAdd gentamicin 5 mg/kg/day IV for the first 7–14 days · spondylitis or endocarditis: 3 months or more
PreventionBoil or pasteurise milk · vaccinate animals
Exam trap
  • Brucellosis needs two drugs for 6 weeks; a single drug or a short course leads to relapse.
  • Doxycycline is avoided under 8 years (tooth staining); use rifampicin + cotrimoxazole.
