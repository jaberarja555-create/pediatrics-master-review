Stridor is a harsh, mainly inspiratory noise from narrowing of the larynx or upper trachea. A child's airway is narrow, so a little swelling causes a big rise in resistance. The key rule: do not upset a child with stridor (no throat examination, no needles, keep with the mother) until the airway is safe.

Causes of stridor

Acute
  • Croup (viral laryngotracheobronchitis): commonest
  • Epiglottitis (Hib)
  • Foreign body inhalation
  • Bacterial tracheitis · retropharyngeal abscess · diphtheria · anaphylaxis (angio-oedema)
Chronic (from birth / infancy)
  • Laryngomalacia: commonest congenital cause; floppy larynx, worse on feeding and lying supine, resolves by 2 years
  • Subglottic stenosis (after intubation), vocal cord palsy, haemangioma, vascular ring

Foreign body inhalation

  • Toddler 1–3 years · peanuts, seeds, beads · sudden choking while eating or playing
  • Larynx: stridor, aphonia · bronchus (usually right main bronchus): cough, unilateral wheeze, reduced air entry · later: recurrent pneumonia in one lobe
  • CXR (inspiratory and expiratory): hyperinflation on the affected side (air trapping), mediastinal shift away · most FBs are not radio-opaque
  • Rigid bronchoscopy: diagnosis and removal
  • Choking child (first aid): effective cough → encourage coughing · ineffective cough, conscious: 5 back blows then 5 chest thrusts (infant) or 5 abdominal thrusts (child > 1 yr), repeat · unconscious: open airway, rescue breaths, CPR
  • No blind finger sweeps

Acute tonsillitis and pharyngitis

  • Mostly viral (adenovirus, EBV) · Group A β-haemolytic Streptococcus (GABHS) in 15–30%, age 5–15 years
  • Bacterial clues: fever, tonsillar exudate, tender anterior cervical nodes, no cough (Centor criteria)
  • EBV (glandular fever): membrane, generalised nodes, splenomegaly · amoxicillin → rash
  • Treatment of GABHS: penicillin V 250 mg 12-hourly (≤ 12 years) or amoxicillin 50 mg/kg once daily × 10 days, or benzathine penicillin 600 000 U IM (< 30 kg; 1.2 MU ≥ 30 kg) once: prevents rheumatic fever
  • Penicillin allergy: azithromycin 5 days
ComplicationsSuppurative: peritonsillar abscess (quinsy): trismus, uvula deviated, "hot potato" voice → drainage · retropharyngeal abscess · otitis · non-suppurative: rheumatic fever, PSGN, scarlet fever
Tonsillectomy≥ 7 episodes in 1 year, 5/year for 2 years or 3/year for 3 years · obstructive sleep apnoea · quinsy twice

Acute otitis media (AOM)

  • Commonest at 6–24 months (short, horizontal Eustachian tube) · pneumococcus, H. influenzae, Moraxella, viruses
  • Fever, ear pain (pulling the ear, crying), irritability · red, bulging drum, lost light reflex · perforation → discharge, pain eases
  • Treatment: analgesia · amoxicillin 40–45 mg/kg 12-hourly × 5 days (IMCI) · co-amoxiclav if no response
  • Chronic suppurative OM (discharge > 14 days): dry mopping 3 times daily + quinolone ear drops
  • Complications: mastoiditis (ear pushed forward, swelling behind) → IV antibiotics, CT · meningitis, brain abscess · hearing loss (glue ear)

Sinusitis and the common cold

  • Common cold: viral, self-limiting 7–10 days · saline drops, fluids, paracetamol · no antibiotics, no cough syrups under 6 years
  • Acute sinusitis: purulent discharge > 10 days, or high fever with facial pain, or worsening after improvement · amoxicillin 10–14 days · orbital cellulitis (ethmoid sinusitis): emergency, IV antibiotics
