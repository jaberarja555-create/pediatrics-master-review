Infective endocarditis (IE) is infection of the heart valves or endocardium, forming vegetations of platelets, fibrin and organisms. It needs an abnormal heart (a jet of blood damaging the endocardium) plus bacteraemia. In children it mainly affects those with congenital heart disease (VSD, tetralogy, PDA, repaired CHD) or rheumatic heart disease. Untreated it is fatal.

Causes

Streptococcus viridansThe commonest (about 50%) · from the mouth: dental caries, tooth extraction · subacute course
Staphylococcus aureusAcute, destructive · after cardiac surgery, central lines, skin infection, IV drug use · S. epidermidis on prosthetic material
OthersEnterococci (after bowel or urinary procedures) · HACEK Gram-negative organisms (culture-negative) · fungi (newborns with long lines, after heart surgery)

Clinical features

  • Prolonged fever (the commonest sign), malaise, poor appetite, weight loss, pallor (anaemia)
  • New murmur or change in an old murmur · heart failure (valve destruction)
  • Splenomegaly · clubbing (late)
  • Vascular and immune signs: splinter haemorrhages (nails) · petechiae · Osler nodes (tender, finger pads) · Janeway lesions (painless, palms and soles) · Roth spots (retina) · microscopic haematuria (glomerulonephritis)
  • Emboli: stroke, brain abscess, mycotic aneurysm, lung abscesses (right-sided IE: VSD, lines), kidney and spleen infarcts

Investigations and diagnosis

Blood culturesThe key test: 3 sets from separate venepunctures before antibiotics, adequate volume · positive in about 90%
EchocardiographyShows vegetations, abscess, new regurgitation (transoesophageal if transthoracic is unclear)
OthersCBC (anaemia, leucocytosis), high ESR and CRP, urine (haematuria, protein), creatinine · rheumatoid factor may be positive
Duke criteriaMajor: typical organism in 2 blood cultures · vegetation or abscess on echo (or new valve regurgitation) · minor: predisposing heart disease, fever ≥ 38 °C, vascular signs (emboli, Janeway), immune signs (Osler, Roth, GN), positive culture not meeting major · definite IE = 2 major, or 1 major + 3 minor, or 5 minor

Treatment

  • Admit · cultures first, then IV bactericidal antibiotics for 4–6 weeks · empirical: benzylpenicillin (or ampicillin) + gentamicin; add cloxacillin if S. aureus likely · vancomycin + gentamicin if prosthetic material or MRSA · adjust to culture · fungal: amphotericin B
  • Treat heart failure and anaemia · surgery for uncontrolled heart failure (valve destruction), abscess, large vegetation with emboli, fungal IE, persistent infection
AHA 2021 · prevention of infective endocarditis
  • Good dental hygiene and regular dental care for every child with heart disease: this prevents more IE than antibiotics.
  • Antibiotic prophylaxis before dental procedures (gum or tooth-root work) only for the highest risk: prosthetic valve or valve repair material, previous IE, unrepaired cyanotic CHD (including shunts), CHD repaired with prosthetic material (first 6 months, or with a residual leak).
  • Dose: amoxicillin 50 mg/kg orally (max 2 g) once, 30–60 minutes before · penicillin allergy: azithromycin or doxycycline (clindamycin no longer advised).
  • Not needed for VSD, ASD, PDA, mitral valve prolapse alone, or for bowel and urinary procedures.

Myocarditis

CausesViruses (commonest: Coxsackie B, adenovirus, parvovirus B19, influenza, COVID-19) · diphtheria toxin (4.9) · rheumatic fever · Kawasaki disease · drugs (anthracyclines)
FeaturesAfter a viral illness: tachycardia out of proportion to fever, breathlessness, poor feeding, chest pain · heart failure or shock · soft heart sounds, gallop, no significant murmur · arrhythmias, sudden death
InvestigationsECG: tachycardia, low voltage, ST–T changes, arrhythmia · X-ray: cardiomegaly · echo: poor contraction, dilated ventricle · troponin raised · viral PCR
TreatmentAdmit, rest, monitor ECG · heart-failure treatment (diuretic, ACE inhibitor; inotropes in ICU) · treat arrhythmias · IV immunoglobulin in some · most recover; some develop dilated cardiomyopathy

Pericarditis

  • Causes: viral, purulent (S. aureus, after pneumonia), TB, rheumatic fever, uraemia, lupus · chest pain relieved by sitting forward, friction rub
  • Effusion: muffled heart sounds, big globular heart on X-ray, low-voltage ECG · tamponade: tachycardia, low BP, raised JVP, pulsus paradoxus → urgent pericardiocentesis · purulent pericarditis: drainage + IV antibiotics
Exam trap
  • Myocarditis gives heart failure with no significant murmur and soft heart sounds; a loud murmur points to structural or valve disease.
  • Digoxin is contraindicated in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.
