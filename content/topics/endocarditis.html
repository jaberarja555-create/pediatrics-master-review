Infective endocarditis (IE) is infection of the endocardium, usually on an abnormal valve or around a defect, where turbulent flow damages the lining and bacteria from a bacteraemia (teeth, skin, lines) settle in a vegetation. In Sudanese children it mostly complicates rheumatic heart disease and unrepaired CHD (VSD, TOF, PDA).

Organisms

  • Streptococcus viridans (from teeth): subacute, commonest
  • Staphylococcus aureus: acute, destructive, also on normal valves (lines)
  • Enterococcus, CoNS (prosthetic valves), HACEK, fungi (neonates, lines)

Clinical features

  • Prolonged fever, malaise, weight loss, night sweats
  • New or changing murmur, heart failure
  • Splenomegaly, anaemia, clubbing (late)
  • Embolic / immune: splinter haemorrhages, Osler nodes (tender, finger pulps), Janeway lesions (painless, palms), Roth spots (retina), petechiae, haematuria (glomerulonephritis), stroke, brain abscess

Diagnosis: modified Duke criteria

MajorMinor
  • Positive blood cultures: typical organism in 2 separate cultures (or persistently positive)
  • Echo evidence: vegetation, abscess, new regurgitation
  • Predisposing heart condition
  • Fever ≥ 38 °C
  • Vascular phenomena (emboli, Janeway)
  • Immunological phenomena (Osler, Roth, GN, rheumatoid factor)
  • Positive culture not meeting the major criterion

Definite IE: 2 major, or 1 major + 3 minor, or 5 minor. Take 3 blood cultures from different sites over 24 h before antibiotics. Also CBC, ESR/CRP (↑), urinalysis (haematuria), echo (transthoracic; TOE if unclear).

Treatment and prevention

  • Empirical: benzylpenicillin (or ampicillin) + gentamicin, add cloxacillin / vancomycin if Staph suspected
  • Streptococcal IE: penicillin 4 weeks (+ gentamicin 2 weeks) · Staph: 6 weeks
  • Surgery: severe valve failure, abscess, large vegetations with emboli, fungal IE
  • Good dental hygiene is the most important prevention
  • Antibiotic prophylaxis before dental procedures for high-risk patients only: prosthetic valve, previous IE, unrepaired cyanotic CHD, repaired CHD with prosthetic material (first 6 months), RHD in endemic areas: amoxicillin 50 mg/kg orally 1 h before

Myocarditis

  • Inflammation of the myocardium, mostly viral (coxsackie B, adenovirus, parvovirus) · also diphtheria, rheumatic fever, Kawasaki, drugs
  • A flu-like illness followed by heart failure, tachycardia out of proportion to fever, gallop, arrhythmia, shock, sudden death
  • ECG: low voltage, ST/T changes, arrhythmia · troponin ↑ · CXR: cardiomegaly · echo: dilated poorly contracting ventricle
  • Treatment: supportive (heart failure drugs, inotropes), rest, IVIG in some · many recover; some develop dilated cardiomyopathy

Pericarditis and pericardial effusion

  • Causes: viral, purulent (Staph, pneumococcus: very ill), TB (common in Sudan, constrictive later), rheumatic fever, JIA/SLE, uraemia
  • Pain relieved by sitting forward, pericardial rub · effusion: muffled heart sounds, raised JVP
  • Tamponade: Beck's triad (hypotension, raised JVP, muffled sounds), pulsus paradoxus → urgent pericardiocentesis
  • ECG: widespread saddle ST elevation, low voltage · CXR: flask-shaped heart · echo confirms
  • Purulent: drainage + IV antibiotics · TB: anti-TB drugs + prednisolone
