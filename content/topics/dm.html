Type 1 diabetes is autoimmune destruction of the pancreatic β-cells, causing absolute insulin deficiency. It is the commonest form in children (peaks at 5–7 years and puberty). In Sudan many children first present in diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) because early symptoms are missed. Treatment is lifelong insulin.

Clinical features

  • Polyuria, new bedwetting (secondary enuresis), polydipsia, weight loss, tiredness, polyphagia
  • Oral or genital candida, skin infections
  • Young children: vomiting, dehydration, FTT, nappy rash
  • DKA (25–40% at diagnosis): vomiting, abdominal pain, Kussmaul breathing, ketotic (acetone) breath, dehydration, drowsiness
  • Do not mistake DKA for gastroenteritis, pneumonia or acute abdomen

Diagnosis (any one, with symptoms)

Random plasma glucose≥ 11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL) with symptoms
Fasting plasma glucose≥ 7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL)
HbA1c≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) · target on treatment < 7%
Supporting
  • Glycosuria, ketonuria
  • Autoantibodies (GAD, IA-2, ZnT8)
  • Low C-peptide

Insulin treatment

  • Total daily dose ≈ 0.7–1 unit/kg/day (pre-pubertal)
  • Up to 1.2–1.5 at puberty
  • Honeymoon phase: less
  • Basal-bolus (preferred): long-acting (glargine/detemir or NPH at night) ≈ 40–50% + rapid/regular before meals
  • Twice-daily premixed (30/70): ⅔ in the morning, ⅓ in the evening, each ⅔ intermediate + ⅓ short-acting (common in Sudan)
  • Injection sites: abdomen, thighs, arms · rotate (lipohypertrophy)
  • Monitor blood glucose 4+ times a day, ketones when unwell · HbA1c every 3 months
  • Targets: fasting 4–7 mmol/L, after meals 5–10 mmol/L

Hypoglycaemia

  • Glucose < 3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL): sweating, shaking, hunger, pallor, irritability, confusion, seizures, coma
  • Causes: missed meal, exercise, insulin excess
  • Conscious: 15 g fast sugar (juice, glucose tablets), recheck in 15 min, then a snack
  • Unconscious: glucagon 0.5 mg (< 25 kg) / 1 mg IM or 10% dextrose 2 mL/kg IV

Definition and severity

  • Hyperglycaemia > 11 mmol/L
  • Venous pH < 7.3 or bicarbonate < 15 mmol/L
  • Ketonaemia (β-hydroxybutyrate ≥ 3) or ketonuria
  • Severity:
    • Mild pH < 7.3
    • Moderate < 7.2
    • Severe < 7.1 (bicarbonate < 5)
  • Precipitants: new diabetes, missed insulin, infection, stress
  • Assess: GCS, dehydration (assume 5% mild-moderate, 10% severe), shock, weight

Initial investigations

Glucose, blood gas, Na, K, urea, creatinine, ketones, CBC, Ca/PO₄, HbA1c · infection screen (cultures, urine, CXR) if indicated · corrected Na = Na + 0.3 × (glucose − 5.5) (mmol/L).

Management (ISPAD / BSPED)

  1. ABC, O₂ · shock only: 0.9% saline 10 mL/kg over 15–30 min (repeat if needed) · non-shocked: 10 mL/kg over 30–60 min
  2. Fluids: deficit (5–10%) + maintenance, replaced evenly over 48 h with 0.9% saline (+ KCl) · avoid big boluses (cerebral oedema)
  3. Insulin: start 1–2 hours after fluids: regular insulin 0.05–0.1 U/kg/h IV infusion · no insulin bolus
  4. Potassium: add KCl 40 mmol/L from the start once K < 5.5 and passing urine (total body K is low; insulin drives K into cells)
  5. Glucose: aim fall 2–5 mmol/L/h · when < 14–17 mmol/L add 5% dextrose (10% if needed); keep insulin running until ketoacidosis resolves
  6. Monitor: glucose hourly · gas, K, Na every 2–4 h · neuro observations hourly · fluid balance
  7. No bicarbonate (except life-threatening hyperkalaemia) · stop IV insulin 30–60 min after the first SC dose, when pH > 7.3, eating
Cerebral oedema: the main cause of death in DKA

4–12 h after starting treatment: headache, falling heart rate, rising BP, confusion, irritability, incontinence, seizures, unequal pupils. Risk: young age, new diabetes, severe acidosis, large fluid boluses, bicarbonate, rapid fall in glucose or failure of Na to rise. Treat at once: mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg IV over 10–15 min or 3% saline 2.5–5 mL/kg, head up, reduce fluids, then CT.
