Liver disease in Sudanese children is mostly infective: hepatitis A (very common, usually mild), hepatitis B (vertical and household spread, chronic), and hepatitis E (outbreaks, dangerous in pregnancy). Schistosomiasis causes portal hypertension with a normal liver (4.10). Chronic liver disease leads to cirrhosis, portal hypertension and liver failure.

Acute hepatitis: features and management

  • Prodrome: fever, malaise, anorexia, nausea, RUQ pain
  • Icteric phase: jaundice, dark urine, pale stool, tender hepatomegaly; the child feels better as jaundice appears
  • Labs: ALT/AST very high (> 10× normal), bilirubin ↑ · PT/INR = best marker of severity
  • Supportive: rest, fluids, normal diet, avoid paracetamol excess and hepatotoxic drugs · isolate (hepatitis A: 1 week after jaundice; hand washing)
  • Admit if vomiting, drowsiness, bleeding, INR > 1.5, hypoglycaemia: acute liver failure

Causes of chronic liver disease in children

  • Chronic hepatitis B (and C) · autoimmune hepatitis
  • Biliary atresia (late), choledochal cyst
  • Wilson disease (> 5 years: liver + neuro + Kayser–Fleischer rings, low caeruloplasmin)
  • Metabolic: galactosaemia, glycogen storage, α1-antitrypsin deficiency, cystic fibrosis
  • Drugs, Budd–Chiari · schistosomiasis (fibrosis with normal function)

Features of chronic liver disease

  • Jaundice, FTT, muscle wasting, clubbing, palmar erythema, spider naevi
  • Firm liver (or small, shrunken), splenomegaly, ascites, prominent abdominal veins
  • Bruising (↓ clotting factors), anaemia
  • Fat-soluble vitamin deficiency (A, D, E, K) in cholestasis · pruritus

Acute variceal bleeding

ABC, two IV lines, cross-match, transfuse to Hb 7–8 g/dL (avoid over-transfusion) · octreotide infusion · vitamin K, FFP, platelets · antibiotics (ceftriaxone) · endoscopic band ligation / sclerotherapy · prevention: propranolol, repeated banding.

Acute liver failure and hepatic encephalopathy

  • Causes: viral hepatitis (A, B, E), paracetamol overdose, other drugs (valproate, isoniazid), Wilson disease, metabolic
  • Encephalopathy grades: confusion → drowsiness → stupor → coma · hypoglycaemia, bleeding, infection, renal failure, cerebral oedema
  • Management: ICU care, glucose infusion (10% dextrose), vitamin K, FFP for bleeding, lactulose, avoid sedatives, restrict protein moderately, antibiotics, N-acetylcysteine (paracetamol), refer for transplant
