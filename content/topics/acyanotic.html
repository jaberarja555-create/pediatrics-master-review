In left-to-right shunts, oxygenated blood returns to the lungs, so the child is pink but the lungs are flooded. Large shunts cause heart failure, recurrent chest infections and FTT; if untreated, pulmonary vascular disease develops and the shunt reverses (Eisenmenger syndrome: cyanosis, inoperable).

Ventricular septal defect (VSD)

  • Commonest CHD (30%) · mostly perimembranous or muscular
  • Small (maladie de Roger): symptom-free, loud pansystolic murmur at the lower left sternal edge (LLSE), thrill · loud murmur = small hole · 30–50% close spontaneously
  • Large: heart failure at 4–8 weeks, FTT, chest infections, quieter murmur, mid-diastolic rumble at apex (↑ mitral flow), loud P2 (pulmonary hypertension)
  • CXR: cardiomegaly, plethoric lungs · ECG: biventricular hypertrophy · echo confirms
  • Treatment: small: observe, dental hygiene (endocarditis) · large: treat heart failure (furosemide, captopril, high-energy feeds), surgical closure at 3–6 months before pulmonary hypertension becomes fixed

Atrial septal defect (ASD)

  • Secundum (commonest, central) · primum (part of AVSD, Down)
  • Usually symptom-free in childhood · found by a murmur
  • Wide fixed splitting of S2 (the key sign) · ejection systolic murmur at the upper left sternal edge (pulmonary flow, not the hole) · tricuspid diastolic flow murmur if large
  • ECG: RBBB pattern, right axis deviation (secundum) · left axis (primum)
  • Complications in adults: arrhythmia (AF), heart failure, paradoxical embolism, pulmonary hypertension
  • Treatment: closure (device at cardiac catheter or surgery) at 3–5 years if large

Atrioventricular septal defect (AVSD)

  • Defect of the endocardial cushions: primum ASD + inlet VSD + a common AV valve · 40% of children with Down syndrome have CHD, and AVSD is the commonest
  • Large shunt → early heart failure and early pulmonary hypertension · ECG: superior (left) axis
  • All Down syndrome newborns need an echocardiogram · surgical repair at 3–6 months

Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA)

  • Common in preterm babies and after congenital rubella, high altitude, girls
  • Continuous "machinery" murmur below the left clavicle · bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure · heart failure if large
  • Preterm: worsens RDS, apnoea, NEC
  • Preterm: ibuprofen 10 mg/kg then 5 mg/kg at 24 and 48 h (or indomethacin, paracetamol) to close it · fluid restriction
  • Term: does not close with drugs → device closure (catheter) or ligation, to prevent endocarditis and heart failure

Coarctation of the aorta

  • Narrowing of the aorta, usually near the ductus (juxtaductal) · associations: Turner syndrome, bicuspid aortic valve, berry aneurysms
  • Neonate (critical): collapse and shock when the duct closes (day 2–7), absent femoral pulses · give PGE₁
  • Older child: hypertension in the arms, weak, delayed femoral pulses (radio-femoral delay), BP in legs lower than arms · ejection murmur at the back between the scapulae
  • CXR (older): rib notching (collaterals), "3 sign" · echo, CT/MRI
  • Treatment: surgery (resection, end-to-end anastomosis) or balloon angioplasty and stent
  • Long-term: hypertension, re-coarctation, aneurysm
