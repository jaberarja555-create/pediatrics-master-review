The neurological station is usually the lower limbs of a child with cerebral palsy, a neural tube defect or a neuromuscular disease. The examination decides two things: whether the lesion is an upper or a lower motor neurone lesion, and its level. A young child is examined largely by observation of posture, play and walking.

Steps of the examination

Higher functionsConsciousness, speech, intelligence and cooperation, orientation (older child)
Cranial nervesFacial asymmetry, squint, pupils, eye movements; vision (follows a toy) and hearing (turns to a rattle)
InspectionPosture (scissoring, frog-leg), deformity (equinus, contracture, pes cavus), wasting, fasciculation, scars on the back
ToneRoll the leg, flex the knee quickly, ankle clonus; spastic (clasp-knife), rigid, or floppy
PowerWatch the child move against gravity and resistance; grade 0–5 in the older child
ReflexesKnee (L3–L4), ankle (S1), plantar response (extensor is normal until walking)
Sensation and coordinationOlder child only: light touch by dermatome; heel–shin test
Gait and backGait (scissoring, toe walking, waddling, ataxic); Gowers' sign; the spine for a sinus, tuft of hair, swelling or scar of a neural tube defect

Definition

Cerebral palsy is a non-progressive disorder of movement and posture caused by injury to the developing brain. The motor picture may change as the child grows, but the brain lesion does not progress. About half of the children also have learning difficulty, and about a third have epilepsy.

Types

Spastic (about 70%)
  • Diplegia: legs more than arms; preterm babies (periventricular leukomalacia); scissoring, toe walking, commando crawl
  • Hemiplegia: one side; early hand preference, arm flexed, circumduction gait
  • Quadriplegia: all four limbs; severe asphyxia; epilepsy and learning difficulty common
DyskineticInvoluntary movements (athetosis, dystonia); kernicterus or severe asphyxia; intelligence often normal
AtaxicPoor balance, intention tremor; cerebellar malformation
HypotonicFloppy infant, often becomes spastic or dyskinetic later

History and examination points

  • Causes: prenatal about 80% (infection, malformation), perinatal about 10% (asphyxia, prematurity), postnatal about 10% (meningitis, kernicterus, head injury)
  • Ask: cried at birth, nursery admission, jaundice, neonatal fits, meningitis
  • Early signs: delayed motor milestones, persistent fisting, early hand preference, persistent primitive reflexes, feeding difficulty
  • Examine: posture, tone, reflexes, contractures, gait
  • Associated problems: squint, vision and hearing loss, epilepsy, learning difficulty, drooling, malnutrition, constipation, hip dislocation
  • No regression: loss of skills means another diagnosis
Management
  • Multidisciplinary team: paediatrician, physiotherapy (the mainstay), occupational and speech therapy, orthopaedics, dietitian, teacher.
  • Spasticity: baclofen or diazepam orally; botulinum toxin injection into spastic muscles; orthoses; tendon release; selective dorsal rhizotomy in selected cases.
  • Treat epilepsy, feeding problems and constipation; check hearing and vision; support the family.

Health checks in Down syndrome

At diagnosisEchocardiography for every baby (heart defects may be silent), thyroid function, red reflex (cataract), hearing test, CBC
Every yearThyroid function, growth on Down syndrome charts, vision and hearing
Watch forAtlanto-axial instability (neck pain, change in gait or bladder control), sleep apnoea, recurrent chest infections, leukaemia, coeliac disease
DevelopmentEarly stimulation, physiotherapy and speech therapy; school with support

Presenting a neurological case

Model presentation

Sara is a 4-year-old girl born at 30 weeks who walks on her toes. On examination she has increased tone in both legs, brisk knee and ankle reflexes, sustained ankle clonus and extensor plantar responses; her arms are nearly normal and she scissors when held upright. These are signs of an upper motor neurone lesion of both lower limbs, most likely spastic diplegic cerebral palsy from periventricular leukomalacia. I would like to plot her growth, test her vision and hearing, and request an MRI of the brain.
