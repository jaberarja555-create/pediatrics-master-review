Most children with frequent infections are normal: young children have 6–10 viral infections a year, especially in crowded homes and nurseries. In Sudan, the commonest causes of secondary immunodeficiency are malnutrition and HIV. Primary (inherited) immunodeficiency is rare but must be suspected when infections are serious, persistent, unusual or recurrent.

When to suspect immunodeficiency (warning signs)

  • ≥ 2 serious infections (pneumonia, meningitis, sepsis, osteomyelitis) in a year
  • Infections with unusual organisms (PCP, fungi, BCG dissemination)
  • Infections that fail to clear with antibiotics, or need IV antibiotics
  • Failure to thrive, chronic diarrhoea
  • Persistent oral thrush after 1 year
  • Family history, consanguinity, early infant deaths
  • Delayed separation of the umbilical cord (> 4 weeks)

First-line investigations

  • CBC with differential (lymphopenia in SCID, neutropenia) and film (Howell–Jolly)
  • HIV test (always) · nutritional assessment
  • Immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM) · vaccine antibody responses
  • Lymphocyte subsets (CD4, CD8, CD19), complement (CH50)
  • NBT / DHR (CGD) · sweat test (CF) when recurrent chest infections

Treatment: prophylactic antibiotics (cotrimoxazole), IVIG replacement for antibody defects, bone marrow transplant for SCID and CGD, avoid live vaccines in severe T-cell defects.

Atopy

  • Tendency to IgE-mediated allergy: eczema, asthma, allergic rhinitis, food allergy (the "atopic march")
  • Common food allergens: cow's milk, egg, peanut, tree nuts, sesame, fish, wheat
  • Tests: skin-prick test, specific IgE · food challenge (gold standard)
  • Management: avoidance, antihistamines, adrenaline auto-injector for serious reactions

Anaphylaxis

  • Severe, life-threatening systemic allergic reaction
  • Triggers: foods, drugs (penicillin), insect stings, vaccines (rare), latex
  • Sudden onset (minutes) of Airway swelling (stridor, hoarse voice), Breathing (wheeze, hypoxia) and/or Circulation (shock), usually with skin signs (urticaria, angio-oedema)
Treatment
  • Call for help, remove trigger, lie flat (sit if breathless)
  • IM adrenaline 1:1000 into the anterolateral thigh: 0.01 mL/kg (10 µg/kg), max 0.5 mL
  • Or: 0.15 mL (< 6 yr), 0.3 mL (6–12 yr), 0.5 mL (> 12 yr)
  • Repeat every 5 min
  • Oxygen, IV fluid 20 mL/kg for shock
  • Then: chlorphenamine, hydrocortisone (4 mg/kg), salbutamol for wheeze
  • Observe ≥ 6–12 h (biphasic reaction)

Urticaria and angio-oedema

  • Acute urticaria: itchy wheals, usually viral infection, foods, drugs · antihistamines (cetirizine)
  • Angio-oedema without urticaria, recurrent, family history: hereditary angio-oedema (C1-inhibitor deficiency): does not respond to adrenaline well → C1-inhibitor concentrate
