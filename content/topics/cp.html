Cerebral palsy (CP) is a group of permanent disorders of movement and posture caused by a non-progressive injury to the developing brain (before, during or after birth up to about 2 years). The brain lesion does not progress, but the clinical picture changes as the child grows. It is common in Sudan because of birth asphyxia, kernicterus and neonatal infections.

Causes

TimingCauses
Antenatal (≈ 80%)Brain malformations, congenital infections (TORCH), vascular events, genetic, maternal problems
Perinatal
  • Birth asphyxia / HIE
  • Prematurity (IVH, PVL)
  • Kernicterus
  • Neonatal hypoglycaemia
Postnatal (< 2 yr)Meningitis, encephalitis, cerebral malaria, head injury, near-drowning, stroke (sickle cell)

Types

TypeLesionFeatures
Spastic (≈ 80%)Upper motor neurone (pyramidal)
  • ↑ Tone (clasp-knife), brisk reflexes, upgoing plantar, clonus, contractures
  • Hemiplegia (arm > leg; early hand preference)
  • Diplegia (legs > arms; preterm PVL; scissoring)
  • Quadriplegia (all four; HIE; worst)
DyskineticBasal ganglia
  • Choreoathetosis, dystonia, variable tone
  • Kernicterus, severe HIE
  • Intelligence often preserved
AtaxicCerebellumHypotonia, wide-based gait, intention tremor · genetic
Mixed—Spastic + dyskinetic

Presentation and associated problems

  • Delayed motor milestones, abnormal tone (floppy then stiff), persistent primitive reflexes, early hand preference, feeding difficulty, abnormal gait (toe walking)
  • Diagnosis is clinical; MRI shows the lesion
  • Epilepsy (≈ 30–40%)
  • Intellectual disability
  • Squint, visual and hearing loss
  • Speech problems
  • Feeding difficulty, GORD, aspiration pneumonia, malnutrition
  • Drooling
  • Constipation
  • Contractures, hip dislocation, scoliosis

Management (multidisciplinary)

  • Physiotherapy (posture, prevent contractures), occupational and speech therapy, orthoses (ankle-foot), walking aids
  • Spasticity: baclofen, diazepam, botulinum toxin injections · orthopaedic surgery (tendon release)
  • Treat epilepsy, reflux, constipation
  • Nutrition (gastrostomy)
  • Vision and hearing aids
  • Special education, family support
