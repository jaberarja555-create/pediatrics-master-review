The EPI diseases (diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, polio) are now uncommon where coverage is high, but outbreaks still occur in Sudan when vaccination falls, especially in displaced and conflict-affected communities. Each has a typical clinical clue that the examiner expects.

Diphtheria

  • Corynebacterium diphtheriae (toxin) · droplet spread · incubation 2–5 days
  • Tonsillopharyngeal: sore throat, low fever, grey adherent pseudomembrane that bleeds when removed, extending beyond the tonsils
  • Bull neck (cervical nodes and oedema) · laryngeal form: stridor, airway obstruction
  • Toxin complications: myocarditis (week 1–2: arrhythmia, heart failure, death) · neuritis (week 2–6: palatal palsy, nasal speech, ocular and limb palsy)
  • Treatment: isolate, diphtheria antitoxin (DAT) at once (20 000–100 000 units by severity) + penicillin or erythromycin 12.5 mg/kg 6-hourly × 14 days · airway care · contacts: erythromycin + vaccine

Pertussis (whooping cough)

CauseBordetella pertussis · very infectious · incubation 7–10 days
Catarrhal (1–2 wk)Coryza, mild cough: most infectious stage
Paroxysmal (2–6 wk)Paroxysms of coughing ending with an inspiratory whoop, vomiting after cough, red face, well between attacks · young infants: apnoea, cyanosis instead of whoop
ConvalescentCough fades over weeks–months ("100-day cough")
InvestigationsCBC: marked lymphocytosis (> 15 000) · PCR / culture of nasopharyngeal swab
ComplicationsPneumonia (commonest cause of death), apnoea, seizures, encephalopathy, subconjunctival haemorrhage, hernia, rectal prolapse, bronchiectasis, malnutrition
TreatmentAzithromycin 10 mg/kg day 1, then 5 mg/kg × 4 days (or erythromycin 14 days): shortens infectivity, not the cough · admit infants < 6 months (apnoea) · no cough suppressants · contacts: azithromycin

Tetanus in children

  • Clostridium tetani spores enter a wound (thorn, nail, ear piercing, otitis media, circumcision); toxin (tetanospasmin) blocks inhibitory neurones
  • Trismus first, risus sardonicus, neck and back rigidity (opisthotonus), reflex spasms to touch and noise, conscious throughout · autonomic instability
  • Neonatal tetanus: 3.9
  • Treatment: quiet dark room · HTIG 500 IU IM (some give 3000–6000 IU) · metronidazole 7.5 mg/kg 8-hourly × 7–10 days · wound debridement
  • Spasms: diazepam 0.1–0.2 mg/kg IV 4–6-hourly, magnesium, ventilation if severe · airway, NG feeding
  • Disease gives no immunity: vaccinate on recovery

Poliomyelitis

CausePoliovirus (enterovirus) 1, 2, 3 · faeco-oral · destroys anterior horn cells (lower motor neurone)
Forms90–95% inapparent · abortive (minor illness) · non-paralytic (aseptic meningitis) · paralytic < 1%
Paralytic polioFever, muscle pain, then acute asymmetric flaccid paralysis (legs, proximal), absent reflexes, sensation intact · bulbar form (swallowing, breathing) · injections and exercise worsen it
LateWasting, limb shortening, deformities · post-polio syndrome
DiagnosisTwo stool samples 24 h apart within 14 days of onset (virus culture) · CSF: lymphocytes
TreatmentSupportive: rest, analgesia, no IM injections, positioning, ventilation for respiratory failure, physiotherapy
Acute flaccid paralysis (AFP) surveillance

Every child < 15 years with sudden weakness of a limb (AFP) must be reported and have two stool samples sent, to prove polio eradication. Commonest cause of AFP today is Guillain–Barré syndrome (10.5). Polio vs GBS: polio is asymmetric, with fever, no sensory loss and a normal CSF protein early.
