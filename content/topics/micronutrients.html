Micronutrient deficiencies ("hidden hunger") are common in Sudanese children, especially with malnutrition, measles and chronic diarrhoea. The most important in public health are vitamin A, zinc, iodine and iron (iron: 8.1).

Vitamin A deficiency

  • Role: vision in dim light (rhodopsin), epithelial integrity, immunity
  • Risk: SAM, measles, chronic diarrhoea, poor diet · deficiency makes measles more severe and deadly
  • Extra-ocular: dry rough skin (follicular hyperkeratosis), ↑ infections, growth failure
WHO eye signs (xerophthalmia)
  • XN night blindness (earliest)
  • X1A conjunctival xerosis · X1B Bitot's spots (foamy grey patches)
  • X2 corneal xerosis · X3 keratomalacia (corneal ulcer, melting) → blindness
  • XS corneal scar

Excess vitamin A: raised ICP (bulging fontanelle, vomiting), liver damage, bone pain; teratogenic in pregnancy.

Zinc deficiency

  • Role: enzymes, growth, immunity, gut repair
  • Features: growth failure, diarrhoea, ↑ infections, perioral and perianal dermatitis, alopecia, poor wound healing, loss of taste
  • Acrodermatitis enteropathica: inherited zinc malabsorption: rash around the mouth, anus and limbs + diarrhoea + alopecia, starting at weaning
Zinc in diarrhoea (IMCI)

Every child with diarrhoea: zinc 10 mg/day (< 6 months) or 20 mg/day (≥ 6 months) for 10–14 days. It shortens the episode and prevents new ones for 2–3 months.

Iodine deficiency

  • Endemic in parts of Sudan (Darfur, Kordofan) · prevented by iodised salt
  • Goitre in children and mothers · reduced IQ, school failure
  • Severe deficiency in pregnancy → endemic cretinism: intellectual disability, deaf-mutism, spastic diplegia, short stature, squint
  • The commonest preventable cause of intellectual disability worldwide

Treatment doses

  • Thiamine 50–100 mg IV/IM in infantile beriberi with heart failure, then 10 mg/day orally
  • Niacin (nicotinamide) 50–300 mg/day orally
  • Pyridoxine-dependent seizures: 100 mg IV stops the fits (under EEG)
  • Folic acid 1–5 mg/day orally
  • Vitamin B12 hydroxocobalamin IM (1 mg alternate days, then monthly)
  • Vitamin C 100–300 mg/day orally: pain settles in days

Vitamin K deficiency

  • Haemorrhagic disease of the newborn (vitamin K deficiency bleeding): breast milk is low in vitamin K, the newborn gut is sterile, little crosses the placenta
  • Early (< 24 h): mother on phenytoin, warfarin, rifampicin · classic (day 2–7): GI, umbilical, circumcision bleeding · late (2–12 weeks): intracranial, cholestasis, malabsorption
  • Labs: ↑ PT (and PTT), normal platelets and fibrinogen
  • Prevention: vitamin K 1 mg IM at birth to every baby (0.5 mg if < 1.5 kg)
  • Treatment: vitamin K 1–2 mg IV + FFP 10–15 mL/kg if bleeding is serious
