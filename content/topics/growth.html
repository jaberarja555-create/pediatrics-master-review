Growth is the increase in the size and number of cells, measured as weight, length (height) and head circumference. Development is the gain of skills and maturation of function (1.2). Both go in parallel. Growth is the most sensitive single sign of a child's health: a child who is ill, underfed or neglected stops growing before any other sign appears.

Periods of childhood

NeonateBirth to 28 days
InfantBirth to 1 year · the period of most rapid growth and brain development
Toddler / preschool1–5 years · growth slows down
School age6–12 years · slow, steady growth
AdolescenceAbout 10–19 years · pubertal growth spurt, then fusion of the epiphyses ends growth

Factors affecting growth

  • Genetic: parents' heights (mid-parental height), race, sex
  • Hormonal: GH (childhood), thyroxine (infancy and brain), sex hormones (puberty), insulin (fetal)
  • Nutrition: the main factor in infancy
  • Chronic disease: CHD, CKD, malabsorption, chronic infection
  • Psychosocial: emotional deprivation, neglect
  • Intrauterine factors: IUGR, maternal smoking, infection

Weight

Birth weightTerm average 3–3.5 kg · LBW < 2.5 kg
First daysLoses up to 10% in the first 3–4 days (fluid shift), regains birth weight by day 10–14
Gain in infancyAbout 750 g/month for the first 4 months · 500 g/month next 4 months · 250 g/month last 4 months
Milestones of weightDoubles by 4–5 months · triples by 1 year (≈ 10 kg) · quadruples by 2–2½ years (≈ 12 kg)
1–6 yearsAbout 2 kg/year · weight (kg) = (age in years × 2) + 8
7–12 yearsAbout 2.5–3 kg/year · weight = (age × 7 − 5) ÷ 2

Length and height

  • Under 2 years: length lying down (infantometer); after 2 years: height standing (stadiometer)
  • Birth: 50 cm · 1 year: 75 cm (+25 cm in year 1) · 2 years: ≈ 87 cm
  • 4 years: 100 cm (double birth length) · 12 years: ≈ 150 cm (triple)
  • From 2 years to puberty: height (cm) = (age × 6) + 77 · gain about 5–7 cm/year
  • Growth below 4 cm/year after age 3 is abnormal: investigate
  • Pubertal spurt: girls ≈ 8 cm/year (earlier), boys ≈ 10 cm/year (later)

Head circumference (OFC)

  • Measured as the maximum occipito-frontal circumference with a non-stretch tape, above the eyebrows and over the occiput
  • Birth 35 cm · 6 months 43 cm · 1 year 47 cm · 2 years 49 cm · adult ≈ 55 cm
  • Gain in year 1 ≈ 12 cm: 2 cm/month for the first 3 months, then less
  • Microcephaly: OFC < 3rd centile (−2 SD) · congenital infection (TORCH, Zika), birth asphyxia, fetal alcohol, genetic (trisomy), familial
  • Macrocephaly: OFC > 97th centile · hydrocephalus, familial, chronic haemolytic anaemia (thalassaemia), rickets, achondroplasia, storage disease

Teeth and bone age

Teeth
  • First tooth (lower central incisor) 6–7 months; delayed if none by 13 months
  • All 20 primary teeth by 2½ years · number of teeth ≈ age in months − 6
  • First permanent tooth: first molar at 6 years
  • Late teeth: rickets, hypothyroidism, familial
Bone age
  • X-ray of the left hand and wrist (Greulich–Pyle); knee in infants
  • The best measure of biological maturity
  • Delayed: constitutional delay, hypothyroidism, GH deficiency, malnutrition, chronic disease
  • Advanced: precocious puberty, CAH, obesity

A growth chart plots a child's measurement against age, compared with a reference population. In Sudan the WHO Child Growth Standards are used (separate charts for boys and girls, on the Road-to-Health card). One measurement shows size; repeated measurements show growth (velocity), which is more important.

Reading a centile chart

NormalBetween the 3rd and 97th centiles (WHO: −2 to +2 SD) and follows its own centile line
FalteringA fall across two major centile lines, or a flat curve, even if still "within normal"
Small but normalBelow the 3rd centile but parallel to it, small parents, normal velocity: familial short stature
Catch-upPreterm babies: plot by corrected age until 2 years

Exam trap

A single reading below the 3rd centile is not enough to call growth abnormal; a child whose weight and height are both on the 10th centile is normal. What matters is the trend: a child crossing centile lines downwards is abnormal even above the 3rd centile.
