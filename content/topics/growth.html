Growth is the increase in body size (number and size of cells), measured by weight, length/height and head circumference. Development is the gain of function and skills. Both are checked at every visit and plotted on a growth chart.

Periods of childhood and phases of growth

NeonateBirth to 28 days
InfantBirth to 1 year: the fastest growth after birth
Toddler1–3 years
Preschool child3–6 years
School child6–12 years: slow, steady growth
Adolescent10–19 years (WHO): pubertal growth spurt
Phases of linear growth · fast in infancy, steady in childhood, a second peak at puberty (girls about 2 years earlier)

Factors affecting growth

GeneticParents' height (strongest normal factor) · race · sex · chromosomal disorders (Down, Turner)
PrenatalMaternal malnutrition, hypertension, smoking, TORCH infections, teratogens, placental insufficiency → IUGR · maternal diabetes → macrosomia
NutritionThe most important postnatal factor in Sudan: PEM, poor complementary feeding, micronutrient deficiency
Chronic diseaseCongenital heart disease, CKD, malabsorption (coeliac disease), chronic infection (TB, HIV), chronic anaemia
PsychosocialPoverty, neglect, emotional deprivation (psychosocial short stature)

Which hormone is the main driver of fetal growth?
ANSWERInsulin (with IGFs); GH has little effect before birth.
When is postnatal growth fastest?
ANSWERIn infancy (first year), then again at the pubertal growth spurt.

Weight

  • Birth weight: average 3–3.5 kg · LBW < 2.5 kg
  • Physiological weight loss: up to 10% in the first 3–4 days (fluid loss, meconium, little milk) · regained by 10–14 days
  • Gain in the first year: ¾ kg/month (months 1–4) → ½ kg/month (5–8) → ¼ kg/month (9–12)
  • Birth weight doubles at 4–5 months, triples at 1 year, × 4 at 2 years
  • After 2 years: about 2 kg/year until puberty

3–12 months(age in months + 9) ÷ 2
1–6 years(age in years × 2) + 8
7–12 years(age in years × 7 − 5) ÷ 2

Used to estimate weight for drug doses and fluids when the child cannot be weighed (emergency).

Exam trap
  • Birth weight doubles at 4–5 months and triples at 1 year.
  • So the statement "birth weight doubles by 1 year" is false.

Length and height

  • Birth length: 50 cm
  • First year: +25 cm → 75 cm at 1 year
  • Second year: +12 cm → about 87 cm at 2 years
  • Birth length doubles at 4 years (100 cm)
  • Then 5–7 cm/year until the pubertal spurt
AgeExpected height
2–12 years(age in years × 5) + 80 cm

Mid-parental (target) height: (father's + mother's height) ÷ 2, + 6.5 cm for a boy, − 6.5 cm for a girl · normal range ± 10 cm.

Head circumference (OFC)

  • Reflects brain growth · fastest in the first year
  • Measure with a non-stretch tape over the most prominent part of the occiput and just above the eyebrows · take the largest of 3 readings
  • Growth: 2 cm/month (0–3 months) → 1 cm/month (3–6 months) → 0.5 cm/month (6–12 months) = +12 cm in the first year
  • Only about +2 cm in the second year
  • Chest circumference (at the nipples / xiphoid, mid-respiration): head larger at birth, equal at about 1 year, chest larger after · head still larger after 1 year → malnutrition (or a large head)

  • First tooth: about 6 months · all 20 primary teeth by 2½–3 years
  • Number of teeth ≈ age in months − 6 (up to 2 years)
  • Permanent teeth (32): first is the first molar at 6 years · complete by 12–14 years except the third molars (17–21 years)
  • Delayed teething = no tooth by 13 months: idiopathic / familial (commonest) · hypothyroidism · rickets · hypopituitarism · hypoparathyroidism · Down syndrome
  • Natal teeth (present at birth): remove if loose (risk of aspiration)

Bone age

  • Measures skeletal maturity · after 1 year: X-ray of the left hand and wrist, compared with a standard atlas (Greulich–Pyle)
  • At birth: distal femur epiphysis on knee X-ray is present in a term baby · absent in congenital hypothyroidism
  • Carpal bones: first appears at about 2–3 months, then roughly one per year
  • Bone age shows how much growth is left: delayed bone age = more growth to come

Exam trap
  • Familial short stature: bone age equals the actual age; the parents are short.
  • Constitutional delay: bone age is delayed (equal to the height age); puberty is late; final height is normal.

Growth charts used in Sudan

  • WHO Child Growth Standards (birth–5 years) printed on the child health card (Road to Health card) · WHO references for 5–19 years
  • Separate charts for boys and girls · lines drawn as z-scores (SD) or centiles: −2 SD ≈ 3rd centile, 0 = 50th, +2 SD ≈ 97th centile
  • Special charts: preterm infants (or corrected age), Down syndrome, Turner syndrome, achondroplasia

Reading the chart

Weight-for-age · child A grows normally; child B shows growth faltering
  • Normal: between −2 and +2 SD and growing parallel to the lines (own channel)
  • One reading below −2 SD or above +2 SD needs assessment
  • Serial readings matter most: a flat curve (no gain) or falling weight is the earliest sign of malnutrition or illness
  • Crossing two major centile lines downward = growth faltering (failure to thrive)
  • A curve rising faster than the lines after illness or treatment = catch-up growth
  • Preterm: subtract the weeks born early (corrected age) until 2 years
Exam trap
  • A child who stays on the same low centile (for example the 10th) at every visit is growing normally.
  • What matters is whether the curve keeps its line, not one single reading.
