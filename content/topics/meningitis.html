Meningitis is inflammation of the meninges, most dangerously by bacteria. Sudan lies in the African meningitis belt, with epidemics of meningococcal disease in the dry season (December–June). Bacterial meningitis kills about 10% of children and leaves up to a third with deafness, epilepsy or disability, so antibiotics must start within the first hour.

Clinical features

Infants (non-specific)
  • Fever or hypothermia, poor feeding, vomiting, irritability, lethargy
  • Bulging fontanelle, high-pitched cry, convulsions, apnoea
  • Neck stiffness is often absent under 1 year
Older children
  • Fever, headache, vomiting, photophobia, confusion
  • Neck stiffness, Kernig's sign (pain on extending the knee with the hip flexed), Brudzinski's sign (neck flexion → hips flex)
  • Petechial / purpuric rash = meningococcus

Contraindications to LP (delay it, treat first)

  • Raised ICP: deep or falling coma (GCS < 9), focal signs, unequal pupils, papilloedema, bradycardia + hypertension, abnormal posturing
  • Shock, respiratory failure · prolonged seizures
  • Bleeding disorder, platelets < 50 000 · purpuric rash (meningococcaemia) · skin infection at the site

Treatment of bacterial meningitis

Empirical (> 1 month)Ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg/day IV (once daily or 50 mg/kg 12-hourly, max 4 g) · or cefotaxime 50 mg/kg 6-hourly
< 3 monthsAdd ampicillin (Listeria): cefotaxime + ampicillin 50 mg/kg 6-hourly
Epidemic meningococcalSingle dose ceftriaxone 100 mg/kg IM (max 4 g) in epidemic settings (WHO)
DurationMeningococcus 5–7 days · Hib 7–10 days · pneumococcus 10–14 days · Gram-negative / neonatal 14–21 days
Dexamethasone0.15 mg/kg IV 6-hourly × 2–4 days, first dose before or with the antibiotic · reduces deafness (Hib, pneumococcus) · not in neonates or meningococcal disease alone
ChemoprophylaxisClose contacts of meningococcus: rifampicin 10 mg/kg 12-hourly × 2 days or ciprofloxacin single dose · Hib contacts: rifampicin 4 days

Supportive care

  • ABC · treat shock (saline 20 mL/kg) · oxygen
  • Fluids: normal maintenance with isotonic fluid (restrict only if SIADH or raised ICP)
  • Convulsions: diazepam then phenobarbitone
  • Raised ICP: head up 30°, avoid hypotonic fluid, mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg or 3% saline
  • Monitor GCS, pupils, BP, Na, urine output, OFC (infants)
  • Improving: fever settles by 48–72 h, alert, feeding

Encephalitis

  • Inflammation of the brain parenchyma: fever + altered consciousness or behaviour, seizures, focal signs
  • Causes: HSV (temporal lobe), measles (acute or SSPE), mumps, enteroviruses, arboviruses, rabies
  • CSF: lymphocytes, normal glucose, mildly ↑ protein · HSV PCR · MRI · EEG
  • Start aciclovir 10–20 mg/kg IV 8-hourly × 14–21 days in every suspected encephalitis
