In the fetus the placenta, not the lung, gives oxygen. Three shunts carry blood past the liver and the airless lungs: the ductus venosus, the foramen ovale and the ductus arteriosus. At birth the first breaths open the lungs, the shunts close, and the two circulations separate. Many heart diseases of the newborn appear when these shunts close.

Causes and associations of congenital heart disease

  • About 8 per 1000 live births · most are multifactorial
  • Maternal: rubella (PDA, pulmonary artery stenosis) · diabetes (TGA, VSD, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy) · lupus (complete heart block) · alcohol (VSD) · lithium (Ebstein anomaly) · warfarin, anticonvulsants
  • Down syndrome: AVSD, VSD · Turner: coarctation, bicuspid aortic valve · Noonan: pulmonary stenosis · Williams: supravalvular aortic stenosis · DiGeorge (22q11): tetralogy, truncus, interrupted arch · Marfan: aortic root dilatation, mitral prolapse

Try it · exam-style
Why is a large VSD silent at birth?
ANSWERPulmonary resistance is still high, so little blood crosses the defect; the shunt and murmur appear as resistance falls over weeks.

Key points in the cardiac examination

GeneralGrowth (heart failure → poor weight), dysmorphic features, central cyanosis (tongue, lips) vs peripheral (hands, feet only), clubbing, respiratory rate, sweating, liver size
PulsesRight arm and femoral pulse together: weak or delayed femoral = coarctation · collapsing (bounding) pulse = PDA, aortic regurgitation · BP in arm and leg (leg is normally higher)
Apex beat4th space just outside the midclavicular line under 2 years · 5th space in the midclavicular line after 5 years · heaving apex = LV pressure load (AS) · hyperdynamic = volume load (VSD, PDA, mitral regurgitation) · left parasternal heave = RVH
Heart soundsS2 splits on inspiration normally · wide fixed splitting = ASD · single S2 = tetralogy, TGA, pulmonary atresia · loud P2 = pulmonary hypertension · S3 can be normal in children; S4 is always abnormal · a thrill is a palpable murmur (grade ≥ 4) and is never innocent

Innocent compared with pathological murmurs

Innocent (functional)Pathological
TimingSystolic (except venous hum), shortDiastolic or pansystolic, or continuous (PDA)
LoudnessSoft, grade ≤ 2, no thrillLoud, grade ≥ 3, may have a thrill
Other signsNormal heart sounds, child well, changes with posture, louder with fever or anaemiaAbnormal S2, click, symptoms (poor feeding, breathlessness), cyanosis, abnormal pulses, radiates widely (to the back)
  • Still's murmur: musical, vibratory, mid-systolic at the left lower sternal edge, age 3–7 years, softer on sitting
  • Venous hum: continuous, under the clavicles, disappears on lying flat or pressing the neck veins
  • Pulmonary flow murmur: soft ejection murmur at the upper left sternal edge (also in newborns: peripheral pulmonary stenosis)
  • Grades: 1 very soft · 2 soft · 3 loud, no thrill · 4 with thrill · 5 heard with the edge of the stethoscope · 6 heard without touching the chest
  • Site of maximum loudness: lower left sternal edge = VSD · upper left sternal edge = PS, ASD flow, tetralogy · left infraclavicular = PDA (continuous) · apex = mitral regurgitation (to the axilla) · upper right sternal edge = AS (to the neck)
Best next step · murmur in a well child
  • A soft systolic murmur with normal heart sounds, normal pulses and a well, growing child is innocent: reassure; any red flag (diastolic, thrill, symptoms, abnormal S2, newborn) → echocardiography.

Give four features that make a murmur pathological.
ANSWERDiastolic timing, a thrill (grade ≥ 4), abnormal heart sounds, and symptoms such as poor feeding or cyanosis.

Normal flow, obstructionCoarctation, aortic stenosis, pulmonary stenosis · pressure load, hypertrophy—
↓ pulmonary blood flow (oligaemic lungs)—Right-to-left shunts: tetralogy of Fallot, pulmonary atresia, tricuspid atresia, Ebstein · cyanosis, spells, heart failure rare
  • Commonest CHD: VSD (about 30%), then PDA, ASD, PS, coarctation · commonest cyanotic CHD: tetralogy of Fallot · commonest cyanotic CHD in the newborn: TGA
  • Eisenmenger syndrome: a long-standing left-to-right shunt (large VSD, PDA, AVSD) → pulmonary vascular disease → shunt reverses (right-to-left) → late cyanosis, clubbing · prevented by repair before pulmonary hypertension becomes fixed

Investigations

Pulse oximetryNewborn screening (after 24 hours): SpO2 < 95% or a difference > 3% between right hand and foot → echocardiography
Chest X-rayHeart size (cardiothoracic ratio > 0.5, > 0.6 in infants) · lung fields: plethoric or oligaemic · shapes: boot-shaped (tetralogy), egg on its side (TGA), snowman (supracardiac TAPVR), rib notching (coarctation, older child)
ECGRight axis and RVH are normal in newborns · LVH (VSD, PDA, AS) · RVH (ASD, PS, tetralogy) · superior (left) axis = AVSD · arrhythmias
EchocardiographyThe diagnostic test: anatomy, shunt direction, pressures, function · catheterisation for intervention
Hyperoxia test100% oxygen for 10 minutes: PaO2 stays < 150 mmHg in cyanotic heart disease, rises in lung disease

General complications of CHD

  • Heart failure (7.4) · poor growth · recurrent chest infections · pulmonary hypertension and Eisenmenger syndrome · infective endocarditis (7.6) · arrhythmias
  • Chronic cyanosis: polycythaemia (with relative iron deficiency), stroke, brain abscess, clubbing, gum and bleeding problems
Exam trap
  • Cyanotic infants are often not distressed in proportion to their cyanosis (tetralogy); breathless infants with plethoric lungs usually have a left-to-right shunt.
  • Brain abscess and stroke are complications of cyanotic (right-to-left) heart disease, not of VSD.
