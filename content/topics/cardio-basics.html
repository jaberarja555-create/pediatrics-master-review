The fetus gets oxygen from the placenta, not the lungs. Three shunts send oxygenated blood past the liver and the high-resistance lungs to the brain and body. At birth, the first breaths and cord clamping reverse the pressures, and the shunts close. Failure of this transition explains PDA, PPHN and the timing of presentation of many heart defects.

Changes at birth

  • Rising O₂ and falling prostaglandin close the ductus · preterm and hypoxic babies keep it open (PDA)
  • Pulmonary resistance keeps falling over 6–8 weeks: a VSD murmur and heart failure often appear only at 4–8 weeks
  • Duct-dependent lesions collapse when the duct closes (day 2–7): pulmonary atresia, critical PS (cyanosis) · HLHS, critical coarctation (shock)
  • Keep the duct open: prostaglandin E₁ (alprostadil) infusion · close it: ibuprofen / indomethacin / paracetamol

Innocent murmurs: Still's murmur (vibratory, musical, LLSE, 3–6 yr) · pulmonary flow murmur · venous hum (continuous, below the clavicle, disappears lying down or with neck pressure). About 40% of children have one at some time.

Congenital heart disease (CHD): overview

  • Incidence 8 per 1000 live births · commonest: VSD (30%), then ASD, PDA, PS, TOF, coarctation, TGA
  • Causes: mostly multifactorial · chromosomal (Down: AVSD; Turner: coarctation; DiGeorge: TOF, truncus) · maternal: rubella (PDA), diabetes (TGA, VSD), alcohol, lupus (heart block), drugs (lithium: Ebstein)
