Bleeding in a child comes from a problem with platelets (number or function), clotting factors, or blood vessels. The pattern of bleeding points to the defect: platelet problems cause skin and mucosal bleeding (petechiae, epistaxis); factor deficiencies cause deep bleeding (joints, muscles) and delayed bleeding after surgery.

Immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP)

  • Autoantibodies destroy platelets in the spleen · 2–10 years, 1–4 weeks after a viral infection or MMR vaccine
  • A well child with sudden petechiae, purpura, bruises, epistaxis, gum bleeding
  • No pallor (unless bleeding), no hepatosplenomegaly or lymph nodes
  • CBC: isolated thrombocytopenia (often < 20 000), normal Hb and WBC · film: large platelets, no blasts
  • Bone marrow only if atypical (pallor, organomegaly, bone pain, abnormal other cells) or before steroids: normal/↑ megakaryocytes
ManagementObservation for most (skin bleeding only), even with low counts · avoid contact sports, IM injections, aspirin and NSAIDs
Treat ifMucosal bleeding that persists, or severe bleeding: prednisolone 2 mg/kg/day × 7–14 days (or 4 mg/kg × 4 days) · IVIG 0.8–1 g/kg once (fastest rise)
Life-threatening bleedIntracranial (< 1%): platelet transfusion + IV methylprednisolone + IVIG, emergency splenectomy
Course80% recover within 6 months · chronic ITP (> 12 months): TPO agonists, rituximab, splenectomy

Haemophilia

  • X-linked recessive: boys affected, mothers carriers · A = factor VIII deficiency (85%) · B = factor IX (Christmas disease)
  • Severity: severe < 1% factor (spontaneous bleeds) · moderate 1–5% · mild 5–40% (after trauma/surgery)
  • Bleeding after circumcision, when crawling/walking (bruises), haemarthrosis (knee, elbow, ankle: commonest), muscle haematoma (iliopsoas), intracranial
  • Labs: prolonged APTT, normal PT, platelets, bleeding time · factor VIII/IX assay
  • Treatment: replace the factor early (within 2 h of a bleed): factor VIII: units = weight × % rise ÷ 2 · factor IX: weight × % rise · FFP or cryoprecipitate when concentrate is unavailable (cryo has VIII)
  • Mild A: desmopressin (DDAVP) · tranexamic acid for mucosal bleeds
  • Joint bleed: RICE, factor, physiotherapy · no IM injections, no aspirin/NSAIDs
  • Complications: haemophilic arthropathy, inhibitors, transfusion infections

Von Willebrand disease

  • Commonest inherited bleeding disorder · autosomal dominant (type 1) · vWF helps platelets stick and carries factor VIII
  • Mucosal bleeding: epistaxis, gum bleeding, menorrhagia, bleeding after dental extraction
  • Labs: ↑ bleeding time / PFA, APTT normal or ↑, ↓ vWF antigen and activity, ↓ factor VIII
  • Treatment: desmopressin (type 1), tranexamic acid, vWF-containing concentrate

Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)

  • Widespread activation of clotting consumes platelets and factors → bleeding + microthrombi
  • Causes: sepsis (meningococcus), severe malaria, shock, asphyxia, snake bite (13.4), leukaemia (APL), burns
  • Labs: ↓ platelets, ↑ PT and APTT, ↓ fibrinogen, ↑ D-dimer, fragmented RBCs
  • Treatment: treat the cause, FFP, platelets, cryoprecipitate (fibrinogen), vitamin K
