Immunization gives protection before exposure. The Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) of the Sudan Ministry of Health gives every child free vaccines against the major childhood killers in the first 18 months.

Killed (inactivated)Pertussis (whole-cell), inactivated polio (IPV), hepatitis A, rabies, injected influenza
ToxoidDiphtheria, tetanus
Recombinant (subunit)Hepatitis B (HBsAg)
Conjugate polysaccharideHib, pneumococcal (PCV), meningococcal A · conjugation to a protein makes them work under 2 years and gives memory
Exam trap
  • Pertussis vaccine is a killed vaccine.
  • BCG, oral polio, measles and mumps vaccines are live.
  • When an MCQ lists these and asks which is not live, the answer is pertussis.

Sudan protocol · with the EPI
  • Pentavalent = diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, hepatitis B, Hib
  • Vitamin A: 100 000 IU at 6 months, then 200 000 IU every 6 months until 5 years
  • Tetanus toxoid (Td) to women of child-bearing age and in pregnancy: prevents neonatal tetanus

Try it · exam-style
Which compulsory (EPI) vaccine protects against pneumonia and meningitis from a capsulated organism?
ANSWERPneumococcal conjugate vaccine (and Hib in the pentavalent).
Why are conjugate vaccines used in infants?
ANSWERPlain polysaccharide vaccines do not work under 2 years; conjugation gives a T-cell response and memory.

Best next step
  • Anaphylaxis after a vaccine → adrenaline 0.01 mg/kg IM (1:1000, 0.01 ml/kg, maximum 0.5 ml), repeat every 5–15 minutes
  • BCG lymphadenitis, non-suppurative → observe (resolves) · fluctuant → needle aspiration

Try it · exam-style
Which vaccine gives gut immunity and herd immunity?
ANSWEROral polio vaccine (excreted in stool).
BCG mainly protects against which forms of TB?
ANSWERSevere forms in young children: miliary TB and TB meningitis.

Contraindications

All vaccinesAnaphylaxis to a previous dose or to a vaccine component · defer in moderate or severe acute illness (high fever)
Live vaccinesSevere immunodeficiency (SCID, agammaglobulinaemia) · chemotherapy · high-dose steroids (≥ 2 mg/kg/day or ≥ 20 mg/day for ≥ 14 days) · pregnancy · household contact of an immunodeficient child: IPV instead of OPV
Pertussis (Penta)Encephalopathy within 7 days of a previous dose · progressive neurological disease (uncontrolled epilepsy, infantile spasms) · continue the other components without pertussis
BCGSymptomatic HIV · known immunodeficiency
RotavirusPrevious intussusception · SCID
Measles (MR)Pregnancy · severe immunodeficiency · recent immunoglobulin or blood (delay 3–11 months)

These are NOT contraindications

  • Mild illness, low-grade fever, cold, diarrhoea
  • Antibiotic treatment, convalescence
  • Malnutrition: vaccinate, especially measles
  • Prematurity: vaccinate by chronological age, full dose
  • Breastfeeding, pregnant mother at home
  • Family history of convulsions or of vaccine reactions
  • Stable neurological disease (e.g. cerebral palsy)
  • Asymptomatic HIV: give all EPI vaccines, including measles
Exam trap
  • A child taking antibiotics, or an infant in hospital, can be vaccinated: these are not contraindications.
  • Egg allergy: older MCQs give MMR as the vaccine to avoid (grown in chick cells).
  • Current practice: MMR is safe; only yellow fever and influenza vaccines need care.

Special situations

Missed dosesNever restart the series · continue where it stopped · keep 4-week intervals
Measles contactMeasles vaccine within 72 hours (from 6 months) · immunoglobulin within 6 days if infant or immunodeficient
Tetanus-prone woundClean the wound · TT/Td if not fully vaccinated or last dose > 5 years · TIG if unvaccinated or unknown
Sickle cell, aspleniaComplete EPI + PPV23 after 2 years · meningococcal · yearly influenza · penicillin prophylaxis
Cold chainFridge 2–8 °C · OPV and measles most heat-sensitive · never freeze Penta, HepB, PCV (shake test) · reconstituted measles: discard after 6 hours
