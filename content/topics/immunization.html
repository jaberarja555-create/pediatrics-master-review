Immunization gives protection against a disease by stimulating the immune system (active) or by giving ready-made antibody (passive). The Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) of the Sudan Federal Ministry of Health gives the routine vaccines free of charge; its goals include eradication of polio and elimination of measles and neonatal tetanus.

Types of immunity

NaturalAcquired (artificial)
ActiveAfter infection (often lifelong)Vaccines · takes weeks · long-lasting, memory
PassiveMaternal IgG across the placenta, IgA in breast milk · lasts ≈ 6 monthsImmunoglobulins and antitoxins (HBIG, TIG, rabies Ig, antivenom) · immediate, short-lived

Types of vaccines

Live attenuatedBCG, OPV, measles / MR / MMR, rotavirus, yellow fever, varicella · strongest immunity, often one dose · avoid in immunosuppression and pregnancy
Inactivated (killed)Whole-cell pertussis, IPV (Salk), hepatitis A, rabies, influenza
ToxoidDiphtheria, tetanus (inactivated toxin)
Recombinant / subunitHepatitis B (HBsAg), HPV
PolysaccharidePPV23, typhoid Vi · B-cell only: poor response under 2 years, no booster effect
ConjugateHib, PCV13, MenA · sugar joined to a protein → T-cell response, works in infants, gives memory

Contraindications

True contraindications
  • Anaphylaxis to a previous dose or a component
  • Live vaccines: severe immunodeficiency (SCID, chemotherapy, high-dose steroids ≥ 2 mg/kg/day for ≥ 14 days), pregnancy
  • Pertussis: encephalopathy within 7 days of a previous dose · progressive neurological disease
  • Rotavirus: previous intussusception
  • BCG: symptomatic HIV
Not contraindications (vaccinate)
  • Mild illness, low-grade fever, cold, diarrhoea
  • Malnutrition (vaccinate: measles is deadly in SAM)
  • Prematurity (use chronological age), breastfeeding
  • Antibiotics, family history of fits, stable neurological disease
  • Asymptomatic HIV: give all EPI vaccines, including measles
  • Egg allergy (MMR is safe)

Postpone in a severe febrile illness (temperature ≥ 39–40 °C). After immunoglobulin or blood, delay live vaccines (measles) for 3–11 months.

Adverse events following immunization

All vaccinesPain, redness, swelling, mild fever: paracetamol 15 mg/kg
BCGLocal abscess, axillary lymphadenitis (BCG adenitis), keloid · disseminated BCG in immunodeficiency
PentavalentFever, persistent crying, febrile seizure, hypotonic hyporesponsive episode
MeaslesFever and rash at day 7–10 · transient thrombocytopenia
AnaphylaxisWithin minutes: adrenaline 1:1000 0.01 mL/kg IM (max 0.5 mL) · observe every child 30 minutes after vaccination

Cold chain

  • Vaccines are kept at +2 to +8 °C from the factory to the child; OPV and measles are the most heat-sensitive
  • Vaccine vial monitor (VVM): inner square lighter than the outer circle = usable; same or darker = discard
  • Freezing destroys Penta, PCV, Td and hepatitis B: the shake test detects frozen vials
  • Reconstituted BCG and measles: use within 6 hours, then discard
