The systemic examination stations follow the same order in every checklist: introduction and positioning, general examination with growth and vital signs, then inspection, palpation, percussion and auscultation of the system, and at the end a summary, differential diagnosis and plan. Marks are lost more often for a missed step than for a missed sign.

Steps of the examination

StartGreet, wash hands, take permission; child sitting on the mother's lap (infant) or at 45°; expose the chest
GeneralWellbeing, consciousness, respiratory distress, pallor, cyanosis, clubbing, dysmorphism; weight, height, OFC; temperature, pulse, RR for a full minute, SpO₂
Inspection
  • Nasal flaring, grunting, head nodding
  • Chest shape: hyperinflation, Harrison's sulcus, pigeon chest, rickety rosary
  • Recession: subcostal, intercostal, suprasternal
  • Scars, chest drains; look from the foot of the bed for less movement on one side
PalpationTrachea (central or shifted), apex beat, chest expansion at three levels, tactile vocal fremitus (older child)
PercussionCompare both sides at each level: dull (consolidation), stony dull (effusion), hyper-resonant (pneumothorax, hyperinflation)
AuscultationAll areas, front and back, comparing sides: air entry, breath sounds (vesicular or bronchial), added sounds (crackles, wheeze, rub), vocal resonance
FinishOffer to examine the back and the ear, nose and throat; cover the child, thank the mother, then summarise

Steps of the examination

GeneralDysmorphism (Down, Turner, Williams), breathlessness, sweating, cyanosis, pallor, growth
HandsClubbing, splinter haemorrhages and Osler's nodes (endocarditis), capillary refill
Pulse
  • Rate, rhythm, volume and character: collapsing (PDA), weak (aortic stenosis, shock)
  • Radial and femoral together: delay or weak femorals = coarctation
  • Offer the BP in the right arm and a leg
Face and neckConjunctival pallor, tongue for central cyanosis, JVP (older child)
InspectionChest bulge (enlarged heart), scars (midline sternotomy, left lateral thoracotomy: PDA ligation, coarctation, shunt), visible pulsations
PalpationApex beat: site and character (heaving: LV; displaced: cardiomegaly) · parasternal heave (RV) · thrills · palpable P2 (pulmonary hypertension)
AuscultationFour areas with diaphragm and bell; heart sounds (S2 splitting); murmur: timing, site of maximum intensity, grade, radiation; back for coarctation
Heart failureTender enlarged liver (the most useful sign in infants), basal crackles, oedema (late in infants)

Try it · exam-style
Why are the radial and femoral pulses felt together?
ANSWERTo detect radio-femoral delay or weak femorals of coarctation of the aorta.
Give three features of an innocent murmur.
ANSWERSoft and systolic with no thrill, varies with posture, in a well child with normal heart sounds.

Steps of the examination

PositionChild lying flat with arms by the sides (infant on the mother's lap); expose from the nipples to the knees, keeping the genitalia covered until needed
GeneralPallor, jaundice, wasting, oedema, lymph nodes, signs of chronic liver disease (spider naevi, palmar erythema)
InspectionContour (distension, pot belly), umbilicus (hernia), dilated veins, scars, traditional cautery marks (they point to a chronic illness the family treated), visible peristalsis (pyloric stenosis)
Superficial palpationWatch the child's face; tenderness, guarding, temperature, superficial masses
Deep palpationStart in the right iliac fossa and move up for the liver and diagonally for the spleen; then the kidneys bimanually (ballotable)
PercussionLiver span (upper border), spleen; shifting dullness or fluid thrill for ascites
AuscultationBowel sounds; bruit over the liver or kidneys
FinishHernial orifices and genitalia (undescended testis, hydrocele); offer the anal examination; cover, thank, summarise

Reporting an organ

  • Liver: size below the costal margin in the mid-clavicular line (cm), span by percussion, edge (sharp or rounded), consistency, surface, tenderness, bruit
  • Normal: the edge may be felt up to 2 cm below the margin in infants
  • Spleen: size in cm from the left costal margin towards the right iliac fossa, consistency, tenderness, notch
  • A palpable spleen is enlarged (except the tip in some infants)

Try it · exam-style
Where does palpation for the spleen start?
ANSWERIn the right iliac fossa, moving towards the left costal margin.
How is ascites confirmed at the bedside?
ANSWERBy shifting dullness (or a fluid thrill in tense ascites).

Hepatosplenomegaly

Hepatosplenomegaly is a common long-case and OSCE finding. The examiner expects the student to describe the organs correctly and then give a differential diagnosis that starts with the infections common in the area, because a big spleen in a child from an endemic region is usually malaria, kala-azar or schistosomiasis.

Causes

InfectionsMalaria (hyper-reactive malarial splenomegaly), kala-azar (huge spleen, fever, wasting), schistosomiasis (portal hypertension: firm spleen, small liver), typhoid, brucellosis, TB, HIV, infectious mononucleosis, endocarditis
HaematologicalThalassaemia, sickle cell disease (young child, before auto-infarction), hereditary spherocytosis, leukaemia, lymphoma
CongestiveHeart failure (liver more than spleen), portal hypertension, Budd–Chiari syndrome
Storage and metabolicGaucher disease (spleen), glycogen storage disease (liver), Niemann–Pick disease

Clues on examination

  • Fever and wasting with a huge spleen: kala-azar; ask about residence (Gedaref, Sennar, Blue Nile).
  • Pallor and jaundice with frontal bossing: haemolytic anaemia (thalassaemia, sickle cell disease).
  • Lymph nodes, bruising and bone pain: leukaemia or lymphoma.
  • Haematemesis, firm spleen, dilated abdominal veins: portal hypertension (schistosomiasis).
Best next step
  • Investigations: CBC with blood film (malaria parasites, blasts, sickle cells, target cells), reticulocytes, liver function, rK39 test or bone marrow or splenic aspirate for kala-azar, Hb electrophoresis, abdominal ultrasound with portal vein Doppler, stool and urine for schistosoma eggs.
Formula
Kala-azar
Child from Gedaref+long fever and wasting+huge spleen, pancytopenia=Visceral leishmaniasis: rK39 test
Clue switch · the detail that flips the answer
Huge spleen crossing the midline, fever, wastingKala-azar
Firm spleen, haematemesis, normal liver functionPortal hypertension (schistosomiasis)
Pallor, jaundice, frontal bossing, transfusionsThalassaemia major
Big tender liver, breathless infant, murmurHeart failure
Try it · exam-style
Name three infections that cause hepatosplenomegaly in a child from an endemic region.
ANSWERMalaria, kala-azar and schistosomiasis (also typhoid, brucellosis, TB).
Which simple test is done first in hepatosplenomegaly?
ANSWERCBC with a blood film.
