Emergency Triage Assessment and Treatment (ETAT) is the WHO system used in Sudanese hospitals to sort every sick child on arrival, before registration, and to treat life-threatening problems at once. Most deaths in hospital happen in the first 24 hours, many while waiting.

The three triage groups

GroupSignsAction
Emergency (E)
  • Airway / breathing: obstructed or absent breathing, severe respiratory distress, central cyanosis
  • Circulation: cold hands + CRT > 3 s + weak fast pulse (shock)
  • Coma (AVPU = P or U) or convulsing now
  • Dehydration: severe (lethargy, sunken eyes, very slow skin pinch) in a child with diarrhoea
Treat immediately
Priority (P)
  • Tiny baby (< 2 months)
  • Very high temperature
  • Trauma or urgent surgical problem
  • Severe pallor
  • Poisoning
  • Severe pain
  • Respiratory distress
  • Restless, irritable or lethargic
  • Urgent referral
  • Visible severe wasting
  • Oedema of both feet
  • Major burns
Front of the queue, assess quickly
Non-urgent (Q)None of the aboveWait in the queue

Assessment: ABCD

  • Airway and breathing: is the child breathing? obstructed? cyanosed? severe distress (grunting, severe indrawing, head nodding)?
  • Circulation: hands warm? CRT (press the nail or sternum 3 s)? pulse weak and fast?
  • Coma / convulsion: AVPU (Alert, responds to Voice, to Pain, Unresponsive) · fitting now?
  • Dehydration (severe) with diarrhoea · check glucose in every child with an emergency sign

Emergency treatments (ETAT)

Airway
  • Choking: back blows / chest or abdominal thrusts
  • Head tilt–chin lift (no trauma) or jaw thrust
  • Suction
  • Recovery position if unconscious
BreathingOxygen (nasal prongs 1–2 L/min, or mask) · bag-and-mask if not breathing
Circulation
  • IV/IO access
  • Shock without SAM: Ringer's lactate/saline 10–20 mL/kg rapidly, reassess, repeat (WHO 2016: 10–20 mL/kg)
  • Severe anaemia: blood
  • SAM: 15 mL/kg over 1 h (2.2)
Coma / convulsion
  • Position, airway
  • Diazepam 0.5 mg/kg rectally (0.3 mg/kg IV)
  • Glucose: 10% dextrose 5 mL/kg if < 2.5 mmol/L (or untested)
DehydrationPlan C (5.1)

Septic shock

  1. Oxygen, IV/IO access in 5 minutes · blood culture, glucose, gas, lactate
  2. Antibiotics within 1 hour: ceftriaxone 80–100 mg/kg IV (± artesunate if malaria)
  3. Fluid bolus 10–20 mL/kg crystalloid, reassess after each (liver, crackles) · up to 40–60 mL/kg
  4. Fluid-refractory: inotropes (adrenaline 0.05–0.3 µg/kg/min or noradrenaline; dopamine where not available) · hydrocortisone if refractory
  5. Correct hypoglycaemia, hypocalcaemia · ICU

Paediatric basic life support

  • Safe approach, stimulate, shout for help · open airway (head tilt–chin lift; neutral in infants)
  • 5 rescue breaths, then check for signs of life (10 s)
  • 15 compressions :
    • 2 breaths
    • Rate 100–120/min
    • Depth ⅓ of the chest (4 cm infant, 5 cm child)
  • Infant: two-thumb encircling or two fingers · child: heel of one hand on the lower sternum
  • Advanced: attach monitor
  • Non-shockable (asystole, PEA: commonest in children): adrenaline 10 µg/kg (0.1 mL/kg of 1:10 000) IV/IO every 3–5 min
  • Shockable (VF/pulseless VT): DC shock 4 J/kg, adrenaline and amiodarone 5 mg/kg after the 3rd and 5th shocks
  • Treat reversible causes: 4 Hs (hypoxia, hypovolaemia, hypo/hyperkalaemia, hypothermia) and 4 Ts (tension pneumothorax, tamponade, toxins, thrombosis)
