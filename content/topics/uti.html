Urinary tract infection (UTI) is common in childhood (about 8% of girls and 2% of boys). In infants it presents with fever and non-specific signs, so the urine must be checked in every febrile infant without a focus. Infection of the kidney (pyelonephritis) in young children can scar the kidneys, especially with vesicoureteric reflux (VUR).

Causes and risk factors

  • E. coli (≈ 80%) · Klebsiella, Proteus (boys, stones), Enterococcus, Pseudomonas (abnormal tracts)
  • Boys are more affected in the first 3–6 months (uncircumcised); girls after (short urethra)
  • VUR, obstruction (posterior urethral valves, PUJ obstruction), neurogenic bladder (spina bifida), stones
  • Constipation, poor hygiene, infrequent voiding, pinworms, sexual abuse

Clinical features

InfantFever, vomiting, poor feeding, irritability, prolonged jaundice, FTT, sepsis
Older childDysuria, frequency, urgency, new enuresis, suprapubic pain, offensive urine (cystitis)
PyelonephritisFever ≥ 38 °C, loin pain/tenderness, vomiting, toxic

Diagnosis

  • Urine sample:
    • Clean-catch (best in infants), midstream (older), catheter or suprapubic aspiration (most reliable, infants)
    • Bags often contaminated (only to rule out)
  • Dipstick (> 3 yr): nitrite (specific) and leucocyte esterase (sensitive)
  • Microscopy (pus cells, bacteria) and culture (gold standard): > 10⁵ CFU/mL of one organism (clean catch)
  • Any growth on suprapubic aspirate
  • > 10³–10⁴ on catheter
  • Upper UTI: CRP, blood culture, RFT

Vesicoureteric reflux (VUR)

  • Backflow of urine from the bladder into the ureter and kidney, from a short intramural ureter
  • Often familial (siblings 30%)
  • Infected urine reaches the kidney → pyelonephritis → renal scarring → hypertension, CKD (reflux nephropathy)
  • Mild reflux usually resolves with growth

Atypical UTI: seriously ill, poor urine flow, abdominal or bladder mass, raised creatinine, septicaemia, no response in 48 h, non-E. coli organism. Recurrent: ≥ 2 pyelonephritis, or 1 pyelonephritis + 1 cystitis, or ≥ 3 cystitis.

Management of VUR and recurrent UTI

  • Prompt treatment of every febrile UTI · treat constipation and voiding problems
  • Antibiotic prophylaxis (high-grade VUR, recurrent UTI): trimethoprim 2 mg/kg or nitrofurantoin 1 mg/kg at night
  • Surgery (ureteric reimplantation, endoscopic injection) for high-grade reflux with breakthrough infections or new scars
  • Follow-up: BP, growth, urine protein in children with scars

Posterior urethral valves

Membranes in the posterior urethra of boys · antenatal hydronephrosis, oligohydramnios · poor stream, palpable bladder, UTI, renal failure · MCUG · endoscopic valve ablation.
